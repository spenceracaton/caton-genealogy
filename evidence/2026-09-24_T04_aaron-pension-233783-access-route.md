## Result

No digitized copy of application 233783 was located. The authenticated Fold3 application-number search still exposes the A1158 numerical-index image and no underlying application packet. The National Archives Catalog identifies the relevant paper record series and makes selected files from it available online, but exact searches within that series for `233783`, `SO-233783`, `Aaron Caten`, and `Aaron Caton` return no result.

The blank certificate fields on T288 and T289, together with the NARA series description, place the file with high confidence in the **disapproved or abandoned veteran applications** rather than the approved-certificate series. NARA states that disallowed or abandoned claims retained their original application number and were called "originals" or `SO` files. The series is arranged numerically from `SO 7` through `SO 1,654,862`; application 233783 therefore should be requested as **Soldier's Original / SO 233783**. This is an archival-filing inference until the jacket itself is retrieved, not a new reading of the application.

The series is unrestricted and the paper copy is held at the National Archives Building in Washington, DC. On-site research is therefore a non-digitized alternative to ordering reproductions. For remote retrieval, the available NARA product is the paid complete-file order.

The T288/T289 filing-date conflict remains unresolved:

- T288: 10 April 1876.
- T289: 10 April 1877.
- A1158: confirms invalid application 233783 but supplies no date.

No date was normalized from application-number sequence. The file jacket, declaration, receipt stamps, and Pension Bureau brief are still required.

## Evidentiary payoff

**High for Aaron's 1876–1877 biography and pension chronology; conditional for parentage.** NARA states that a veteran's application typically gives name, unit, service, residence, age or birth information, and property. This file should also explain the claimed disability and why the application was disapproved or abandoned. Its date-bearing papers should resolve the T288/T289 year conflict.

The parentage value depends on explicit testimony. The highest-value pages would be:

1. a declaration giving birthplace, earlier residences, or migration chronology;
2. an affidavit or deposition stating **where and when** a witness first knew Aaron, especially before his August 1841 Ohio marriage;
3. a family statement explicitly naming a parent, sibling, or childhood household;
4. correspondence or a rejection brief explaining whether Aaron died before completing proof and whether any dependent continued the claim.

A witness's name, address, unit, or proximity alone will not establish kinship. A standard 1876 disability declaration is not expected to name parents. The file can materially sharpen the childhood-placement problem without necessarily solving parentage.

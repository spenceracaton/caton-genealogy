## Image-level observations

- **1836, image 192:** the C-page contains separate rows reading `Cayton
  Thomas` and `Cayton William`. Each has a visible `1` in the first numeric
  field. No household brace or explicit relationship connects the rows. The
  source page and a crop are preserved in the evidence directory.
- **1837, image 263:** the previously preserved candidate page contains
  separate rows reading `Cayton Thomas`, `Cayton William`, and `Cayton Thos.`;
  each has a visible `1` in the first numeric field. See the existing A1 page
  and crop.
- **1838, images 311–312:** the C-page sequence was visually reviewed. No
  `Caton` or `Cayton` row was located in the inspected sequence. Images 311 and
  312 and focused C-page crops are preserved.
- **1839, image 356:** the C-page was visually reviewed. No `Caton` or `Cayton`
  row was located. The page and three crops are preserved.

The column headings are not visible on the candidate pages. The first numeric
field is therefore reported only as a visible entry; this note does not assign
it a tax-column meaning.

## Interpretation boundary

**Proof:** The 1836 and 1837 pages contain separate handwritten `Cayton` rows
with the stated visible entries. The inspected 1838 and 1839 C-page sequences
did not show a located `Caton` or `Cayton` row.

**Inference only:** `Cayton` remains a spelling-variant candidate in the
regional cluster. The 1836–1837 pages do not show Thomas carrying Aaron and
Joseph as a two- or three-person tithable household, and they do not identify
any Cayton as Aaron, Joseph, or Thomas Caton's son.

**Limit:** This is not a negative finding for every page of every annual tax
list. It covers the image-level C-page sequences listed above; the 1838–1839
non-hit is not proof that no Caton-family entry appears elsewhere in those
annual sections. The annual tax route remains open only for identifying the
Cayton family and establishing page completeness, not as current positive
parentage evidence.

## Finding 1 — the 1898 notice is recovered, but the niece is not identified

The original page resolves the notice citation and corrects the inherited wording. The printed masthead is *The Boonville Weekly Advertiser*; the SHSMO/Newspapers.com archive catalogs the issue as *The Boonville Daily News and Advertiser*. The item is on Friday, 11 March 1898, page 8, column 3.

> —Joseph Caton, a former citizen of Boonville, who left here in 1847, is in town, the guest of his niece, Mrs. Sawtell. He was engaged in the tailoring business in Boonville when a young man, and returns with the expectation of again becoming a citizen of the town. After he left here he lived in Brunswick for twenty years and in Marshall about the same length of time. The weight of years sits lightly on him and he is still very active.

The item ends there. The next paragraph is a Washington City dispatch. No omitted spouse name or continuation appears.

The biography identifies Joseph the tailor. It does not identify the niece. On 18 March 1898 the same archive carried this separate Boonville item:

> —Our young friend, Gip Sawtell, of Cleveland, Ohio, is in Boonville, the guest of his mother, Mrs. Mary Sawtell. He is now holding the responsible position of bookkeeper in a very large packing house in Cleveland, Ohio. Gip is a native of Boonville, where he has many friends, who are pleased to know that he is getting on well in the world.

Mary Sawtell is therefore a real competing candidate in the exact place and week. Julia A. (Caton) Sawtell remains possible, but convergence on the surname and city is insufficient to choose her.

Julia's existing 1916 Missouri death certificate was rendered and inspected for this pass. It states that Julia A. Sawtell was born 15 December 1843 in Missouri; her father was Aaron Caton, born Virginia; her mother was Sarah Gardner, born Ohio; informant Stella L. Curtis supplied the information. This proves what the certificate records about Julia's parents. It does not identify the 1898 Mrs. Sawtell.

## Finding 3 — Joseph's origin is directly conflicted in contemporary obituaries

### *The Brunswicker*, 27 May 1910, p. 1

The full local obituary says:

> **Joseph Caton Dead.**
>
> Joseph Caton was born in Fairfax county, Virginia, July 24, 1823, and died at the home of his daughter, Mrs. Henry Shanz, in Marshall, Mo., Saturday May 21, 1910, his age therefore being 86 years, 9 months and 27 days.
>
> At the age of ten years, Mr. Caton came with his parents to Missouri, settling first at Boonville. He lived there till the year 1847, when he moved to Brunswick, and he was an honored citizen of this community till 1866. Since that time he has visited many parts of the country, and has made his home in a number of different places.
>
> About the time he came to Brunswick Mr. Caton was united in marriage to Miss Harriett Dunn, who preceded him to the grave Jan. 12, 1863. To their union were born six children, five of whom survive the parents. They are: H. D. Caton, who lives in Colorado; T. E. Caton, Tulsa Oklahoma; Otis O. Caton, Salisbury; Mrs. Sue Dunn, Boulder, Colorado, and Mrs. Sallie Shanz, of Marshall, Mo. Gabriella, the second child, died in early childhood.
>
> Mr. Caton became a member of the Methodist church in young manhood, and continued a faithful member of that church till his death.
>
> The remains were shipped to Brunswick, Monday, and after funeral services at the Methodist church by Rev. C. O. Ransford, were taken to the Douglas cemetery.
>
> The Brunswicker offers sincere sympathies to the sorrowing family.

The surname spellings `Dunn` and `Shanz` above are preserved as printed and are not silent corrections to Dumm and Schanz. The obituary is contemporaneous and locally detailed, but the informant is not named. Its statements about 1823 and the age-ten migration are later biographical testimony, not a birth or migration record.

The page 5 match is a same-issue funeral-attendance item. It reports that Mr. and Mrs. A. T. Kendrick, Mrs. E. C. Ruckel and son, William Shanz, and O. O. Caton attended Joseph's funeral at Brunswick. It names no older generation.

### *Marshall Republican*, 27 May 1910, p. 1

The retained image confirms the original printed wording:

> Mr. Caton was born in Forkware county, Va., on July 24, 1823.

The image also prints the surviving sons as `H. D. Caton`, `O. O. Caton`, and `T. E. Caton`, not the `J. E. Caton` in C076. “Forkware” remains the newspaper's typesetting. Reading it as Fauquier is an interpretation.

### *Chariton Courier*, 3 June 1910, p. 3

The LOC issue route exists and was retrieved directly. The item states:

> Joseph Caton, 87 years old, a native of Virginia, passed away Saturday at the home of his daughter, Mrs. Henry Schantz of Marshall. Five children survive him.

This adds no county, parents, or siblings. Its compressed form and later publication make it likely derivative of the Marshall-area death notice rather than a separate family witness.

### Source-dependence assessment

- The 1898 Boonville visit notice is contemporary to the visit and independent of the 1910 death notices.
- The Marshall and Brunswick obituaries are separately composed: one prints Forkware and a Marshall residence narrative; the other prints Fairfax and a Brunswick-centered life narrative. They are distinct publications, but their informants are unknown and could overlap within Joseph's children. They cannot be treated as two independent witnesses to birthplace.
- The Chariton item is a short digest and is likely derivative of a Marshall-area notice. It is not an independent parentage witness.
- The 1932 Sue and Clint catalog entries are not two narratives until their pages can be compared.

The second proof obligation is only partly answered. A contemporary Brunswick obituary records Fairfax County and migration with unnamed parents; a Marshall obituary records Forkware County. No accessible record in this pass names Joseph's parents. No statement can yet be attributed specifically to a descendant because the obituary informants are unidentified and the Dumm pages remain unavailable.

## Conclusion

The strongest inherited relationship claim is weaker after source recovery. The paper really did call Joseph's unnamed hostess his niece, and the Joseph identity is secure. The assertion that she was Julia is not secure because Mary Sawtell is a documented contemporary candidate. Joseph and Aaron may have been brothers or half-brothers, but this notice does not yet prove it.

Joseph's origin evidence is also more conflicted than the canonical claims state. One contemporary obituary prints Forkware County; another detailed local obituary prints Fairfax County and says Joseph came to Missouri with his parents at age ten. Neither names those parents. The two-page Sue Caton Dumm record remains the single best next record because it can address the parent and sibling questions at daughter-level proximity.

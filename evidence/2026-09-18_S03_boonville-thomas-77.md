S03 research note — the 77-year-old Boonville tailor Thomas Caton

Result

S03 remains partial. The 1850 published transcription is strong enough to preserve the elderly man’s observation as a separate research identity, but not strong enough to solve him. The key primary records requested by the stream — the 1850 manuscript, the 1840 p.145 Thomas age row, the 1860 full Cooper schedule, and Cooper probate images — were not available to inspect in this execution environment.

Short documented biography

The narrowest supported biography is therefore short. On 14 August 1850, according to the published Cooper County transcription, a man named Thomas Caton, age 77, born Virginia, occupation “Taylor,” was enumerated as the last person in John P. Maddex’s household 66 in Boonville. No real-estate value or later-column mark is transcribed for Thomas. The next census line begins household 67. One internal warning is important: the transcription assigns a school-attendance mark to John A. Maddex, age 1, which is implausible and may reflect a row/column shift or transcription error. That makes inspection of the manuscript essential before upgrading any column-level reading. The 1850 schedule does not state relationships, so the record does not identify Thomas as Maddex’s father, father-in-law, employee, boarder, or any other relation.

No reliable death date, death place, burial, estate, heir list, or former Virginia residence was found for this elderly Thomas in the records reached in S03.

1850 same-name control

The Cooper transcription also contains a plainly different Thomas Caton in household 1038: age 40, male, farmer, $300 real estate, born Kentucky, followed by Mrs. Caton and children Lucinda, James, Newton, Nimrod, Nancy and an infant. Those observations are not reasonably merged with the 77-year-old Virginia-born tailor in household 66.

A public FamilySearch lineage page for William Thomas Caton identifies his parents as Thomas Caton Jr. (1803–1863) and Elizabeth Ann Laurie (1804–1883) and lists siblings including Lucinda, James, Newton and Nimrod. That pattern resembles household 1038 and is useful as a C-grade locator only. It is a derivative tree page, not the primary bridge needed to equate the 1828 Fauquier groom with household 1038. The 1999 descendant account and Pleasant Hill cemetery material already in the project remain derivative/context evidence, not identity proof for the elderly tailor.

Maddex household test

The 1850 transcription shows a household that appears family-like: John P. Maddex, 47, Virginia-born constable; Parthema G., 39, Virginia; Jane, 17, Virginia; Tebisha D., 15, Missouri; Michael G., 8, Missouri; John A., 1, Missouri; then Thomas Caton, 77, Virginia-born tailor. The schedule itself supplies no relationships.

The searchable Cooper marriage compilation did not surface a pre-1850 John P. Maddex/Maddox marriage under the tested Maddex/Maddox and Parthema/Parthena/Parthemia variants. It did surface a John P. Maddox — Sarah H. Cobbs marriage dated 16 November 1857, which proves the search layer can return the surname and name combination. That 1857 entry has not been proved to be the same John P. Maddex as household 66. The compilation warns that it is not necessarily complete or accurate, so its silence before 1850 cannot supply Parthema’s maiden name or exclude an out-of-county marriage.

No evidence reached here makes Parthema a Caton or otherwise explains Thomas’s presence in the household.

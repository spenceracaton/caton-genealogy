# S04 — Test the older Broad Run Aaron as an alternative rather than assuming an uncle

**Target in every stream:** Aaron Caton, reported born 16 May 1820 in Virginia, married Sarah Ann Gardner in Delaware County, Ohio, in August 1841, later a Missouri resident and tailor. His father and mother remain unidentified. These are investigation instructions, not newly proved relationships. [R1]

## Question and scope

Did the older Aaron Caton associated with Prince William/Broad Run leave a wife, children or estate capable of including target Aaron? This is a bounded alternative-hypothesis test. It is not a new preferred pedigree or a license to research colonial Catons indefinitely.

The repo reports an adult Aaron witnessing a 1795 deed and serving on juries in 1813. It also reports a Full-Text result in a 1833-1834 Rappahannock deed describing a deceased Aaron as trustee. The latter document and its recited original trust are the precise unfinished acquisition. [R2:C153-C157]

## Why the exclusion is inadequate

The 1810 household reportedly contains one adult man and one adult woman, without children. That describes residency on one census date, not a lifetime of childlessness. Absence as a named 1820 household head does not prove death. The narrative that target Aaron was named for a deceased childless uncle requires several unproved relationships and dates. [N5]

A 28 February 1825 death appears in later handoffs without an adequate supporting citation in the reviewed passages. Treat it as a locator to validate, not an event or a located estate packet. The earlier 1795 witness's minimum age should also not be fixed by an unverified assumption about the applicable witness-age rule.

## Primary acquisition chain

**Open the 1833-1834 Rappahannock deed and every continuation.** Use the retained C153 search evidence to recover the exact image rather than guessing a book page. Transcribe all parties, residences, date of the current instrument, exact description of Aaron, and every recited date/book/page. Identify why a trustee is being mentioned: replacement, reconveyance, debt satisfaction, title dispute or something else.

**Retrieve the earlier trust in its actual recording jurisdiction.** Do not assume that because the later instrument was recorded in Rappahannock the earlier one was also recorded there. Follow the recited locality and dates. Compare the White/Horton associates and land description with the retained 1795 and 1813 records. A name-and-neighborhood resemblance supports investigation but is not yet proof that every Aaron reference is the same person.

**Build a narrow death interval from dated evidence.** Record last demonstrable act alive and first dated statement deceased. A later reference to a dead trustee establishes only that he was dead by that statement, not that he died when the original trust was signed. Test the 1825 date against an original record or an identified primary transcription. If its origin cannot be found, leave it unverified.

**Search the estate and widow within that interval.** Start in the jurisdictions actually supplied by the instruments. Reuse the existing Prince William published-index negative, with its known scope. Do not repeat the same literal search and call it new. Look instead for omitted record classes, alternate indexed parties, court orders, deeds by heirs, a widow's remarriage, guardianship or a trustee-successor proceeding. S02 owns generalized childhood volumes; send it the newly identified guardian or widow rather than duplicating its page review.

## Additional bounded sources

The Fauquier Superior Court deeds/wills group on LVA Reel 44 is outside the general indexes. Prior FamilySearch metadata points to film 31609/DGS 004087718 but reports a possible gap; LVA's subdivisions suggest the coverage must be checked on film. This is an acquisition question, not proof that an 1820s estate exists there. [A1,A5]

Once a widow or heir is named, trace that person through a small number of records that can expose Aaron's relationship. A widow marrying again may place children under a different surname. A daughter receiving a distribution may carry the only explicit sibling list. Do not spend a full pass reconstructing every White or Horton without a named bridge.

## Discriminating outcomes

A record identifying a son Aaron born about 1820, with further age/trade/migration evidence, would materially redirect the project. A statement naming brothers Thomas or Moses would establish an older-generation relationship but would not, by itself, make target Aaron a son or nephew. Keep those propositions separate.

An original death demonstrably long before target Aaron's plausible conception would exclude this man as his biological father while leaving other kinship possibilities. A well-understood complete heir proceeding that names other children but no appropriate Aaron could weaken the hypothesis; a partial will, debt settlement or unspecific index absence is not enough for a categorical exclusion. Evidence that the Rappahannock trustee was a different Aaron would invalidate that chronology link and require a new endpoint for the Broad Run man.

## First-pass endpoint and handoff

The first deliverable should be the fully transcribed later deed, its earlier trust, and a death-interval table—not another list of Aaron surname hits. If those instruments name no kin, report precisely that and decide whether one bounded estate-window search is justified.

Return spouse/child/guardian names as observations, an explicit source for each life-date boundary, and separate ratings for same-person identity and parentage. This stream is complete when it yields a testable family connection or a genuinely bounded result that permits it to be parked. It should not be expanded to English or colonial ancestry before a connection to target Aaron exists.

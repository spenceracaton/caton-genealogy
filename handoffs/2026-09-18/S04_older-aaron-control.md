# S04 — Test the elder Broad Run Aaron as an alternative, bounded

**For:** a ChatGPT research agent with a FamilySearch sign-in. **Prepared:** 18 Sep 2026. **Coordinator:** Spencer Caton via Claude Code.

**Target of every stream:** Aaron Caton, b. 16 May 1820, Virginia; m. Sarah Ann Gardner, Delaware Co., Ohio, Aug 1841; tailor; d. Boonville, Mo., 17 Jan 1878. Parents unknown. Nothing in this file is a proved relationship.

## Question

Did the elder Aaron Caton of Prince William County (Broad Run) leave a wife, children, or an estate that could include the target Aaron? This is a control on the "named for a dead childless uncle" narrative (C157), which rests on: childless-looking 1810 household; absent as head in 1820; "dec'd" by 1834; and an unverified 28 Feb 1825 death date. None of those proves he died childless or before 1820.

## Sequence

1. **Re-read image 192 and its continuation (images 191, 193) for the recited deed.** Look for a recited date, grantor, book, or page in the consideration clause and in the description of the land. Transcribe the whole instrument, all parties and residences, and every recited date. Say why a trustee is mentioned: reconveyance after debt satisfied, replacement of a dead trustee, title clearing, or other.
2. **Find the original trust deed in its actual jurisdiction.** Rappahannock was formed from Culpeper in 1833; land Thorn occupied in 1834 was Culpeper land when the trust was made. Search Culpeper deed books c.1810–1833 (FS catalog; Full-Text Search keyword `Aaron Caton` and `Caton`, place Culpeper, record type deeds, and again with no type filter). Then Fauquier deeds (John White was of Fauquier). Retain the instrument: date, grantor (debtor), beneficiary (creditor), trustee Aaron Caton with residence if stated, land description, witnesses. The trust date is the last certain date he was alive in a public capacity.
3. **Death interval.** Table: last dated act alive (trust deed or 1813 jury/tax, whichever is later) → first dated statement deceased (8 Apr 1834 unless an earlier one appears). Test 28 Feb 1825: search for its origin (published Prince William or Fauquier abstracts, Find a Grave, compiled trees). If no primary or identified transcription supports it, record `unverified` and move on.
4. **Estate and widow, within the interval and in the jurisdictions the instruments supply.** Fauquier will/order books 1813–1834 (LVA reel inventory; FS images where available). Fauquier Superior Court deeds/wills are outside the general indexes: LVA Reel 44; FS film 31609 / DGS 004087718 with a reported 1818–1829 gap — check coverage on film, do not assume the gap. Culpeper 1813–1834 will and order books. Look for administration, widow's dower, guardianship of Caton orphans, trustee-successor orders, and deeds by heirs. Reuse C177; do not re-run it.
5. **Trace one named bridge only.** If a widow or heir is named, follow that one person through the few records that could expose Aaron's relationship (remarriage, distribution, guardianship). Do not reconstruct the Whites or Hortons without a named bridge.

## Pre-registered outcomes

- A record naming a son Aaron b. c.1820 → materially redirects the project; still needs trade/migration corroboration.
- A statement naming brothers Thomas or Moses → establishes the older generation's relationship only.
- An original death demonstrably before c. Aug 1819 → excludes him as biological father; other kinship stays possible.
- Complete heir proceeding naming other children and no Aaron → weakens; partial will or index absence does not exclude.
- The Rappahannock trustee proves to be a different Aaron → the 1834 boundary is void; the Broad Run man needs a new endpoint.

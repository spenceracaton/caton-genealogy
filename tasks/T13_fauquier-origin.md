# T13 — Was the family from Fauquier County, Virginia?

**Priority 1. Aaron's county of birth is now directly documented; his parents are not.**

Aaron's signed 5 January 1864 veteran enlistment states that he was born in
**Fauquier County, Virginia**, was age 44, and was a tailor (G027). This proves the
county-level origin at grade A but does not connect him to any Fauquier Caton household.

Joseph Caton's obituary (C076, *Marshall Republican*, 27 May 1910, verified against
the page image) states he was born **"in Forkware county, Va., on July 24, 1823."**

"Forkware" is not a Virginia county. The letter-groups map almost exactly onto a
compositor misreading handwritten **Fauquier**: *Fau*→*For*, *q*→*k*, *u*→*w*,
*ier*→*are*. Fauquier is northern Piedmont, ~200 miles east of Wheeling.

**Joseph's records may still identify Aaron's kin network, but the sibling link remains
unproved.** Julia A. (Caton) Sawtell is the strongly leading identity for Joseph's
unnamed 1898 hostess, yet the notices never state the kinship path (G032, D-014).

The documented Fauquier origin also fits two independent findings: **C075** (Wheeling is unsourced, and the 1820
Ohio Co. census holds no Caton household) and **C066** (no Caton appears in any
published Wheeling county history).

## Census reconstruction — done, but not parent proof

The 1820 Fauquier census DOES contain **Thos. Caton**: males under 10 = **2**, 10-16 = 1,
16-18 = 1, 45+ = 1; females under 10 = 1, 26-45 = 1. No male 26-45, so no alternative
father in the household. Joseph (b. 1823) correctly absent.

The 1830 census has exactly three county households: Thomas Senior, Thomas Junior, and
William. None contains a boy aged 5-20, so it does **not** place Aaron (10) or Joseph
(7) in this family. The Caton entries disappear from the 1840 Fauquier index under the
tested Caton/Caten/Catton/Kitton/Kitten/Cayton/Catan/Cation spellings.

The original marriage papers identify **William as the son of Thomas Caton Jr.** and
**Ann as the daughter of Thomas Caton Sr.** (C092). The 4 Dec 1830 road order groups
Thomas, William, and Thomas Senior in the same road district (C087). This establishes a
real multigenerational cluster but leaves the two younger 1820 males unidentified.

## Fauquier-to-Cooper migration lead — not parent proof

A published descendant account reports that **Thomas Caton and Elizabeth Ann Lawrey**
married in Fauquier on 6 Dec 1828, had four children in Virginia, and then had later
children in Missouri. A Cooper County cemetery transcription independently places
Elizabeth Lowrey (1804-1883) as wife of Thomas William Caton. This may identify the
20-30-year-old Thomas Junior household in the 1830 census as a Fauquier-to-Cooper
migrant, but it does not identify Aaron or Joseph: both were born before that marriage,
and neither appears in the reported child group. Treat it as a migration route to verify
from the original marriage packet and early Cooper records, not as a parent assignment.

## Statewide context (C084)

An LVA Chancery Records Index sweep of all 107 Virginia localities for the surname
Caton returns **69 causes in 21 localities**. Northern Virginia holds 46 of them:
**Fairfax 20, Arlington 17**, Alexandria 2, **Fauquier 3**, Prince William 2,
King George 1, Frederick 1.

This cuts both ways. Fauquier is **not** an isolated anomaly — it sits inside a real,
dense Northern Virginia Caton population. But **Fairfax has nearly seven times as many
causes**, and Fairfax was the alternative reading of "Forkware" that could not be
excluded. Letterforms favour Fauquier; record density favours Fairfax.

## Do not over-read

"Forkware→Fauquier" remains only an inference about **Joseph's** obituary, graded C.
Aaron's Fauquier birthplace no longer depends on that reading or on Joseph being his
brother. It still does not identify Aaron's parents or place him in a specific household.

TRANSCRIPTION: line 24, dwelling/family 66: **Thomas Caton | 77 | M | Taylor | [no real-estate value] | Va**. The line follows six Maddex entries headed by John P. Maddex, 47, constable, real estate $1,200, Virginia-born. No relationship is stated.
STATES DIRECTLY: A man recorded as Thomas Caton, age 77, male, a “Taylor,” born in Virginia, was enumerated in John P. Maddex's numbered household in Boonville. The schedule does not identify a wife, child, parent, or relationship for Thomas. It does not mark him married within the year, in school, unable to read/write, or disabled.
INFERRED: Thomas was born about August 1772–August 1773 if the recorded age is exact. He was probably a resident or lodger in the Maddex household on the enumeration date. Co-residence does not establish kinship. The original confirms the age conflict already visible in the derivative transcription.
SOURCE GRADE: A for the 1850 manuscript observation
INFERENCE GRADE: C for any identification with a Fauquier or 1846 Cooper Thomas
IDENTITY MATCH: The defensible identity is limited to **an elderly Virginia-born tailor, distinct from the 40-year-old Kentucky-born farmer in District 23 and from the 30–40-year-old Cooper head in 1840**. He is a plausible, unproved candidate for “Thos Caton Snr” on the 1846 Isaac Martin estate note. He is not identified as the Fauquier elder by any record examined.
CONFLICTS: At literal ages, the 1850 man would have been about 37–38 in 1810, conflicting with the Fauquier Thomas recorded 45+; about 47–48 in 1820, compatible with 45+; and about 56–57 in 1830, conflicting with the Fauquier Thomas Senior recorded 60–70. A single-man merge therefore requires age error in at least the 1810 and 1830 schedules or requires that the Fauquier Thomas identities changed between censuses. The 1840 Cooper Thomas aged 30–40 cannot normally become age 77 in ten years.
WHAT WOULD WEAKEN THIS: A complete original probate or death record identifying the Boonville tailor's wife, children, birthplace, or prior residence as a different family; a manuscript correction showing age 77 or the household placement was copied incorrectly; or proof that the 1846 “Senior” was another Thomas.
NEXT DISCRIMINATING RECORD: **Cooper County probate “Inventory of Names,” volume A, 1849–1900, Missouri State Archives reel C1997, original Caton/Cayton/Caten pages.** A locator for a Thomas estate can lead to the packet most likely to name heirs. A complete, legible index with no Thomas under all variants would weaken, but not disprove, a Cooper County death/probate explanation. A packet positively identifying incompatible heirs would materially weaken a Fauquier merge.
NEGATIVES: No examined record names Aaron as this Thomas's child. No primary Maddex marriage or kinship record was found in the bounded public-source pass. The public probate PDF was not retrievable, so no probate-index negative is claimed. Exact-name public searches did not surface an 1860 Cooper entry for the elderly man, but the complete 1860 manuscript was not visually searched and no census negative is claimed. Newspaper and cemetery access remained incomplete; search snippets are not promoted to evidence.

## The 1846 Senior/Junior note

The already-retained original is Isaac Martin's estate inventory, not a Caton estate. It records “Thos Caton Snr Thos Caton Junr” against a note dated 7 March 1846, due twelve months after date. Certification is 8 October 1850. The entry establishes an association between two men distinguished as senior and junior. It does not state father and son, residence, survival, age, wife, children, or Fauquier origin.

The new census evidence makes a two-man working hypothesis coherent: the 77-year-old Boonville tailor could be “Senior,” and the 40-year-old farmer could be “Junior.” That remains inference grade C. The note itself cannot assign either label to a census household.

## Maddex marriage and household test

A bounded public-source pass did not find a primary marriage record for the John P. Maddex aged 47 in 1850. Searchable Cooper County marriage abstracts for 1840–1849 did not contain Maddex/Maddox or John P.; the compilation expressly warns that it is incomplete and does not cover an earlier or out-of-state marriage, so this is not a marriage negative.

Secondary Cooper materials describe a John Maddex who married Mariah Sweeney in Virginia and a John P. Maddex born in 1833, husband of Mariah. That man would be about seventeen in 1850, not forty-seven. Those materials cannot identify the 1850 householder without resolving the age/name conflict. They provide no Caton relationship.

The manuscript's adult woman is Parthema G. Maddex, 39. It is reasonable to test her as the householder's wife, but the census does not state that relationship and no marriage record was recovered. No evidence establishes Thomas as her father, father-in-law, employee, boarder, or other kin.

## Answers required by the stream

1. **Most defensible identity:** An elderly Virginia-born tailor living in John P. Maddex's Boonville household in 1850, distinct from the 1840 Cooper head and 1850 District 23 farmer. He is plausibly, but not demonstrably, the “Senior” on the 1846 note. He is not proved to be the elder Fauquier Thomas.
2. **Records that could name his children:** A Thomas probate packet, will, letters of administration, administrator's bond, final settlement, partition deed, or obituary. The C1997 probate names index is the immediate locator; the packet is the evidentiary target.
3. **Whether Aaron is named:** **No.** Aaron is not named in the Boonville census, 1846 debt entry, Thomas–Elizabeth deed, Maddex material, or any verified probate record from this pass.
4. **Effect on Thomas-as-father:** The original age confirmation weakens use of the older Boonville man as corroboration for the elder Fauquier Thomas. It does not disprove the separate hypothesis that the Fauquier elder was Aaron's father. It removes an unverified identity bridge; the parentage hypothesis remains unproved.
5. **Next single record:** Original Cooper probate “Inventory of Names,” volume A, 1849–1900, reel C1997, Caton/Cayton/Caten section. Retrieve the exact Thomas locator and then the named packet. A positively incompatible wife/children or origin would weaken the Fauquier identification; a complete index with no Thomas would only modestly weaken a local-probate explanation.

## Conclusion

The original 1850 manuscript confirms the reported Boonville entry without resolving identity. The evidence supports two Cooper County Thomas identities: a younger family man whose age fits the 1840 head and the 1850 District 23 farmer, and an older Virginia-born tailor in the Maddex household. The 1846 Senior/Junior note is consistent with that pair but does not identify either man. The older man's exact reported age fits the 1820 Fauquier elder but conflicts with that elder's literal 1810 and 1830 brackets. No record examined names Aaron or any child of the older Boonville Thomas.

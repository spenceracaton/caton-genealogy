# T14 — George W. Caton of Alexandria: does he connect to Aaron?

**What is established (C121–C123).** George W. Caton, b. 1800 Alexandria, tailor;
wife Sarah H. Moore b. c.1807 Maryland; to Missouri 1829–30 (St. Louis), Boonville
from 1832; Boonville's tailor of the 1830s and a charter Mason there in 1841 with a
Laurie; ruined c.1848–49; St. Clair Co. 1850; d. 1865. Children: Joseph? (claimed by
the import, unproven), George R. c.1828 D.C., Thomas N. c.1830 Mo., Nathan T. 1832
St. Louis, Sabina c.1835, Rebecca c.1842. His father: an Irishman who fought at
Monmouth (1778) and died aged 98, alive long enough for Nathan (b. 1832) to remember him.

**What is not established.** Any record placing Aaron or Joseph in George W.'s
household, or naming George W.'s father. Sarah cannot be Aaron's mother (C123).

2. ~~1820 census, D.C.~~ **READ 12 Sep (C133–C135).** The only Caton household on
   the D.C. roll is **Michael Caton, Third Ward, Washington City**: two males 16–26
   (George W., 20, fits one), no male under 10, no male over 26. No George Caton

   Alexandria County section of the same roll (IGN 005156898) was browsed** — it is

   **Washington City** 1827–30 with Michael and Edward Caton, printers, and Nathan
   Moore, constable — so the adult George's D.C. records are Washington ones.
3. **1830 census, D.C.** (M19 roll 14, whole District, small). George Caton, tailor,
   was at 9th St W between I and K North, Washington City, when the 1830 directory was
   compiled — look for him in **Washington City** first, with a male under 5 (George
   R.). Also any Michael or Edward Caton. The import's prediction of Aaron (10) and
   Joseph (7) in some Alexandria household rests on the discarded 'came to Missouri
   at ten' claim (C120); read the roll without it.
4. **George Caton – Sarah H. Moore marriage, 5 Oct 1822** (import, unsourced). Her
   father Nathan Moore was a Washington City constable 1822–30 (C134), so this is a
   **Washington County, D.C.** marriage: FamilySearch 'District of Columbia Marriages,
   1811–1950', groom Caton 1820–25. A licence gives ages, sometimes a bondsman — a
   bondsman named Thomas or Michael Caton is the link.
5. **St. Clair Co., MO probate, 1865** — George W.'s estate (Wills v.1–3, 1865–1921).
   An administration or distribution lists heirs; Aaron and Joseph on it, or not, is
   decisive either way. Verify the 1850 household image (M432-413, p.4) on the way.
6. **Nathan T. Caton's death, Rossland BC, 30 Sep 1916** — BC death registration
   (Royal BC Museum index; image on FamilySearch) names parents; *Columbia Chronicle*
   (Dayton WA) obituary c. 4 Oct 1916 on Washington Digital Newspapers (site refuses
   automated fetches; open in a browser).
6b. **1850 census, Washington City** — Thomas Caton, printer, and Patrick Caton,
   bookbinder (C136): ages and birthplaces. A Virginia- or Ireland-born Thomas is

6c. **Daily National Intelligencer, Oct 1822** — marriage notice for George W. Caton
   and Sarah H. Moore. Chronicling America (LCCN sn83045242) blocks automated search;

7. **Alexandria artisans 1780–1820** — T. Michael Miller, *Artisans and Merchants of
   Alexandria, Virginia, 1780–1820*, vol. 1 (Heritage Books). A Caton tailor before

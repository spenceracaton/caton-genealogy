# S05 — Recover Aaron’s own statements and records retained by his immediate family

**Target in every stream:** Aaron Caton, reported born 16 May 1820 in Virginia, married Sarah Ann Gardner in Delaware County, Ohio, in August 1841, later a Missouri resident and tailor. His father and mother remain unidentified. These are investigation instructions, not newly proved relationships. [R1]

## Question and why this is independent

What did Aaron himself, or someone demonstrably close to him, say about his birthplace, earlier residence, parents, siblings or training? This route does not require the Fauquier Thomas model to be right. It can identify a county, an early acquaintance or a family relationship that tells the other streams where to look.

The documented service identity is Aaron Caton, Company E,2nd Missouri Cavalry, Merrill's Horse. The ledger also contains a disputed 1876 pension-index lead under Aaron Caten, formerly read as a different company/regiment. C184's argument that the card is probably his is not a substitute for inspecting it. Do not enter 1876 as a proved application date or order a file on the basis of that inference alone. [R1; R2:C023,C184]

## First acquisition: settle the pension index card

Open the actual T288 General Index card, not just the search result. The existing project route is FamilySearch collection 1919699; NARA also describes this index. Capture the entire card, including all application/certificate numbers, claimant names, service lines, filing dates, residence/state and cross-references. Use an alternate index or service-record comparison if a letter/number remains ambiguous. An empty certificate field should not be treated as a conclusive explanation of the application's fate without the case record. [A16]

Match on the combined name, company, regiment, known service and residence. If the card truly belongs to another unit/person, reject that identity match and record the discriminating detail. A negative in a separate soldier index cannot justify rewriting what the card says. If it is Aaron's, obtain the correct application/certificate file, including rejected or transferred-file routing when indicated.

## Second acquisition: the complete service and pension files

Service and pension files are different record groups. Do not treat a state service card or brief abstract as the complete federal record. NARA's current guidance provides separate request routes, NATF 86 for service and NATF 85 for pension records; ascertain any charge and obtain authorization before ordering. [A15-A17]

For the compiled service record, examine enlistment/descriptive papers and retained originals for the most specific birthplace or former residence, occupation, age, physical identification and addresses. A precise locality can be decisive even without a parent. Check reenlistment and discharge papers too; do not focus only on the first abstract.

For the pension case, create an affidavit table: speaker, date, relationship claimed, how long the speaker knew Aaron, where they first met, exact family statements and whether the statement appears copied from another source. Prioritize witnesses who knew him before military service. A comrade who met Aaron in 1862 is not independent evidence of his1820 birth unless the basis of knowledge is explained. A claim containing only wartime disability material is still a real file, but may not advance parentage.

NARA describes pension files as potentially rich in supporting family documents; this is a reason to inspect the case, not a promise that Aaron's application includes a Bible, birth certificate or parents. [A15]

## Third acquisition: the William Bramwell Caton archive

A prior research note identified a specific Poulin auction listing associated with Aaron's son William Bramwell Caton,1847-1941:10 August 2025, lot 3056, item 57245780, inventory 02-24320/JS. The catalog reportedly described an inscribed 1930 card, obituaries, service copies and research papers. The objects and inscription have not been read or authenticated, and present ownership is unknown. [N4; A21]

Ask for retained high-resolution front-and-back photographs and scans of the biographical/research enclosures, with their source notes. If the auction house has no copies, ask whether it can forward an inquiry to the consignor or buyer; do not seek disclosure of private customer information. Do not buy anything or imply a purchase commitment. Avoid another round of thumbnail viewing if no document text is legible.

For each item distinguish a statement plausibly by William, an identified family informant, later dealer description and modern research enclosure. A 1930 inscription could contain family knowledge but cannot be primary eyewitness evidence of 1820. An unsourced pedigree photocopy remains an unsourced pedigree even when sold beside an authentic photograph.

## Fourth acquisition: tightly targeted late-life notices

Recover or extend the exact contemporary notice underlying Aaron's reported January 1878 death and immediate-family notices that might identify surviving siblings or an origin county. Work a defined newspaper/title/date range and spelling variants, not all Missouri Catons. The known short notice in C108 is a starting point, not a discovery to repeat. Distinguish target Aaron from Aaron F., the younger Cooper County man. [R2:C108,C149-C172]

S06 owns Joseph's papers and niece report, S07 owns Ohio local documents, and S03 owns the older Thomas's estate. Send a new locality or named early associate to the relevant owner rather than opening parallel duplicate searches.

## Tests and success criteria

An explicit parent statement must be assessed for identity and knowledge, then checked against records of that parent. A precise Virginia county would materially narrow the search but would not establish a father. A statement naming a brother is useful even if the brother is not Thomas, William or Joseph.

If the pension card is not Aaron's, the particular 1876 lead fails. If the authentic files contain no family or prewar geography, stop expanding into generic military history. If the auction enclosures merely repeat existing internet pedigrees, classify them as dependent copies, not corroboration.

## Deliverable

Return a verified index-card identity, exact file-request identifiers or a complete-file analysis; separately return a provenance inventory for any family archive items. Include every newly specific locality and every witness claiming pre-1841 acquaintance. A successful first pass may be a correct pension locator rather than a parent name, but it must not end at another general catalog description.

## Required return and handling

Return `found`, `not-found`, `partial`, or `blocked`, identifying the exact record(s), image/page range, transcription, source grade, inference grade, conflicts with existing claims, and the next discriminating test. A blocked download is not a not-found result. Consult `RECORD_RETURN_TEMPLATE.md` for the complete form.

Retain full-page images plus relevant crops and source locators when permitted; do not put private correspondence, restricted archival copies, or modern personal information in a public repository without appropriate permission. Do not alter `BRIEF.md`, `claims.jsonl`, other agents' evidence files, or task frontmatter. Write a new evidence file for coordinator review. Documentary work only; no DNA.

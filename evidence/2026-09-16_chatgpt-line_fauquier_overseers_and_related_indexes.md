## G001 — Fauquier Board of Overseers of the Poor finding aid

Source: [Fauquier County (Va.) Board of Overseers of the Poor Minutes, 1804–1845](https://arvasarchive.org/catalog/vi_vi02514), Library of Virginia finding aid as displayed in the ARVAS catalog; [original LVA EAD record](https://ead.lib.virginia.edu/vivaxtf/view?docId=lva/vi02514.xml). The supplied LVA EAD URL redirects to the current catalog record.

### Finding-aid text and metadata

**Title:** Fauquier County (Va.) Board of Overseers of the Poor Minutes, 1804-1845.

The finding aid identifies the Fauquier County Circuit Court as creator, English as the language, one volume dated 1804–1845, and chronological arrangement. It describes the volume as covering assistance in money, food, and clothing; poorhouse matters; burial of the deceased; poor levies and their expenditure; and apprentice bindings. Its exact phrase for the apprenticeship content is: “as well as binding children as apprentices” (6 words).

The itemized examples are principally 1843–1845 papers: overseers’ accounts, parish levies, payments and bills for poorhouse food, fuel and medical services, certificates, and lists of overseers and accounts. One item is a list of accounts for 1847. The finding aid’s historical note says the county was formed from Prince William in 1759 and explains that Virginia’s elected Overseers of the Poor replaced Anglican vestries and churchwardens in 1780; it says children were bound out as apprentices, with boys learning trades and girls domestic skills.

Access information identifies the Library of Virginia, Archives Reference Services, 800 East Broad Street, Richmond, VA 23219; archdesk@lva.virginia.gov; (804) 692-3888. It reports no restrictions and says the access copy is Fauquier Reel 116. The preferred citation names the Local Government Records Collection, Fauquier County Court Records. Physical description: one volume and one microfilm reel. Barcode: 1125621. The acquisition note says the material arrived in shipments of Fauquier court papers and the microfilm was generated by Backstage Library Works through LVA’s Circuit Court Records Preservation Program.

The ARVAS record reports zero components and zero items available online. This is finding-aid evidence about the series, not a transcription of the underlying minutes. It confirms that the surviving volume’s stated date span covers the proposed 1826–1838 read and that apprenticeships are among its described subjects. It neither identifies a Caton entry nor establishes that a Caton was bound out.

This replaces the search-snippet basis for C195’s record-scope premise with the finding aid itself: the volume includes apprentice bindings. The actual volume still needs to be read to test whether it names Aaron, Joseph, a parent, or a master.

## G002 — APA 739 finding aid and Fauquier folder

Source: [A Guide to the Virginia Auditor of Public Accounts Overseer of the Poor Annual Reports and Checklists, 1800–1909 (APA 739)](https://arvasarchive.org/catalog/vi_vi04679), Library of Virginia finding aid as displayed in the ARVAS catalog; [original LVA EAD record](https://ead.lib.virginia.edu/vivaxtf/view?docId=lva/vi04679.xml).

### Finding-aid text and metadata

**Title:** A Guide to the Virginia Auditor of Public Accounts Overseer of the Poor Annual Reports and Checklists, 1800-1909.

The creator is the Virginia Auditor of Public Accounts (1776–1928). The collection is in English, consists of 36 boxes (extent shown as 18.75), and is arranged alphabetically in two series: Virginia cities and counties and West Virginia cities and counties. The reported years are 1800–1864, 1871–1881, 1884–1907, and 1909. The access page gives no restrictions, and the preferred citation identifies State government records collection, APA 739, Library of Virginia.

The scope description characterizes the reports as written statistics about poor whites and free Black people supported at public charge, poor rates, donations, poorhouse/workhouse populations and expenses, plus some payments to staff and service providers. It explicitly says “and sometimes minutes are included” (5 words). For the likely appearance of names, it states: “Beginning in the late 1840s - early 1850s, more detailed information is included such as the names of the poor” (19 words). It says this later detail may include age, physical condition, length and reason for assistance, moral character, and sometimes a death note. The historical note says a 20 February 1829 law required annual reports retroactive to 1 January 1800; reporting ended after 1909. Acquisition is from the Auditor of Public Accounts. Arrangement distinguishes Series I, Virginia Cities and Counties, 1800–1909, and Series II, West Virginia Cities and Counties, 1800–1862.

The container entry is Box 4, Folder 4, Fauquier County, 1829–1851. A second Fauquier entry is Box 22, Folder 5, 1874. APA 739 therefore supplies a Fauquier administrative-record route covering the 1830s, but its guide describes reports as primarily totals and expenses, says minutes appear only sometimes, and dates more detailed personal information to the late 1840s–early 1850s. It does not justify expecting a named child or parent in an 1830s report. The folder itself remains uninspected.

## G004 — Prince William Reliquary cumulative and annual index sweep

Sources: [RELIC newsletters and indexes](https://www.pwcva.gov/department/library/enewsletters); [Prince William Reliquary, Vol. 6, No. 3 (July 2007)](https://www.pwcva.gov/assets/2021-07/PWR_3-2007.pdf).

The RELIC guide describes the annual indexes as the every-name route. I checked Volumes 1–8 annual indexes and searched both available Volume 9 issue PDFs. Caton index hits were:

- **Vol. 1, p. 36:** Robert M. Caton; the referenced article places him in a 1948–1949 poll-tax list.
- **Vol. 2, pp. 73, 94:** J. A. Caton and Charles F., Gertrude, and Sarah H. Caton; the referenced entries are 1941–1949 poll-tax lists.
- **Vol. 3, p. 91:** Bertha B. and Sarah H. Caton; the article lists them as white women voters in Gainesville District in 1921 and 1922.
- **Vol. 4, p. 45:** J. W. and T. J. Caton; the referenced entries are 1920–1938 Prince William death notices (J. W. Caton, 1922; T. J. Caton, 1930).
- **Vol. 5, p. 93:** “Caton (Katon)” appears under the surname Carney, in a 1925–1928 doctor’s ledger; it is not an early Caton parentage entry.
- **Vol. 6, p. 72:** Thomas Caton, in a transcript of a 1800 Snowden Horton estate account.
- **Vols. 7–8 and both available Vol. 9 issues:** no Caton hit.

The Vol. 6, No. 3 article is “Dumfries District Court Land Causes 1793–1811,” transcribed by William M. Balderson, Jr., edited by Donald L. Wilson, continued from Vol. 6, No. 2. In the account settlement ordered filed in court, the commissioners report is dated 17 January 1800. The printed page 72 transcription reads:

> To proved A/c pd
> Thomas CATON - 5. 0

This records an account payment of £5 to a Thomas Caton within the Snowden Horton estate settlement. The published transcript is identified secondary work (grade B); it is not the original manuscript image. The entry is a new early Prince William name-and-place lead. It does not identify which Thomas Caton this was and does not establish kinship with Aaron, Thomas Sr., or Thomas Jr.

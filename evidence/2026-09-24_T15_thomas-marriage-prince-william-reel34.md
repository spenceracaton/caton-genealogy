# T15 — Thomas Caton marriage/remarriage: Prince William reel 34 and pre-1810 window

Research date: 24 September 2026. Research note only. No canonical claim, brief, registry, census-ledger, or task-frontmatter change.

STATUS: partial negative at derivative-index level; the reel 34 route is closed as a marriage-record route because the target volumes are estate fiduciary bond books, not marriage bond books.

## Result

No Thomas Caton marriage or remarriage candidate was found in Prince William County for either requested window, approximately 1795–1809 or 1810–1819. No bride, surety, consentor, parent, or witness was identified.

The central result is a record-type correction. The two Prince William volumes previously treated as the best remaining marriage-adjacent route—**Bond Book 1815–1826** and **Bond Book 1815–1833** on Library of Virginia reel 34—are estate administration/executor books. They do not contain marriage bonds. Their lack of a Caton entry cannot be used as evidence against a Thomas Caton marriage.

The second correction concerns survival. The current T15 frontmatter says a Prince William marriage register for 1794–1850 survives. The official Library of Virginia inventory and Prince William County's RELIC research guide do not support that statement. RELIC states that no official Prince William marriage records remain before 1854; the LVA inventory begins its marriage holdings with 1854–1865 certificates and 1859 marriage-register indexes. The official pre-1854 county marriage route is therefore a record-loss boundary, not an unsearched surviving register.

## Scope and exclusions

This pass tested:

- the record type and name coverage of the two reel 34 bond books;
- the RELIC compiled marriage index for Prince William residents, which aggregates non-register sources from 1731–1930;
- the surviving published extract of the Dumfries Episcopal Church marriage register for 1818–1824; and
- whether any result justified retrieval of an original bond, consent, or return.

The already completed Fauquier, Loudoun, Culpeper, and Alexandria searches were not repeated. No original marriage bond or consent was retrieved because no candidate record surfaced.

Variants tested where the interface or extracted text allowed exact checking: `Caton`, `Cayton`, `Catton`, `Caten`, `Cathon`, `Catron`, `Cation`, `Cason`, `Catten`, `Cotton`, `Katon`, `Kayton`, `Keaton`, `Kitton`, and `Kitten`.

## 1. Library of Virginia inventory: record type and survival boundary

The [Library of Virginia Prince William County microfilm inventory](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/ccmf/VA/VA227) places both target books under **Bonds/Commissions/Oaths**, not under Marriage Records and Vital Statistics:

- reel 34: Bond Book, 1815–1826, unpaged index, 168 folios;
- reel 34: Bond Book, 1815–1833, unpaged index, 148 folios.

The same inventory's marriage section begins with:

- reel 119: Marriage Certificates, 1854–1865;
- reel 33: General Index to Marriage Register, 1859–1946, male and female;
- reel 33: Marriage Register No. 1, 1859–1936; and
- reel 119 onward: Marriage Licenses beginning in 1865.

The inventory warns that many pre-Civil War Prince William records were lost, destroyed, or stolen in 1863. It exposes no official register, license, bond, or certificate series covering either 1795–1809 or 1810–1819.

Prince William County's official [*Roots in Prince William* research guide](https://www.pwcva.gov/assets/documents/library/Roots_in_PW-2011.pdf), pp. 17–18, states that “There are no official marriages remaining for the period prior to 1854.” It identifies the local 1731–1930 marriage database as an ongoing compilation from many sources that does **not** include the official register. These are collection/survival statements, not evidence that Thomas did not marry in Prince William.

## 2. Reel 34 bond books: estate bonds, not marriage bonds

Ronald Ray Turner's 398-page transcription, [*Prince William County Virginia Bond Book 1732–1847*](https://historicprincewilliam.org/pwcvirginia/documents/BondBook.pdf) (copyright 2007), contains the exact two target books. The PDF was downloaded and its embedded text searched on 24 September 2026. Downloaded file size: 3,113,706 bytes; SHA-256: `751c4e3346e457442727e3ca0bce18fe589070ede422b39f2c41768147a6aea4`.

### Book 3, 1815–1826

The first entry, `(3-01)`, dated 6 November 1815, binds John Linton as administrator of Mary Brundidge, late Mary Linton. The last entry, `(3-162)`, dated 6 November 1826, binds James Florence as administrator of William Florence. Entries sampled throughout use the recurring estate-administration condition: inventory the deceased person's goods, administer them according to law, account to the court, and yield the estate if a will and executor later appear.

This is the LVA-listed 168-folio Bond Book 1815–1826. Its function is administration of estates, not authorization of marriages.

### Book 4, 1815–1833

The first entry, `(4-01)`, dated 6 November 1815, binds John Oglevy as executor of Redwood/Renwood Evans. The last entry, `(4-148)`, dated 5 June 1833, binds Alfred S. P. Windsor as administrator de bonis non with the will annexed of Sampson Windsor. The entries are executor and related testamentary-estate bonds.

This is the LVA-listed 148-folio Bond Book 1815–1833. Its function is estate administration under wills, not marriage.

### Name and record-type search

Exact, case-insensitive searches of the PDF's extracted text found:

- no occurrence of `marriage`; and
- no occurrence of any target surname variant listed above.

The back-of-volume surname index was also checked at the C and K sections; it contains no target variant. This is a bounded no-hit in Turner's derivative transcription. The underlying reel image was not inspected, so the no-hit is not promoted to original-image proof. More importantly, even a perfect no-hit would concern estate bonds, not a marriage.

## 3. Prince William compiled marriage index: pre-1810 and later windows

RELIC's official [Digital Archives page](https://www.pwcva.gov/department/library/RELIC-digital-archives) links **Marriage Records of Residents of Prince William County, 1731–1930**, explicitly described as compiled from various sources other than the official register. The collection's all-names surname index was inspected in its public mirror's page viewer:

- [C-surname index page 4](https://www.yumpu.com/en/document/view/46327260/marriages-file-eservices-of-prince-william-county-virginia/4), spanning Calvert through Daniel; and
- [K-surname index page 12](https://www.yumpu.com/en/document/view/46327260/marriages-file-eservices-of-prince-william-county-virginia/12), spanning Keyes through Kulp.

No listed entry matched the target C- or K-spelling variants. Because this compilation spans 1731–1930, the index check covers both requested windows at the compilation layer, including the approximately 1795–1809 first-marriage window.

This result is **not** a record-level negative. RELIC describes the database as ongoing and assembled from miscellaneous sources after the official records were lost. The index can establish that no target variant is presently listed in the compilation; it cannot establish that no marriage occurred.

## 4. Dumfries Episcopal Church extract

The public transcript [“Marriages at Dumfries Episcopal Church”](https://genealogytrails.com/vir/princewilliam/marr_dumphrieschurch_1815-1824.html) says its entries come from the Dumfries Episcopal Church Register and cites *Magazine of Virginia Genealogy*, vol. 24, no. 1 (February 1986). Despite the page title's 1815 start, the displayed list runs from **28 July 1818 through 25 February 1824**.

No Thomas Caton or target surname variant appears. For the requested remarriage window, this closes only the published church-extract segment from 28 July 1818 through 30 December 1819. It does not cover 1810–17, it is not the original register image, and it cannot support a countywide negative.

## Evidence assessment

**STATES DIRECTLY:** The official LVA inventory classifies the two reel 34 targets outside the marriage series; the transcribed contents are estate administration/executor bonds. Official Prince William research guidance says no official county marriage records remain before 1854. The available derivative marriage compilation has no indexed target variant, and the displayed Dumfries extract has none in its 1818–19 portion.

**INFERRED:** Nothing in this pass identifies either woman in Thomas's 1810 or 1820 household. The 1810-to-1820 female-age change still permits both live explanations: a remarriage between censuses or an age/enumeration issue involving the same wife. The marriage hypothesis is not strengthened or weakened by the estate-bond no-hit.

**SOURCE GRADES:** B for the record-type and survival findings, based on an official repository inventory/guide corroborated by a complete derivative transcription; C for the surname no-hits in the RELIC compilation and Dumfries transcript; no grade for an original marriage record because none was located.

**CONFLICTS:** The statement in T15 that a Prince William register for 1794–1850 survives conflicts with the official LVA holdings inventory and RELIC's express statement that no official marriages remain before 1854. The earlier T25 description of reel 34 as the highest-value marriage-adjacent route is superseded at the record-type level: these are estate fiduciary books.

**NEGATIVE LIMIT:** Do not encode “Thomas Caton did not marry in Prince William.” Encode only that no target variant was found in the specified derivative indexes/transcripts and that the official pre-1854 marriage record series is lost.

## Conclusion

No direct marriage evidence was found. Reel 34 should be removed from the Thomas marriage search plan because its target books are estate bonds. The Prince William pre-1854 official marriage-record route is closed by record loss, while the nonofficial RELIC compilation and the surviving 1818–19 Dumfries extract produce only bounded derivative-index no-hits. The identity of Thomas's wife or wives remains unproved.

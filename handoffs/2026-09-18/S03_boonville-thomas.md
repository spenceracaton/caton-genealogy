# S03 — Identify the 77-year-old Boonville tailor Thomas Caton (1850) and trace his estate

**For:** a ChatGPT research agent with a FamilySearch sign-in. **Prepared:** 18 Sep 2026. **Coordinator:** Spencer Caton via Claude Code.

**Target of every stream:** Aaron Caton, b. 16 May 1820, Virginia; m. Sarah Ann Gardner, Delaware Co., Ohio, Aug 1841; tailor; d. Boonville, Mo., 17 Jan 1878. Parents unknown. Nothing in this file is a proved relationship.

## Question

Who was the Thomas Caton transcribed as a 77-year-old Virginia-born tailor in Boonville, Cooper County, Missouri, in 1850? Where did he die, and did any record name his children or former residence? Can he be connected, or shown unconnected, to the Fauquier Thomases, to Aaron, or to Joseph?

Why it ranks high: an elderly Virginia-born tailor of the right generation in the town where Aaron died (Sixth Street, 1878) and where Joseph and George W. Caton worked as tailors (C191). Why it is not assumed: at 77 in 1850 he was born c.1772–73, later than the 1810 Fauquier head (45+, so b. ≤1765) and the 1830 head (60–70, b. 1760–70) allow. Do not pick whichever age helps. Earlier conversation overstated that the 1828 groom had been "convincingly separated" from this man; it has not.

## What is already in hand — do not re-acquire

Maddex**. A second entry, household 1038: `Thomas, 40, Kentucky`, with Lucinda, James, Newton, Nimrod. Original not yet retrieved.

Grade C. Pleasant Hill Baptist cemetery transcription: Elizabeth Lowrey 1804–1883, wife of Thomas William Caton. Neither links the 1828 groom to the 77-year-old.
- Cooper probate index PDF (mogenweb) is garbled; Cooper wills/admin 1818–1847 compilation does not cover a death after 1850. Neither is a negative.

## Sequence

1. **1850 manuscript.** FS "United States Census, 1850", Cooper County, Missouri (NARA M432; working roll 397 — verify in the viewer). Retain the full Maddex household page, column header, and the preceding/following page if the household breaks. Transcribe every member: name, age, sex, colour, occupation, real estate, birthplace, marks in the "married within the year", "attended school", "cannot read/write", "deaf/dumb/blind/insane/idiotic/pauper/convict" columns. Note corrections and possible row shifts. Do not assign relationships the record does not state.
   1b. Retain the household-1038 Thomas (40, Kentucky) page separately. Read the 1840 p. 145 `Ths Caton` row's male brackets: is there a male 60–70 or 70–80?
2. **The Maddex household.** Limited question: why would this Thomas live there? Get John P. Maddex's marriage (Cooper County marriage records on FS), his wife's maiden name, and any obituary or probate naming family. Maddex/Maddox variants only with identity support. A married daughter is a lead to test, not a maiden name to invent. If several unrelated adults are in the house, say so.
3. **Bracket Thomas after 1850.** 1860 census, Cooper County (all townships and Boonville). Cooper County tax lists, Boonville directories or newspapers 1850–1865 (SHSMO digital newspapers; Chronicling America Missouri titles), Walnut Grove and other Boonville cemetery transcriptions. Absence from 1860 does not date a death. Extend beyond 1865 only on a locator.
4. **Probate and heirs.** FS "Missouri, Probate Records, 1750–1998", Cooper County: original index images (not the garbled PDF), administrations, bonds, inventories, sale bills, settlements, distributions, guardianships, and deeds by heirs, 1850–1870. Index every named administrator and heir. Distinguish heir from purchaser, creditor, witness, guardian, and administrator; a sale bill full of Catons may be customers.
5. **Backward trail, bounded.** Only if a 1850–70 record names a former Virginia residence or a relative: follow that one document. Do not force an unnamed older male in a Laurie or Maddex household into being Thomas. If an estate names William, Ann Laurie, or another proposed relative, transcribe the wording for S01/S08; do not match it yourself.

## Pre-registered outcomes

- Manuscript shows a different age, name, or trade → correct the baseline first; the transcription was the only source.
- A separate life history (different wife, children, or a document giving him other parents) → breaks the Fauquier identification without proving another candidate.
- Household, estate, and 1850–65 windows genuinely tested with no connection → park the identity link; an unrelated elderly tailor removes a distraction.
- Do not use surname + trade + migration resemblance as identity.

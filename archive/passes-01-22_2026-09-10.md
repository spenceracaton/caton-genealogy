## 3. Alfred Caton hypothesis

### Alfred Caton
Working profile:
- born 29 Jan 1805
- birthplace often given as Leesburg, Virginia; some compiled records mislabel it Leesburg, Maryland
- married Sarah Chaddock/Chadduck in Ohio County, Virginia, 1828
- lived in Wheeling / Belmont County, Ohio region in 1830s
- later Missouri
- died reportedly 9 Nov 1889, but exact death county is still not independently established
- children include Charles Caton, John Harrison Caton (b. 1838 Belmont County), Melinda J. Caton

Why Alfred matters:
- His reported birth date makes him 15 at Aaron’s birth. Fatherhood is possible but less likely; age alone does not exclude it.
- He is geographically well placed to be an older brother, half-brother, uncle, or cousin.
- His family occupied the Wheeling/Belmont region during Aaron's childhood.
- He later migrated to Missouri.

### George/Jemima claim — DOWNGRADED
Compiled trees identify Alfred's parents as:
- George Thomas Caton
- Jemima Prall VanKirk-Caton

Do **not** accept this.

Independent Washington County, Pennsylvania records prove a Thomas Caton + Susanna family whose children included:
- George Caton
- Milche Caton, wife of Thomas Praul/Prall
- Prescylla Caton, wife of Gideon VanKirk

Thus **Caton, Prall/Praul, and VanKirk genuinely occur in one family network**, but this creates a strong possibility that later tree-builders combined surnames from collateral relatives to create “Jemima Prall VanKirk.”

No independent record has yet been found proving:
- a Jemima Prall VanKirk married George Caton
- George was Alfred's father

Track the documented man simply as **George Caton, son of Thomas and Susanna**, unless a primary source supplies another name.

### Alfred probate
- Warren County probate index contains no Alfred Caton estate through 1900.
- Do not infer Warren County residence merely from the 1869 Melinda litigation.
- Montgomery and Lincoln counties remain probate/obituary targets.
- Reported Truxton/Lincoln County death is still compiled-tree evidence, not independently proven.

## 4. Thomas and Susanna Caton of Washington County, Pennsylvania

This is a **real, independently documented family**, but connection to Aaron is not proven.

Research value:

- This family supplies a genuine George Caton and explains the Prall/VanKirk surnames.
- It is a plausible ancestral network, but **no bridge to Aaron or Alfred has yet been proven**.

## 5. 1820 Harrison County, Virginia lead

A significant census lead was developed, but it remains provisional.

### What was found
- The 1820 Ohio County, Virginia index appears to contain **no Caton household**, despite Aaron's reported Wheeling birth in May 1820.
- Alexander Gosney is present in the area; **Elizabeth Caton married Alexander Gosney in Ohio County in 1818**, proving Caton presence near Wheeling shortly before Aaron's birth.
- Harrison County, Virginia (now WV), roughly 70–90 miles southeast of Wheeling, has a CATON entry on page 88.

Comparison of two transcriptions/indexes suggests:
- independent page-88 index: CATON and CASTOE
- FamilySearch-derived/OCR transcription: **David “Caster”** and **Jane “Castoe”**
- This makes it highly plausible that “David Caster” is actually the indexed **David Caton**.

Provisional reconstructed David household:
- head: male 26–44
- female 26–44
- **two males under 10**
- **three females under 10**

Aaron, an infant in 1820, could fit one of the two boys.

### Important limitation
This is **not proof that David was Aaron's father**.
No later record has yet connected Aaron to David.

A Harrison-area Theophilus Caton born c.1804/05 married there in 1829, but David's 1820 household lacks a male aged 10–15, so Theophilus cannot simply be assumed to be an older son living in David's household.

### Bad submitted pedigree discarded
A submitted tree linked a Harrison John Caton to William Caton + Jane Hargreaves. Independent Lancashire evidence showed a James Hargreaves Caton marrying in Lancaster, England in 1812 with William Caton as witness. This strongly suggests a transatlantic tree merge. Do not use that pedigree.

## 7. George W. Caton — Missouri tailor lead

This is currently one of the most interesting FAN-club leads.

A **George W. Caton**, born about 1800 in Virginia:
- married Sarah H. Moore in Washington, D.C., in 1822
- was a **tailor**
- was established in Missouri by the early 1830s
- a Cooper County history places George W. Caton, tailor, in Boonville in the 1830–1840 period
- 1850 census: George W. Caton, age about 50, born Virginia, occupation tailor, in St. Clair County, Missouri
- children reportedly include George R. (born c.1828 Washington, D.C.) and **Nathan Thomas Caton** (born 1832 St. Louis)

Why he matters:
Aaron was also:
- Virginia-born
- a tailor
- later in Missouri

The combination of surname + Virginia origin + skilled trade + Missouri migration is more probative than surname/geography alone. Tailoring could have been learned through family/apprenticeship networks.

Aaron may also have had infant twin sons in 1853 named:
- Levi Andrew Caton
- **Aaron Nathan Caton**

These child names currently come from compiled/WikiTree-style material and require independent confirmation. If correct, the recurrence of **Nathan** alongside George W.'s son Nathan Thomas is suggestive, not proof.

### Important caution: multiple George W. Catons
A separate **George Wesley Caton** lived in Muskingum County, Ohio:
- married Mildred Buckley
- both described in an 1891 county biography as Virginia natives
- daughter Mildred Caton born Muskingum County in 1841
- George W. Caton's will appears in Muskingum County Will Book D, p. 553
- cemetery indexing places a Moses Caton (1767–1839) and George W. Caton (d. 1847) in the Roseville community

Do **not** merge the Missouri tailor George W. with the Muskingum George Wesley without evidence.

## 15. Research standard

Use a simple evidence hierarchy:

**A — Primary / near-primary**
- census image
- marriage bond/license
- probate/will
- deed
- military service record
- contemporary church/cemetery record
- death certificate
- contemporary newspaper

**B — Strong secondary**
- county history or biography based on identified subject/family
- scholarly/local-history publication with sources

**C — Lead only**
- FamilySearch collaborative tree
- Ancestry public tree
- WikiTree
- Find a Grave biography without cited records
- unsourced genealogy pages

Never convert a C-level relationship into the working tree without A/B corroboration.

For same-name men, separate by:
- age
- spouse
- occupation
- children
- county
- migration timeline

## 19. Third research pass — 9–10 September 2026: new leads and current frontier

This section records findings developed after the second-pass handoff. Unless explicitly marked A/B-level, treat web indexes, compiled genealogies, and search snippets as leads requiring inspection of the underlying record.

### A. George W. Caton / Missouri tailor cluster strengthened, but kinship remains unproven

The 1850 St. Clair County, Missouri census transcription independently places **George W. Caton**, about 50, born Virginia, as a **tailor**. His son **George R. Caton**, about 22, is also listed as a tailor; another son appears as Thomas N./Nathan Thomas. This makes tailoring demonstrably a family occupation in George W.'s immediate household rather than merely a one-person occupational coincidence.

This increases the relevance of George W. to Aaron because Aaron was also Virginia-born, a tailor, and later in Missouri. It still does **not** prove a relationship.

The Cooper County history already places George W. Caton, tailor, in Boonville in the 1830–1840 period. The central-Missouri Caton surname by itself is weak evidence because multiple distinct Caton families were present there.

### B. Joseph Caton is Aaron's near-contemporary

A Chariton County 1850 census transcription gives:
- **Joseph Caton**, 28, born Virginia
- Harriet, 20, born Ohio
- DeCoursey, 2, born Missouri
- Edgar, 4 months, born Missouri

This aligns closely with the inspected 1898 James Monroe Dumm biography already preserved in the evidence pack, which identifies Joseph and Harriet and sons Eugene DeCourcey and Thomas Edgar.

**Implication:** Joseph was born about 1822, only about two years after Aaron. If related, brother/cousin is much more plausible than Joseph being an older-generation ancestor.

## 20. Fourth research pass — 10 September 2026: the transatlantic question

**Research question changed.** Sections 1–19 answer "who were Aaron's parents?".
This section answers a different question that had not previously been worked:
**where in England did the Caton line come from?** The two questions are linked
but not the same, and the deep end can be attacked independently of Aaron.

Verbatim extracts of every source cited here are in

### A. The central finding: in this family, "Caton" is a late Americanisation of KITTON / KITTEN

Every contemporary record of the immigrant generation spells the name **Kitton**
or **Kitten**. Not one spells it Caton. The Caton spelling appears only later, and
the changeover is reportedly visible in Washington County, Pennsylvania around 1790.

Contemporary attestations, in order:

| Date | Record | Spelling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1675 | Maryland land patent, Liber 18 f. 329 — headright for a transported servant | **Kitton**, Theophilus |
| 1688 | Will of John Acton, Anne Arundel Co. (Wills 6.1) | **Catherine Kitton** |
| 1699 | Deed, John Gray → Theophilus Kitton, "Grays Luck", 361 acres | **Kitton** |
| 1720s | Petitions/Acts of Assembly, Baltimore Co. | **Edward Kitton**, **Theophilus Kitton** |
| 1724–31 | Provincial Court Land Records | **Catharina Kitten**, plantatrix |
| 1727 | Act of Assembly ch. 19 confirming the 1699 deed | **Kitton** |
| 1734 | Will of Katherine Kitten, Anne Arundel Co. (Wills 21.96) | **Kitten** (C-level, verify) |
| 1790 | Washington Co., PA census, Amwell Twp. — reportedly "Kitten" for the man otherwise called Theophilus Caton | **Kitten** (C-level, verify) |

**Consequence for the England question.** The old folk explanation — that an
English "Caton" was misheard as "Kitten" — runs the wrong way. The documented
direction is Kitton/Kitten → Caton. So the English target is **not** Caton of
Lancashire, and the previously discarded Lancashire pedigree (section 5) is
doubly dead. The target is the surname **Kitton**.

### B. The Maryland immigrant, documented at A level

**Theophilus Kitton**
- Transported to Maryland **1675**; recorded in the Land Office headright index,
  Patent Liber 18, folio 329 (MSA SC 4341). Compiled material adds that he came
  "with 34 other people" — plausible for a single patent claim, unverified.
- Married **Catherine Acton**, who appears by name as **Catherine Kitton** in the
  1688 will of **John Acton** of Anne Arundel County. Compiled material makes her
  Acton's sister and the widow of John Buck; the will's own phrasing groups her
  with a named sister and three other legatees.
- Bought **"Grays Luck", 361 acres**, from John Gray by deed of **16 November 1699**,
  land then in Baltimore County, later Anne Arundel.
- **Planter**; dead by 1727 (styled "late of Baltemore County"), reportedly d. 1711.
- **Son: Edward Kitton**, stated as such in the 1727 Act — this father/son link is
  primary, not compiled.

Note the Anne Arundel County circle around the Acton will: Marriott, Howard,
Gaither, and **Dorsey**. "Dorsey" later turns up as a *given* name among the
Amwell Township, Washington Co., PA Catons, and "Greenberry" — an Anne Arundel
County given name derived from Col. Nicholas Greenberry — turns up in the
Muskingum branch (section 6). These are Maryland naming fingerprints carried
west, and they support the Maryland origin of the Pennsylvania Catons.

### C. The English hypothesis: the Kitton family of Spalding, Lincolnshire — NEW, UNPROVEN

Parish register of **St Mary and St Nicholas, Spalding, Lincolnshire** (FreeREG):

- **George Kitton m. Susan Spaine, 11 May 1648.**
- Baptisms: George 12 Jan 1650/1; William 16 Jan 1656/7;
  **Theophilus, 6 Mar 1658 — "father George Kitton, labourer; mother Susan."**
- **George Kitton buried 9 April 1660.**
- **Theophilus baptised 4 May 1660 — the register reads "Theophilus ye sonne of
  widdow Kitton (Posthumus)."**
- **Susan Kitton buried 1 April 1662.**

Read together: George Kitton, a labourer, died in the spring of 1660; his widow
Susan bore a posthumous son a month later and named him Theophilus, the elder
Theophilus of 1658 having evidently died. Susan herself was buried in 1662. That
leaves **a Theophilus Kitton, orphan son of a Fenland labourer, born May 1660**.

Searching FreeREG across every county:
- **No burial of any Theophilus Kitton in England, 1600–1800.**
- **No marriage of any Theophilus Kitton in England, 1600–1750.**
- No Theophilus Kitten, Ketton, Catton or Caton baptism 1600–1700 anywhere.

He simply stops appearing in England.

**Why this fits.** A pauper orphan of a labourer, aged **15 in 1675**, is the
textbook profile of an indentured servant shipped to the Chesapeake. Fifteen is
the *typical* servant age; the compiled American estimate of "b. abt 1653"
(making him 22) is an unsourced back-projection and must not be used to exclude
the 1660 baptism. Forename and surname are both uncommon, and the combination
in the right decade is a strong signal. The Spalding family also used **George,
Edward, William, Susan(na), Thomas and Mary** — the same stock of names the
Maryland Kittons and the Pennsylvania Catons went on to use.

**Where Kitton belongs geographically.** It is a rare Fenland surname. Pre-1700
FreeREG entries cluster in South Holland, Lincolnshire (Spalding, Pinchbeck,
Whaplode, Holbeach, Deeping St James, Fosdyke) and across the county line into
the west Norfolk marshland (West Walton), with a separate unrelated family at
Shrawley, Worcestershire. After 1700 the centre of gravity moves into Norfolk
proper — consistent with the 1891 figure that roughly half of all UK Kittons
were then in Norfolk. England had only ~63 Kittons in the 1881 census.

**Status: HYPOTHESIS.** No document links the Spalding Theophilus to the Maryland
Theophilus. Do not enter it in a tree. It is the first specific English placement
this project has had, and it is testable.

**What would kill it.** Any Spalding-area record of Theophilus Kitton after 1675
(burial, marriage, a child's baptism naming him as father, a settlement or
apprenticeship record). Also: FreeREG's coverage is uneven — Spalding is
thoroughly transcribed and most parishes are not — so a second Theophilus Kitton
may be sitting in an untranscribed register elsewhere. This must be tested
against FamilySearch, FindMyPast and Ancestry England baptism indexes before the
hypothesis is treated as anything more than a lead.

### D. Corrections and upgrades to earlier sections

- **Section 5 (bad Lancashire pedigree).** Correctly discarded then, and now for

- **Section 6 (Theophilus / Muskingum cluster).** No longer an unconnected

- `"Theophilus Kitton" OR "Theophilus Kitten"` — **0 results**.
- `Kitton` — 134 results; **Norfolk Record Office 24**, Lincolnshire Archives 1.
  Probate and service records name Kittons of Bagthorpe, Fakenham, Little Hockham,
  Great Ringstead, Norwich, Worstead, Salhouse — all Norfolk.
- Confirms the two spellings are one name: **WO 121/186/170, "WILLIAM KITTEN alias
  WILLIAM KITTON, Born NORWICH, Norfolk."**

**Honest correction to section 20C.** At national level the surname's centre of
gravity is **Norfolk**, not Lincolnshire. FreeREG's 17th-century concentration at
Spalding is substantially an artefact of that parish being well transcribed while
most are not. So the Spalding candidate now rests **on the forename alone** —
Theophilus is rare, and a Theophilus Kitton born 1660 who vanishes from England
matches a Theophilus Kitton shipped from London in 1675 at the standard servant age
of fifteen. That is still a good argument. It is not a geographic one, and **an
untranscribed Norfolk Theophilus Kitton cannot presently be excluded.**

### D. Where the four joins now stand

1. **Spalding Kittons → Theophilus Kitton, Maryland 1675.** Hypothesis. Unchanged.
   Rests on the forename and on his disappearance from English records.
2. **Theophilus Kitton → the Maryland Kitten family.** Proven. Now with the
   emigration record itself.
3. **Maryland Kittens → the Caton family of Amwell Twp., Washington Co., PA.**
   Upgraded from "strong but not closed" to **substantially closed on the name
   question**: the 1790 schedule shows Kitten where later records show Caton. What
   is still outstanding is the *parentage* link — the Sarah Fisher Rowles will.
4. **Washington Co. PA Catons → the Wheeling/Belmont Catons → Aaron.** Unproven.
   Untouched by this pass. Still the load-bearing gap.

### E. What is left, and who can do it

**Cannot be done from here — needs a paid subscription or a records request:**
1. **Norfolk baptisms 1640–1670 for Theophilus Kitton/Kitten/Ketton.** The single
   most decisive remaining check on the England question. FindMyPast, Ancestry or
   FamilySearch England baptism indexes; or the Norfolk Record Office.
2. **Spalding overseers of the poor / churchwardens' accounts and apprenticeship
   indentures, c. 1670–1676**, Lincolnshire Archives. A pauper orphan bound out or
   shipped to the plantations is minuted by name. This is the record that could
   *prove* join 1.
3. **Will of Sarah Fisher Rowles**, Maryland Prerogative Court — the asserted proof
   that the Amwell Thomas was a son of Edward Kitten and Rachel Rowles. Baldwin's
   *Maryland Calendar of Wills* vols. 3–8 are not freely digitised; vols. 1, 2 and 6
   are, and were checked (nothing relevant).
4. **Katherine Kitten's 1734 Anne Arundel will** (Wills 21.96; inventory 20.164),
   for verification against the printed Calendar, vol. 7.

**Can be done by you, and is worth more than all of the above:**
5. **Y-DNA.** Still the highest-yield action available and still not started. You
   are an unbroken patrilineal Caton. A FamilyTreeDNA Y-37 (Big Y-700 if the budget

   Aaron's male line belongs to this family at all — and can match living English

### F. Discipline, restated

- Aaron's parents remain **unknown**, and nothing in this pass changed that.
- The Maryland immigrant and his emigration are now documented at A level.
- The English origin is still **a hypothesis about the immigrant of a family Aaron
  has not been proven to belong to**, and the geographic argument for Lincolnshire

  in 1660 is the only candidate found so far", not "the family came from Spalding".

### A. Aaron Caton's 1850 household — FOUND (A level)

Found through the USGenWeb Census Project's complete county transcription, not by

**Three things here are new:**

1. **Julia was born in Massachusetts, about 1843.** Aaron and Sarah married in
   Delaware County in August 1841, were in **Massachusetts** by about 1843, and
   were back in Ohio by about 1845. No previous source in this project mentions a
   New England interval. Massachusetts has kept statewide vital records since
   1841, and a birth record for Julia would name both parents, Aaron's occupation
   and residence, and possibly his birthplace to the town.
2. **Mary E. Caton, b. c.1845 Ohio, is a child not previously recorded anywhere.**
3. Nancy at 9/12 in August 1850 **independently corroborates** the compiled
   "Nancy Roseman Caton, b. 10 November 1849". Section 12's compiled child list
   is looking sounder.

Completeness: the county index runs to 22,258 lines and contains no other Caton
under any spelling variant.

### B. Aaron's Civil War service card — RETRIEVED (A level)

Missouri State Archives Soldiers' Records, card 2199, free image.

> **Caton, Aaron. Age 44. Pvt., Co. E, 2 Reg't Cav. Vols. Captain Norville.
> Enlisted Aug. 15, 1861 at Chillicothe, Mo. Mustered in Sept. 3, 1861 at Benton
> Bks, Mo. Reenlisted Jan. 5, 1864. Residence, Chillicothe, Mo. Mustered out
> Sept. 19, 1865 at Nashville, Tenn.**

Four corrections and one large new fact:

- **Enlisted 15 August 1861**, not 7 September. Section 1 should be amended.
- **Age given as 44**, implying birth c.1817. The 1850 census gives age 30,
  implying c.1820, and the family date is 16 May 1820. One is wrong. Flagged, not
  resolved.
- **Aaron survived the war.** He re-enlisted as a veteran volunteer on 5 January
  1864 and mustered out on 19 September 1865 at Nashville — four years' service.
  His life after 1865 is now an open, dated research window rather than a blank.
- **Residence of record: Chillicothe, Livingston County** — he lived there, he did
  not merely enlist there. For 1860, lead with Livingston County. Against that,
  William was at Sturgeon, Boone County, in October 1862; search both.
- Company E's captain was **Norville**.

### C. William B. Caton's service card — RETRIEVED (A level)

### D. William Bramwell Caton's 1901 biography — RETRIEVED (B level)

### E. Corrections this pass forces

- **Section 19C.** The "Julia B. Caton, superintendent 1889–1891" in the Cowley
  County officer list is **William's wife, Julia Blankenmeister Caton** — not a
  daughter, and not Aaron's Massachusetts-born Julia. Nothing may be built on it.
  Separately, the pass *confirms* section 19C's reasoning: Harry A. Caton was born
  c.1870 and the family did not reach Winfield until October 1879, so the adult
  tailor "Harry Caton" in the 1880 directory is still unexplained and the Eugene
  DeCourcey hypothesis still stands, unproven.
- **Section 19F is closed.** The lone 1850 Ohio County CATON on p.160b is
  **Harrison Caton, 14, born Virginia**, living in the Wheeling household of John
  Boring, bricklayer, with two other young tradesmen — an apprentice or boarder,
  not a son. Born c.1836. Alfred Caton's son is recorded elsewhere as **John
  Harrison Caton, b. 1838 Belmont County**; close, not identical, do not merge.
  Harrison is the **only** Caton in the whole county in 1850.
- **Section 3 (Alfred).** The Chaddock family is present in Ohio County in 1850 —
  **John Chaddock, 39, b. Pa; Malinda, 40, b. Ohio** — and Alfred's daughter was
  **Melinda J. Caton**. The Chaddock identification of Alfred's wife is now
  supported by a naming pattern, not just a marriage-index spelling.
- **Section 10 (Elizabeth Caton Gosney).** Alexander Gosney is gone from Ohio

- **Section 2 / section 19K — the Cooper County quarantine needs rethinking.**
  The direct line lived in **Cooper County from 1866 to 1879**: William apprenticed
  at Boonville, married there in 1869, and left for Kansas in 1879. That is the
  same county as George W. Caton the Boonville tailor, the William/Aaron F. Caton
  household, and Joseph Caton in later life. Keeping the *identities* separate
  remains right. Treating the Cooper County Catons as an unrelated cluster is no
  longer safe. In particular, the quarantined **Aaron Caton – Nancy Johnson
  marriage, Cooper County, 12 October 1869** moves from "probably a different man"
  to **"plausibly the direct-line Aaron, unproven"** — right county, right year,
  and Aaron demonstrably alive since September 1865. The missing piece is

# How Aaron's parentage can still be validated — ranked tests (14 Sep 2026, rev. evening)

State: Aaron Caton, b. 16 May 1820 Virginia, is a son of the Broad Run (Prince William
Co.) Caton family — almost certainly of one of two brothers, **Thomas Sr.** (Fauquier;
b. 1760–65; two boys under ten in 1820) or **Moses** (Fairfax; b. 1767; one boy under ten
in 1820 — **but Moses's boy is his known son Moses B. Jr., b. Jan 1811 (C181)**, so only
Thomas Sr.'s two boys under ten are unexplained). Neither had the boys at home in 1830.
Thomas leads on Joseph's Fauquier birth (1823), the 1898 "uncle Joseph" article, the
Cooper Co. cluster Aaron died among, and now the 1820 arithmetic.
Every test below either names a parent outright or moves the balance between the brothers.
Ordered by (chance it exists) × (what it proves) ÷ (effort).

## A. Records that would NAME a parent

1. **Fauquier personal-property tax, 1833–1840 — Thomas Caton's tithables.** A white
   male over 16 was a tithable. Aaron turned 16 in May 1836, Joseph in 1839. If the list
   shows "Caton, Thomas — 2" (or 3) tithables in 1836–39, the sons were in his house
   before the family left for Ohio. Moses's Fairfax list 1836–38 is the control. *Full-Text
   `Caton`, Fauquier, 1833–1840; open each year.* One evening; decisive if positive.
2. **Thomas Sr.'s second-marriage bond, 1810–19** — not in Fauquier (C173), not Loudoun
   (C175), not Culpeper to 1815 (C182). Prince William register 1794–1850, then Fairfax,
   Stafford, Culpeper 1816–19. Names Aaron's probable
   mother; a consent names her father. Then test her name against Aaron's daughters
   (Julia Ann 1843, Mary E. 1845, Nancy Roseman 1849): a first daughter named for the
   father's mother is the Virginia norm.
3. **Thomas Sr.'s estate, 1836–50.** He left Fauquier alive after Apr 1835 and is in no
   1840 Caton household. Where he died, someone administered him — not Prince William,
   which never probated a Caton before 1922 (C177). Order: Daniel Laurie's
   1840 Cooper Co. household (a male 70–80 = Thomas living with daughter Ann); Cooper and
   Chariton probate 1840–50; Ohio probate (Muskingum, Licking, Franklin, Delaware — the
   Delaware C-files are already negative, C141).
4. **Moses B. Caton's 1839 Muskingum estate** (index lists Moses and a Sidney Caton).
   Heirs named: Aaron on the list puts him in Moses's family; absence with the other
   children present excludes Moses. *FamilySearch Ohio probate, Muskingum, 1839–45.*
   Lower priority since C181: Moses's 1820 house has no slot for Aaron unless the
   enumerator skipped a newborn; this is now a formal exclusion, not a discriminator.
5. **Sue (Caton) Dumm's 1932 family history**, Boulder (request out, C118). Joseph's
   daughter writing family history may name his parents; Joseph is Aaron's brother at B.
6. **Fauquier chancery 1861-012 and any suit touching Thomas Sr.'s land** — a Caton
   partition or debt suit 1836–50 in Fauquier or Prince William would list his heirs.
   LVA index is negative for Caton parties in Prince William before 1909 (C084);
   re-run Fauquier with surname-only and 1835–1860 dates.

## B. Records that move the balance

7. **"Caton Branch" land descent** — Prince William deeds 1800–1840 (books survive
   better than order books). A deed between the brothers ("my brother Moses") or from
   the father fixes relationships; a deed by "Thomas Caton of Fauquier" selling Prince
   William land dates his move. *Full-Text `Caton`, Prince William, 1800–1840, no filters.*
8. **The 1834 Rappahannock trust deed** ("Aaron Caton dec'd, trustee") — the recited
   deed's date brackets the elder Aaron's death; his role for the Whites of Fauquier says
   which brother's circle he moved in.
9. **Cooper Co. 1843 tax list / any 1843–44 Boonville record of Aaron.** Julia was born
   in Missouri in Dec 1843 and Aaron was back in Ohio by June 1844 (C144): a visit, not a
   move — to whom? A tithe under Aaron's name in Cooper 1843 puts him beside William and
   the Laurys (Thomas's people) or George W. (the tailor).
10. **Lot 2, Hoagland's Addition, Sixth Street** (the Laurie lot, partitioned 1865, C149)
    — who bought it? If S. G. Sawtell, Aaron died on Thomas's family's lot. *Full-Text
    `Hoagland` Cooper 1865–1870.*
11. **Aaron's second Boonville obituary, Jan–Feb 1878** — a fuller notice than C108 may
    give county of birth or parents. The Missouri Digital Newspaper Project now lives on
    Newspapers.com, free through shsmo.org/collections/newspapers/mdnp (portal token;
    the sandbox gets 401). Same search covers Thomas Sr.'s death if it was in Cooper Co.
12. **Delaware Co. OH, 1841: the minister — closed (C178).** "Titus Case, M.G." was
    Rev. Titus Case (1797–1860), elder of the *Christian Church*, Orange Township — not
    Methodist. What it gives instead: the Gardners lived in southern Delaware County
    (Orange/Liberty/Berlin) in 1841 — the place to look for Sarah Ann's family (T03).
13. **Aaron's 1850 neighbours** — checked (C-note): tradesmen, none Virginia-born; a
    Jacob Heller, tailor b. Pa., next door. No cluster around him in Ohio.
14. **The cluster's Ohio stop, 1835–37 — was it Delaware County?** Daniel Laurie's son
    Joseph was born in Ohio c.1835 and his daughter in Missouri c.1838 (C098, C126); the
    stop lies between censuses. If it was Delaware County, it explains the one thing
    story A cannot: Aaron marrying a Delaware County girl in 1841 (he met the Gardners
    of Orange/Liberty Twp as a boy of 15–17 and came back for her), and it makes Thomas
    Sr., Ann and Daniel Laurie Aaron's household in the years he vanishes. *Full-Text
    `Laurie` / `Lowry` / `Laury` / `Caton`, Delaware, Ohio, 1834–1838* (deeds, tax
    duplicates, court); then Muskingum for the same years (Moses's family were there).
    A single Laurie or Thomas Caton deed or tax entry in Delaware County 1835–37 would be
    the strongest circumstantial link the project could have short of a named parent.

## C. Creative sources still untried (free; some need your browser)

- **archive.org full-text API** (`be-api.us.archive.org/fts/v1/search`) — reads
  lending-restricted books by snippet. Used tonight for Gott, Baird, DAR Magazine, the
  Ohio 1840 index, Sedalia obituaries, and (evening) the Prince William will index,
  Culpeper marriages, Loudoun wills, the Ohio Company suits (C179). IA holds NO
  Prince William, Fairfax or Fauquier marriage compilation and no tax-list abstracts
  for these counties (advanced-search title sweep, 14 Sep) — those are FamilySearch.
- **Virginia Chronicle** (LVA newspapers) — Alexandria/Warrenton 1800–45: removal sales,
  estate notices, runaways naming Thomas, Moses, Aaron. Cloudflare here; free for you.
- **HathiTrust full-text** — `"Thomas Caton" Fauquier`, `"Moses Caton"`, `"Caton Branch"`.
- **BLM GLO patents** — Moses's/George W.'s Muskingum entry dates the Ohio arrival.
- **Colorado Historic Newspapers** — Sue Dumm's obituary.
- **Muskingum County Chapter OGS** cemetery readings (Goshen Methodist) — Moses's wife's
  stone, if any, gives her name and the couple's dates.
- **Fauquier Historical Society / Afro-American Historical Association of Fauquier** —
  the 1836–40 tax lists are also abstracted in their "Fauquier tithables" series.
- **DAR GRS** — an Irish "Caton" Revolutionary ancestor claimed by any descendant would
  point to the Monmouth story's source. Needs a browser.
- **Prince William Reliquary** (RELIC, Bull Run Regional Library) — indexed abstracts of
  the surviving Prince William books; `Caton` in its cumulative index.

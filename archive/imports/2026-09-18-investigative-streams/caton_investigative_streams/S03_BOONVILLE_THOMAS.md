# S03 — Identify the elderly Boonville tailor and reconstruct his estate and household connections

**Target in every stream:** Aaron Caton, reported born 16 May 1820 in Virginia, married Sarah Ann Gardner in Delaware County, Ohio, in August 1841, later a Missouri resident and tailor. His father and mother remain unidentified. These are investigation instructions, not newly proved relationships. [R1]

## Question

Who was the Thomas Caton reported as a 77-year-old Virginia-born tailor in Boonville in 1850? Can he be connected independently to the Fauquier Catons, to Aaron or Joseph, or to a different family? Where did he die, and did his records name children or a former residence?

This is a strong concrete lead because it concerns an older-generation man, a trade and a precise household—not because his fatherhood is already likely enough to assume. It was documented in earlier September 15-17 work; retrieving the same transcription again is confirmation, not a new breakthrough. [N3; N5]

## Evidence baseline

The detailed published census transcription gives Thomas 77, occupationTaylor, Virginia, in John P. Maddex's household, household 66, line 24, printed p. 148, enumerated 14 August 1850. The working manuscript locator is NARA M432 roll 397; verify the roll, jurisdiction and page system in the viewer. The original has not yet been acquired in this conversation. [A12]

Another transcribed Cooper household,household 1038, contains Thomas 40, Kentucky, with children including Lucinda, James, Newton and Nimrod. The identity of the 1828 Thomas/Elizabeth Lawrey groom relative to either 1850 man remains unresolved. Compiled ages, cemetery associations and matching militia names cannot settle that identity on their own. My earlier conversational statement that the groom had been separated convincingly from the older tailor was stronger than the records support. [N3; R2:C088,C096,C128]

## Sequence

**Verify the 1850 manuscript first.** Save the complete Maddex household, preceding/following continuation and column headings. Read Thomas's name, age, sex, occupation, birthplace and any property or condition marks. Note corrections or potential row shifts. Transcribe every household member, but do not assign relationships not written in the record. Retain the 40-year-old Thomas's full manuscript separately so age, wife and children's names can be compared without importing a compiled pedigree.

**Identify the Maddex household's connection.** Research John P. Maddex and his spouse with the limited question: why might this Thomas live there? Possibilities include kinship, boarding, employment or another arrangement. Obtain their marriage, a death/obituary or probate that names family, and a short migration timeline. Use Maddex/Maddox or other variants only with identity support. A possible married daughter is a lead to test, not a maiden name to invent. If multiple unrelated adults are in the household, record that context rather than assuming everyone is family.

**Bracket Thomas after 1850.** Locate him in the next available local record, tax list, directory, newspaper, cemetery record or 1860 census. Start with Cooper 1850-1865. Absence from 1860 does not date a death: he may have moved, been omitted or appeared under a variant. Extend beyond 1865 only on a locator or documented associate rather than a nationwide fishing expedition.

**Search death-related property records.** Use readable county probate indexes and original administrations, bonds, inventories, sales, final settlements, distributions, guardianship references and deeds by heirs. Index all relevant surnames and named administrators, not just Thomas. The available 1818-1847 compilation does not test a death after 1850, and the garbled digital probate index is not a reliable surname negative. Request a legible index section when the image is inaccessible. [A13,A14; N5]

**Work a bounded earlier-locality trail.** An original 1850-1860 record naming former Virginia residence or relatives should drive backward research. Search a specific deed, letter, church transfer or court paper involving that locality and person. Do not force an 1840 unnamed older male in a Laurie or Maddex household into being Thomas. If an estate names William, Ann Laurie or another proposed relative, send the actual wording to S01/S08 for independent matching.

## What to extract from an estate

Capture the identity of the deceased; filing and death dates separately; widow; children and their married names; grandchildren representing a deceased child; residential addresses; agents and powers of attorney; and exactly which heirs received which shares. Distinguish an heir from a purchaser, creditor, witness, guardian or administrator. An estate sale list full of Catons may describe customers rather than sons.

Check whether the document purports to identify all relevant heirs and at what date. A will naming only some beneficiaries is not automatically an exhaustive list of children. A son's absence matters only after understanding the instrument and whether he died, was provided for separately, or appears through descendants.

## Tests that could disprove the proposed merger

At approximately 77 in 1850, Thomas's estimated birth is 1772-1773. The reported 1810 Fauquier male 45+ and 1830 male 60-70 are older than that estimate allows. Do not silently choose whichever age supports the merger. A demonstrated separate life history, incompatible spouse/children or a contemporaneous record assigning him different parents/children can break the Fauquier identification. A simple surname, occupation and migration resemblance cannot establish it.

If the 1850 manuscript actually shows a different age, name or trade, correct the baseline first. If it confirms the entry but no connection emerges after the household, estate and departure windows are genuinely tested, park the identity link rather than manufacture one from occupational coincidence.

## Scope and deliverable

S03 owns both Missouri Thomas census controls and this older man's household/estate trail. It does not own all Missouri Catons or George W.'s ancestry. S01 supplies the Virginia identity matrix; S08 receives named daughters or wives; S05 owns Aaron's federal records.

Return a short documented biography, an estate/heir table if found, and a separate equivalence assessment for each Virginia Thomas. A negative identification is useful: an unrelated elderly tailor would remove a distraction without proving another candidate.

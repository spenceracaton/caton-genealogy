## Definition of progress

Progress is a newly acquired record, a demonstrated identity link, a contradicted hypothesis, a precise validated source locator, or a genuinely bounded negative. Re-reading the same transcription is not a new discovery. A third account copying the same genealogy is not independent corroboration. A full-text or index failure is not proof of record absence.

Use the repository's A/B/C framework but grade source observations separately from reasoning. A photographed typescript can be directly observed while its copied relationship is still derivative. A source gradedA can contain mistaken information; a persuasive argument can rest on incomplete coverage. Every inference must say what would materially weaken it.

For a future parentage conclusion, assemble an identity-correct chain of evidence for Aaron and each proposed parent. An explicit parent statement still needs source/informant assessment. A well-supported indirect proof may also be possible, but it must explain the relevant conflicts and alternatives rather than merely count coincidences. The goal is not to choose the most attractive story.

# S01 — Resolve the Fauquier Thomas identities before extending the pedigree

**Target in every stream:** Aaron Caton, reported born 16 May 1820 in Virginia, married Sarah Ann Gardner in Delaware County, Ohio, in August 1841, later a Missouri resident and tailor. His father and mother remain unidentified. These are investigation instructions, not newly proved relationships. [R1]

## Question and purpose

Which records concern the same Thomas Caton, and which concern different men? In particular, can William's consenting father be identified with an older Fauquier household head, with the Elizabeth Lawrey groom, or with neither? This is an identity investigation, not an attempt to fill census slots with desired children.

The copied December 1828 consent calls William the son of Thomas Caton Jr., while the reported 1830 census places both William and Thomas Jun in the 20-30 bracket. That conflict invalidates a simple same-name merger; it does not prove a specific alternative pedigree. The copy names another Thomas among the witnesses, but two appearances of a name are not by themselves a complete identification of two individuals. [N1; R2:C082,C092,C193]

## Starting evidence and uncertainties

The 1810 and 1820 counts have external transcription checks, but the current work has not independently recovered both manuscripts. The 1810 entry has one male 45+, one female 16-26 and one female 45+; the 1820 entry has two boys under 10 and no male 26-45. The women are unnamed and their relationships unknown. The 1830 transcription available online skips the target page. [N2]

Use observation labels, not a premature family tree: `TH-FQ1810`, `TH-FQ1820`, `TH-FQ1830-SEN`, `TH-FQ1830-JUN`, `TH-CONSENT1828`, `TH-GROOM1828`, and `TH-CONSENT1835`. These labels do not assert seven distinct men. S03 separately owns `TH-BOON1850-77` and `TH-COOPER1850-KY40`.

## Exact acquisition sequence

**First, obtain Fauquier 1830 p. 471.** Capture the full names/age page, printed column headings, all three Caton rows, neighboring rows, and the facing continuation if present. Establish the actual roll and frame from the viewer. Do not assume a previously guessed roll number is verified merely because the page number was repeated. The important reads are the adult-male brackets, all young-male columns, and the older household's female counts.

**Second, retain the two earlier manuscripts.** The derivative locators are 1810 p. 263B, transcription line 11, handwritten p. 398, M252-68; and 1820 p. 46, last household after William Cockrill, M33-136. Read against the actual headings and neighboring rows. Preserve the anomalous 1820 16-18/16-26 combination; do not silently repair it. [A9-A11]

**Third, acquire the entire connected marriage packet once.** William/Ann F. Lawrey: DGS 007578973 image 149, typed p. 279; Thomas/Elizabeth Lawrey: same group, reported image 145; Ann Caton/Daniel Laury: volume 5 image 45 at A8. Capture the index, body, endorsements, witnesses, dates, and any pointers to originals. Distinguish execution, bond, permission, license, return and ceremony dates. Ask the custodian whether loose originals survive before promising signature analysis. S08 should reuse this acquisition rather than fetch it again. [A7,A8; R2:C092]

**Fourth, read the already-located tax hits and their annual context.** C156 records 17 Full-Text results, including Thomas entries in the 1809-1819 and 1820-1832 volumes. Open these before starting another surname search. Capture year/title/header pages, assessment dates, districts, and all Caton entries in each sampled list. Then fill the annual gaps needed to establish coexistence and disappearance, initially 1809-1835, extending 1836-1840 only where informative. A first surviving appearance does not necessarily mean a young man just reached taxable age; two tithables do not identify two sons. [R2:C156,C196]

## Analysis protocol

Create a row per record containing exact name/suffix, date, locality, age information, spouse named, children explicitly named, occupation, associates, original/copy status, locator and competing identities. Separately record each proposed equivalence and the evidence for and against it. Keep a conflicting age visible even when other features match.

Read census ages without first displaying the preferred pedigree. A second reader should repeat only the disputed cells, ideally without being told which answer would help the hypothesis. This is quality control, not a second independent historical source. Treat blanks, illegible cells and zeroes distinctly. On the stated birth dates, Aaron was ten and Joseph six on 1 June 1830; use the correct 10-14 and 5-9 categories without calling Joseph seven.

## Predetermined tests

If the younger 1830 Thomas is confirmed 20-30, reject the ordinary identification of that particular man as the father of the adult William shown 20-30. Do not reject every older Thomas, and do not automatically create a third father. If the younger Thomas is 40-50, the age conflict is reduced, but identity is still not proved. If the older head appears in successive tax lists under different suffixes, that may support changing local styles, not establish fatherhood by itself.

If the 1820 count changes, revise only conclusions dependent on that count. The presence of another adult would not make the two boys grandsons automatically; its absence would not make them sons. If the evidence distinguishes the 1810 and 1820 heads, the marriage/mother reconstruction must be rebuilt rather than patched with an unexplained age adjustment.

The smallest useful result is a retained 1830 image with verified counts and an identity matrix showing what remains unresolved. After one bounded access session, switch from repeated failed downloads to a precise image/copy request. Do not hold all other streams hostage while waiting.

This stream owns Virginia Thomas census, tax and marriage acquisition. S03 owns Missouri Thomas manuscripts and later estate records; S08 owns broader wife and Lawrey kinship analysis. Return a proposed identity map, not a new genealogy. Success means fewer unsupported equivalences, whether or not Thomas becomes a stronger father candidate.

# S02 — Find an apprenticeship, guardianship or other childhood record naming Aaron

**Target in every stream:** Aaron Caton, reported born 16 May 1820 in Virginia, married Sarah Ann Gardner in Delaware County, Ohio, in August 1841, later a Missouri resident and tailor. His father and mother remain unidentified. These are investigation instructions, not newly proved relationships. [R1]

## Question and reason for priority

Was target Aaron bound to a trade, placed under a guardian, maintained by local authorities, or named in a family court proceeding before his 1841 marriage? A record joining his name to a parent, master, place and age could resolve more than another generation of surname-and-census comparisons.

Aaron and the probable relative Joseph were tailors; their absence from the presently reconstructed 1830 Caton households motivates this search. It does not prove that they were brothers, poor, orphaned, or formally apprenticed. The search remains worthwhile even if a Thomas identity changes. Do not condition all childhood research on first proving a particular Thomas destitute. [R1; R2:C195]

## Source map and corrections

The principal bounded source is Fauquier's Overseers of the Poor Minutes,1804-1845, LVA Reel 116. The official inventory marks it available for interlibrary loan. The Gott Library's Poorhouse Project is a separate access/contact lead, not proof that the register has a complete public transcript. [A1-A3]

Gott's *Fauquier County, Virginia, Guardian Bonds,1759-1871* is another index target. Do not use catalog 642321, film 1689208/DGS 8682856: the previous citation points to Chatham County, North Carolina. [N1; A6]

Fauquier Fiduciary Records,1833-1940, include loose guardians' and other fiduciaries' papers in fourteen boxes. Their overall date range does not guarantee a relevant 1830s file. Ask for an internal list before requesting an undirected search of every box. [A4]

## Acquisition and review sequence

**Access first.** Check the shared request register for any outstanding inquiry. Ask the Gott Library whether its overseers/indenture work has a transcript or index, and whether Gott's guardian-bond volume has Caton variants. Request precise page/box citations and costs before paid work. If no usable index exists, arrange authorized access to Reel 116 through the holding institution or an eligible local library. A catalog hit is not a successful register search.

**Read the public-placement material in a bounded window.** Start with 1823-1835, then extend 1836-1841; expand earlier only when an entry or family death warrants it. Search Caton, Caten, Catton, Cayton, Katon and justified handwriting variants, and terms such as bound, apprentice, orphan, guardian, indenture, maintenance and tailor/taylor. Where names are not indexed, read the relevant sequence rather than relying on full-text search alone.

**Follow entries through time.** Capture the initial order, executed indenture, approval, master, trade, term, stated age/birthdate, named parent, security, cancellation, reassignment and completion. A later release can identify the same child after a sparse first order. Extract nearby related entries but do not declare neighboring names relatives.

**Then test private or court-ordered arrangements.** Relevant county court volumes include 1822-1828 on Reel 57,1829-1835 on Reel 58 and 1836-1840 on Reel 59. Search deed indexes and deeds for a privately recorded indenture; do not assume the child is the indexed party. Once a master is named, rerun the bounded indexes under that person's name and follow the master's relocation only as far as it can connect the boy. [A1]

**Use guardianship/loose files as their own record class.** Extract the ward, person whose property is managed, guardian, bond date, securities, settlement and any relationship wording. A guardian's name is not a father's name. A file may concern inherited property rather than the child's day-to-day residence. A court or document term should be interpreted in its actual historical context, not turned into an unsupported claim that both parents were dead.

## Decision rules and safeguards

An entry naming Aaron, a father, and a birthdate compatible with 16 May 1820 is a major positive, but distinguish any same-name child through later trade/residence/kin evidence. A record with Aaron alone is a locator, not a parentage finding. A master named Thomas is not a father unless the wording or separate evidence establishes that relationship.

A named father other than Thomas must be preserved immediately and tested, not absorbed into a supposed Thomas guardianship. An explicit illegitimacy or step-relationship also matters; do not translate legal care automatically into biological parentage. Read the record's exact language.

No public binding after a complete review would close that source/window, not prove no apprenticeship ever occurred. A failed search at Fauquier would not eliminate another county, private instruction, unrecorded employment or a missing record. Low assessed property may motivate a hypothesis but does not itself establish destitution.

## Geographic expansion and division of work

Do not call Prince William, Fairfax, or Washington a dead end solely because one overseers series is missing. Inventory alternate court/deed sources and expand only when family locality or a named master supplies a reason. Do not assume an Alexandria record of the D.C. period belongs to Washington City; establish the correct court and place before retrieving.

S01 supplies Thomas observations, but this stream can search the children's names immediately. S07 owns Ohio local childhood/employment records, and S04 owns the older Aaron's specific estate/trust chain. Share a discovered master or guardian rather than assigning two agents to the same volume.

## First-pass deliverable

Return either the complete linked childhood record chain, a page-by-page coverage log for a defined source/window, or an exact outstanding acquisition request. Include sources searched under child, parent, guardian and master. The objective is a named relationship or a bounded elimination of a record source—not confirmation of the apprenticeship story itself.

# S03 — Identify the elderly Boonville tailor and reconstruct his estate and household connections

**Target in every stream:** Aaron Caton, reported born 16 May 1820 in Virginia, married Sarah Ann Gardner in Delaware County, Ohio, in August 1841, later a Missouri resident and tailor. His father and mother remain unidentified. These are investigation instructions, not newly proved relationships. [R1]

## Question

Who was the Thomas Caton reported as a 77-year-old Virginia-born tailor in Boonville in 1850? Can he be connected independently to the Fauquier Catons, to Aaron or Joseph, or to a different family? Where did he die, and did his records name children or a former residence?

This is a strong concrete lead because it concerns an older-generation man, a trade and a precise household—not because his fatherhood is already likely enough to assume. It was documented in earlier September 15-17 work; retrieving the same transcription again is confirmation, not a new breakthrough. [N3; N5]

## Evidence baseline

The detailed published census transcription gives Thomas 77, occupationTaylor, Virginia, in John P. Maddex's household, household 66, line 24, printed p. 148, enumerated 14 August 1850. The working manuscript locator is NARA M432 roll 397; verify the roll, jurisdiction and page system in the viewer. The original has not yet been acquired in this conversation. [A12]

Another transcribed Cooper household,household 1038, contains Thomas 40, Kentucky, with children including Lucinda, James, Newton and Nimrod. The identity of the 1828 Thomas/Elizabeth Lawrey groom relative to either 1850 man remains unresolved. Compiled ages, cemetery associations and matching militia names cannot settle that identity on their own. My earlier conversational statement that the groom had been separated convincingly from the older tailor was stronger than the records support. [N3; R2:C088,C096,C128]

## Sequence

**Verify the 1850 manuscript first.** Save the complete Maddex household, preceding/following continuation and column headings. Read Thomas's name, age, sex, occupation, birthplace and any property or condition marks. Note corrections or potential row shifts. Transcribe every household member, but do not assign relationships not written in the record. Retain the 40-year-old Thomas's full manuscript separately so age, wife and children's names can be compared without importing a compiled pedigree.

**Identify the Maddex household's connection.** Research John P. Maddex and his spouse with the limited question: why might this Thomas live there? ## Question and independence

Where was Aaron immediately before his August 1841 Delaware County marriage, who brought him into that community, and did the Fauquier Caton/Laurie network pass through the same place? This is the missing geographic bridge. It is not necessary to assume the families migrated together in order to search for whether they did.

The repo documents the 1841 marriage and later Ohio records. It reports an 1844 quitclaim involving Aaron, Sarah and Jonathan Gardner; an 1846 chattel mortgage naming James A. The Laurie family's Ohio-born child around 1835 is a route clue, not a proven Ohio county or evidence that Thomas accompanied them. [R2:C144-C146,C186,C189]

Start with the full 1841 marriage packet, not just the minister's return. Check for application, license, bond, consent, witness, affidavit and docket cross-reference. Read both sides/continuations of the 1844 quitclaim. The ledger identifies DGS 007842562 image 597 and notes that the acknowledgment/recording continuation was not yet seen. Capture execution, acknowledgment and recording dates separately, and locate the acknowledgment. An explicit shared residence or joint transaction would strengthen the route hypothesis; it would not alone prove parentage.

A same-year appearance of a common Lowry surname in Delaware County is weak without age, spouse and other identity features. An alternative employer or family network could explain Aaron's Ohio arrival even if the Fauquier family remains relevant elsewhere. Preserve that independent explanation rather than treating every record as confirmation of a group migration.

If a complete bounded local-record review shows no relevant Laurie stop, report the tested source/window. Do not claim the stop was elsewhere solely from that absence. # S08 — Identify the women and prove the Caton–Lawrey relationships without presupposing a remarriage

**Target in every stream:** Aaron Caton, reported born 16 May 1820 in Virginia, married Sarah Ann Gardner in Delaware County, Ohio, in August 1841, later a Missouri resident and tailor. His father and mother remain unidentified. These are investigation instructions, not newly proved relationships. [R1]

## Core question

Who could have been Aaron's mother, and what do records of women and in-laws reveal about the proposed family? This stream should not be postponed until a father is completely proved: a woman's estate, remarriage or inherited-land transaction may identify the children more clearly than the father's surviving records. But finding a wife of a candidate father does not automatically identify the mother of every child.

The immediate structure to test is the trio of documented/reported Fauquier marriages: Thomas/Elizabeth Lawrey, William/Ann F. Lawrey, and Ann Caton/Daniel Laury. The marriages themselves are distinct propositions from the claim that three Caton siblings married three Laurie siblings. No such reciprocal sibling structure should be assumed just because the pattern is attractive. [R2:C088,C092,C098]

## Correct starting assumptions

The 1810 census's older woman is not identified as the wife, nor its younger woman as the daughter. The younger woman could have been a wife already, or could have had another relationship; the 1810 and 1820 Thomas entries could also concern different men. Therefore the old search restricted to an 1811-1819 remarriage to a widow is too narrow. [N2]

The identity of the 1828 Elizabeth Lawrey groom remains unresolved. Do not use an online birth/death pair or military service by similarly named children to attach that groom to the older Boonville tailor or the Kentucky household. S01/S03 own those identity checks. This stream asks which women and children each actual document identifies.

## 1. Build the named-women register from the marriage packets

Reuse the complete packets acquired by S01. Separate Ann Caton from Ann F. Lawrey and Elizabeth Lawrey. Record every spelling, surname, signature or copied name, named father, stated age/status, bondsman, witness and date. Do not normalize Ann into Nancy, add a middle name, or change Lawrey into a particular modern spelling without evidence.

For each parent consent, identify whose consent it was and to whom it applied. A statement that someone was 21 may attest legal age rather than an exact birthday; a voluntary consent may not conclusively establish minority. Preserve the wording instead of computing an unjustifiably exact birth boundary. In the 1835 packet, distinguish bond, permission, recorded and ceremony dates, rather than resolving conflicting dates by assumption. [A7,A8]

The William packet associates Thomas Guttridge, James Payne of Thomas, and Presley Johnson with the transaction. Those are leads to the named bride/groom's social network, not automatic blood relatives. James R. Payne in 1850 Missouri should not be equated with the 1829 bondsman without an independent identity bridge.

## 2. Prove Laurie parents and sibling links

Treat Catharine/Catherine Laurie, Joseph Laurie, Daniel, Elizabeth, Ann F., Newton and Joseph N. as separate subjects until a document connects them. Do not merge two Catharines from inconsistent ages in later histories. Search the parents' wills/administrations, marriage consents, land partitions, heirs' deeds and powers of attorney. Extract whole heir lists including married daughters and representatives of deceased children.

Work the records in which women have an economic interest: a release of an inherited share, a distribution receipt, a settlement, or an agreement joining a husband and wife. Ask what legal interest the instrument actually conveys; a dower release names a wife but does not normally enumerate children. An in-law estate may provide the explicit sibling connection missing from census proximity.

## 3. Acquire the specific family-paper target

The SHSMO finding aid for Cochran-Jeffress Family Papers,C4669, identifies folder 2 as the Jeffress Family Bible,1831-1942; Isabel Laurie is indexed in folders 1,2,4. Request the relevant Bible pages and genealogical references with title/publication pages and contextual notes if available. This is a precise existing holding, but the finding aid does not establish Isabel's parents or her relationship to the Caton spouses. [A20]

Read the Bible entries for when and by whom they were written. An 1831 date inside a later family Bible can be retrospective. Keep family-history books, Bible entries and modern pedigree notes distinct, even when they are filed together. Check for copied language and citations before treating a repeated relationship as independently corroborated. If the collection concerns a different Laurie family, record that and stop the branch.

S07 uses any Ohio county/date revealed here; it should not submit a duplicate archive request. S06 owns the Dumm papers, not this collection.

## 4. Broaden the candidate-wife search responsibly

Begin with named spouses emerging from records, then search the relevant Virginia marriage indexes and originals. A practical initial window around 1795-1823 can test a wife present by 1810 and later possibilities, but it is a starting window, not a biological or legal exclusion. Expand earlier if the demonstrated household history warrants it. Prior search negatives must retain their exact county, volume, date range, variants and original-versus-abstract limits.

Prioritize Fauquier/Prince William and the counties supplied by actual parties, sureties or residences. Fairfax or Stafford should enter on a documented reason, not simply because all adjacent counties were named in an old checklist. Search widows under a former married name when evidence identifies one; do not require widowhood from the census alone.

Once a plausible woman is identified, trace her parents/siblings and estate only far enough to determine whether Aaron appears as a child or grandchild. A named maternal grandparent's distribution to Aaron could identify his mother without first solving every Thomas.

## Tests that decide the question

An explicit statement tying target Aaron to a mother, accompanied by age/residence or family evidence resolving identity, is the strongest result. A record naming a candidate father's wife after 1820 does not prove she gave birth to Aaron. A documented earlier wife dying before his birth, remarriage after his birth or an explicit step-relationship can separate those propositions.

A wife/daughter identification in 1810 should be rejected if an independent record assigns the women differently. If William and Ann prove children of different Thomases, discard the assumed sibling model rather than fitting both into one census household. If the Lawrey spouses are not siblings, that weakens the proposed intact-family migration interpretation but does not undo their individually documented marriages.

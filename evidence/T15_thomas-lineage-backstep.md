# T15 — Thomas Caton ancestry: first backward pass

Research date: 16 September 2026

STATUS: partial

## Question

Can the elder Thomas Caton of Fauquier, the proposed father of Aaron Caton,
be linked to an earlier Caton generation?

## Working anchor and limits

The Aaron-to-Thomas link remains a strong structural hypothesis, grade C, not a
proved parent-child relationship (C099). The elder Fauquier Thomas is dated to
about 1760–65 from the 1810–1830 census sequence (C159, C080, C082). Prince
William records list a Thomas Caton in the 1790s cluster with Aaron and Moses,
but the identity link to the Fauquier census head is not explicit (C154, C156).

An existing image review reports a handwritten `Cayton | Thomas` in FamilySearch
DGS 7893689, image 288 of the 1782–1805 land-tax group. Its year is unresolved,
and identity with either the 1790s or Fauquier Thomas is unproved. A direct
retrieval attempt on 16 September returned HTTP 403 before the image opened; no
new image reading was made. See
[`2026-09-15_T15_prince-william-land-tax-cayton-1782-1805.md`](2026-09-15_T15_prince-william-land-tax-cayton-1782-1805.md).

## Earlier Thomas Caton candidate in Frederick County

Several records establish an earlier Thomas Caton in Frederick County, Virginia,
as a real historical person, but none names him as father or ancestor of the
Fauquier/Prince William Catons:

- The Handley Regional Library finding aid lists a 28 May 1747 item involving
  Thomas Caton and James Dick, described as an appointment of Aaron Rawlings
  (James Wood Collection, item 447). The finding aid describes the collection
  as including Frederick County material; the item itself has not been viewed.
- The Archives of Maryland transcription of the 10 May 1757 proceedings names
  “Thomas Caton Gentleman a Justice of the Peace” in Frederick County, Virginia.
- The Frederick County sheriff-history list records Thomas Caton beginning an
  appointment on 2 September 1761; it gives no end date.
- The Library of Virginia's ARVAS catalog search describes two relevant Land
  Office files: a 10 February 1752 letter to G.W. Fairfax about a Frederick
  County land dispute, and papers concerning 300 acres disputed by Samuel Harris
  and Thomas Caton, dated 1759–1763. The catalog entries are metadata only; the
  papers have not been retrieved.

These records make the Frederick County Thomas a possible older-generation
candidate by chronology and region only. No reviewed source identifies his
children, parents, or a move to Prince William/Fauquier. Do not join him to the
Fauquier Thomas or to Aaron's line yet.

## Separate Maryland same-name lead

The Maryland State Archives' will abstract for Alexander Black (proved 8 March
1757; will dated 7 December 1740) names “Thoms Caton, my apprentice” as a
beneficiary and executor. It does not name Thomas's parents. No record reviewed
here connects this Anne Arundel County Thomas to either the Frederick County
Thomas or the later Virginia Catons. Keep it as a separate identity lead.

## Current result

The backward trail currently stops at an unnamed prior generation for the
Broad Run Caton group. The Frederick County Thomas is the strongest earlier
same-name documentary lead found in this pass, not a proved ancestor. The
Maryland apprentice and the 1782–1805 `Cayton` entry remain separate until a
record links them.

## Best next record tests

1. Retrieve the Library of Virginia Land Office papers in Box 1, folders 5–6
   (1752 letter and 1759–1763 dispute) and inspect whether they identify Thomas's
   residence, associates, or family. The catalog alone is not relationship

2. Reopen FamilySearch image 288 with the image viewer or an accessible copy to

# Thomas Caton after 1835 — Cooper County research

Research date: 17 September 2026. Research only; canonical files unchanged.

## Result

No record examined identifies Fauquier Thomas Caton Sr. as a Cooper County resident or decedent, or names Aaron or Joseph as his children. A newly located primary record gives a specific lead: **“Thos Caton Snr” and “Thos Caton Junr” appear together against a note dated 7 March 1846 in Isaac Martin's estate inventory.** The identities and relationship remain unproved.

Read BRIEF.md, PARENTAGE-TESTS.md, and relevant claims before searching. C092 distinguishes Ann's father Thomas Sr. from William's father Thomas Jr.; the documents do not state a relationship between the two men. C138 is a reported, qualified Fauquier volume-index search, not proof that Thomas migrated alive. C149 excludes Aaron F. from the direct-line Aaron research.

## 1. Image-verified debt entry: two Thomases, 1846

**Grade A for the recorded entry; C for identification with either Fauquier man.**

- Repository: FamilySearch, Missouri, Probate Records, 1750–1998; Cooper, Probate Records 1847–1854, image-group metadata volumes A–B.
- DGS **007636420**, **image 237 of 656**, manuscript **pp. 388–389**, estate of **Isaac Martin**.
- Permanent image: <https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-L9LD-Z936-2>.
- Local original: `1850_Cooper_IsaacMartin_inventory_p388-389_ThomasCaton.jpg` (4785 × 3624).
- Local crop: `1850_Cooper_IsaacMartin_inventory_p388_ThomasCaton_crop.jpg`. Original-image crop: left 500, top 1990, width 2000, height 420 pixels. One crop inspection; no contrast changes.
- Location on page: p.388, below the Felix G. Wright / Geo. W. Shirley / John Harned entry and above John Higginbotham.
- Name/date reading from the image: **“Thos Caton Snr Thos Caton Junr”**, followed by a note dated **“March 7 1846”**. The following line specifies twelve months after date. The machine transcript misreads the first suffix as “Son”; the image reads an age-distinguishing suffix, not the word “son.”
- The continuation on p.389 places the inventory in Isaac Martin's estate and dates its certification **8 October 1850**, with Anthony S. Walker named in the inventory-making passage.

**What this establishes:** a Cooper County estate inventory records a debt instrument associating two men styled Thomas Caton senior and junior, dated 1846.

**What this does not establish:** father and son; the identity of either man; their ages, birthplaces, wives, or children; where the note was executed; their physical residence in Cooper County in 1846; or that either was still alive in 1850. A debt can remain listed after an obligor's death. This is Isaac Martin's estate, not a Thomas Caton estate. Neither Aaron, Joseph, Ann nor William is named in this entry. The original note was not retrieved.

## 2. Image-verified deed: Thomas and wife Elizabeth, acknowledged 1850

**Grade A for the deed wording; C for identifying its Thomas with a Fauquier man.**

- FamilySearch, Cooper, Deeds 1850–1853, **image 443 of 653**, **Deed Book Q, p.260** (the handwritten book letter resembles a 2; identify additionally by image and page).
- Permanent image: <https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLK-6SM7-H>.
- Local original: `1850_Cooper_DeedQ_p260_ElizabethCaton.jpg` (4615 × 4147).
- Local crop: `1850_Cooper_DeedQ_p260_ElizabethCaton_crop.jpg`; original-image left 215, top 1770, width 2050, height 1950 pixels.
- James **Deckard** and Christina his wife convey forty acres, **NE¼ NE¼, section 35, township 46, range 19**, for $100 paid by Thomas for the benefit of Elizabeth his wife and the heirs of her body. The body spells Thomas's surname **Cayton**; the marginal recipient/grantee entries use **Caton**.
- Acknowledgment: **10 October 1850**, before **J. A. Johnston, J.P.** Recordation: **7 February 1853**. Margin: delivered **4 March 1853**, with **Thos Caton** named.

This establishes a named wife and a parcel for one Thomas. It supplies no age, Virginia origin, parent, named child, or Sr./Jr. suffix. Do not assign Elizabeth to Fauquier Thomas Sr., or assume the deed's Thomas is either obligor in the 1846 note. “Heirs of her body” is a conveyancing clause; it does not name children.

## Interpretation boundary

The 1846 debt entry is a new documentary association worth retaining. It does **not** extend the established life of Fauquier Thomas Sr. beyond 1835 until the men are identified. No explicit connection to Aaron or Joseph was found. No Thomas estate, administrator, death notice, or burial was verified. Do not call Cooper probate, deeds, newspapers, cemeteries, or Thomas's post-1835 history “negative” or “closed” on the strength of this bounded pass.

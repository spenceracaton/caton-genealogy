# T21 — Moses Caton, Sr., Muskingum probate case 1718

## Research question

Does Moses B. Caton's 1839 Muskingum estate identify heirs or Virginia kin who
could distinguish whether Aaron Caton was Moses's son?

## Source and retrieval

- FamilySearch catalog: [Ohio, Muskingum County, probate case files, 1804–1934](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/olib:2804142).
- The authenticated Full-Text search of the Muskingum administrative-docket index
  (DGS 104066994) returned one Caton result. Its image 12 of 116, printed page 18,
  lists `CATON, MOSES, SR. #1718` with page reference 54.
- Case 1718 is in DGS 104013531, `Probate Estate Case Files 1804–1929`,
  cases 01712–01721. The four adjacent case images 115–118 are the packet for
  Moses Caton:
  - image 115: cover identifying `Moses Caton Decd`, `Presley W. Sears Adm`, and
    case no. 1718;
  - image 116: inventory bill for property belonging to Moses Caton, deceased,
    of Newton Township, Muskingum County, Ohio, signed/dated 8 October 1839;
  - image 117: additional case-1718 cover material identifying Moses Caton;
  - image 118: account of property belonging to Moses Caton, deceased, of Newton
    Township, with sale dated 8 November 1839. The account includes George W.
    Caton among the estate's debtor/note entries.

## Finding

The original images establish an identifiable Muskingum probate case for a Moses
Caton, Sr., deceased in Newton Township in 1839, administered by Presley W. Sears.
The sale/account image records a financial transaction involving George W. Caton.
This is consistent with the already documented Moses B. Caton / George W. Caton
Muskingum branch, but the packet does not state a middle initial, heirs, parents,
or a relationship between Moses and George. It contains no Aaron Caton reference.

## Interpretation and limits

- Proof: Moses Caton, Sr. had Muskingum case 1718; the packet contains his 1839
  inventory and sale account; George W. Caton appears in the account.
- Inference only: this is the Moses B. Caton identified by the collateral census,
  marriage, and cemetery evidence in C162/C164/C181/C187.
- No parentage weight: the packet does not name Aaron as heir, son, nephew, or
  otherwise related. It therefore does not move Aaron's parentage from the
  existing Fauquier-leading inference.

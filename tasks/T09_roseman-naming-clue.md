# T09 — "Roseman" as a surname carried in a middle name

**Priority: low, but cheap, and nobody has looked.**

Aaron's daughter is recorded in compiled material as **Nancy Roseman Caton**, and
the 1850 census now corroborates a Nancy born c. Nov 1849 (claim C017). The
compiled first and middle name is therefore more credible than it was.

**Roseman is unexplained.** In this period a surname in a child's middle name
usually carries a mother's maiden name, a grandmother's maiden name, or the name
of a close family associate. Sarah's maiden name is Gardner, so Roseman is not
hers. It is one of the few naming clues in the family that points somewhere nobody
has searched.

**Task:**
1. Find Roseman families in **Delaware County, Ohio** and in **Ohio County,
   Virginia / Belmont County, Ohio** in the 1820–1850 censuses.
2. Check for any Roseman–Caton or Roseman–Gardner marriage, deed, will, witness
   or bondsman connection.
3. Check whether a Roseman appears near Aaron in the 1850 Delaware County
   enumeration — the household is 1160/1163, Town of Delaware, p.179a.

**If Roseman traces to the Wheeling area rather than to Delaware County**, it
becomes a live candidate for a name carried from Aaron's own side, which is
exactly the generation the whole project is missing.

Treat a negative as informative and record it. Do not promote any Roseman
connection above C-level without a document.

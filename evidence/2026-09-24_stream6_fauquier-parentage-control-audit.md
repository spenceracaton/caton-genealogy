**Research date:** 24 September 2026\

## Question and evidence boundary

Target: Aaron Caton, reported born 16 May 1820 in Virginia; a tailor; married Sarah Ann Gardner in Delaware County, Ohio, in August 1841. His parents remain unknown. The 16 May date and Wheeling tradition are compilation-derived leads, not original-record facts. Aaron's Company E service card gives age 44 at enlistment in August 1861 (approximately 1816–17), an unresolved age conflict rather than a reason to select a preferred census slot.

The test is whether a source connects Aaron to a Fauquier person or household. Source quality, the information supplied, identity, and relationship are assessed separately below. A household age tally, surname, trade, shared state of birth, or county-level co-location does not name a relationship.

### Source dependencies and overstatements

1. **C099 is a synthesis, not an independent source.** Its census, occupation, Ohio, Missouri, and Cooper observations supply association; its statement that co-location in the same Ohio county would “close the case” exceeds what co-location could prove. It cannot supply the missing identity link.
2. **C110 cannot currently carry an uncle/sibling inference.** Its underlying 1898 newspaper item is not retained at source level. The article's alleged “niece” language, even after recovery, must be separated from the identification of Mrs. Sawtell and from the route of kinship.
3. **The 1820 census and the reported birth date are not two independent identifications.** The schedule provides anonymous age slots. The exact date comes through an unrecovered compilation trail; it neither identifies Aaron in the house nor resolves which boy he would be.
4. **The Boonville chronology consists of different source layers.** The 1850 age and tailor entry are currently a derivative transcription (C205); the 1846/1850 Cooper images are primary but do not identify the people across records (C206). They must not be fused to resolve the age conflict.
5. **The William consent has a separate identity question.** C092 preserves a typescript reading that calls William's father Thomas Jr.; the 1830 image has a 20–30-year-old Thomas Jun, William's contemporary (D-012/C208). The original loose consent is still required. This affects reconstruction of the Fauquier family, not a proved Aaron link.
6. **The WikiTree/GEDCOM parent assignment is not an alternative parent candidate.** C070 traces it to one unsourced import with demonstrated errors. Ohio County Thomas, Tyler/Wetzel Thomas, and George W. Caton are record-based leads but no record names any as Aaron's father. A surname cluster is not an identified candidate parent.

## 2. Direct contrary evidence

No record directly contradicts the possibility that Aaron was a son of Thomas Sr. The strongest contrary constraints are indirect:

- Aaron is not named in the 1820 or 1830 Fauquier schedules. The 1830 Thomas Sr. household has no male under 15, leaving no continuous household trace for a boy born about 1817–20.
- Joseph's reported 1823 birth makes him unavailable as either 1820 under-10 boy. This removes an implicit but invalid way of accounting for the two slots.
- The service-card age (approximately 1816–17) conflicts with the compilation-derived 16 May 1820 date, but either age still falls under 10 in August 1820. It does not discriminate among the two boys or against the Fauquier household.
- The presently transcribed Boonville tailor would be born about 1772–73, outside the proposed Fauquier Thomas's 1810 and 1830 brackets. This is a real chronology conflict only if the manuscript reading and the identity merge are both established; neither is currently established.

`G-PENDING-1`: No available record connects Aaron Caton to the Fauquier Thomas household; the 1820 age slot is permissive, not identity evidence.\
`G-PENDING-2`: The 1898 Joseph “niece” account cannot currently establish a Joseph–Aaron relationship or its route because the original article and Mrs. Sawtell's identification are unverified.\
`G-PENDING-3`: The 1850 Boonville tailor must remain separate from the Fauquier Thomas candidate and the 1846 Cooper obligors until the manuscript and a relationship-bearing record are examined.

## 3. One alternative-origin control

### Start point: Aaron's Delaware County marriage

The retained original record-book image, Delaware County marriage record p.291, was re-read and cropped at native source resolution (`evidence/2026-09-24_stream6_1841-marriage-p291-crop.png`). It says that Aaron Caton and Sarah Gardner were licensed 7 August 1841; Titus Case, M.G., solemnized their marriage on 8 August; the return was recorded 15 October. It supplies no ages, parents, residence, witnesses, bondsman, consent, prior residence, or named associate other than the officiant. Titus Case's later identification as a Christian Church elder supports a possible Gardner-side local context but does not place Aaron in Orange Township or connect him to any Caton family.

Existing Delaware coverage was not repeated. C141 reports a C-surname page-through of 1808–1851 probate/guardianship loose files; C142 reports a C-surname plaintiff-index check, 1834–46. Both are legacy-unstructured negatives, and neither supports a countywide absence. The unexamined appearance dockets and chronological “Bonds 1807–1851” remain unchosen: current records do not establish their exact document types or a reasonable expectation that they would name Aaron's parent or pre-1841 household.

Because the marriage yields no new Aaron locality or named pre-1841 associate, this stream did **not** expand into Ohio probate, appearance dockets, Belmont County, or a statewide name search.

### Control selected and access result

**Control proposition:** If the compilation-derived Wheeling/Ohio County route is real and Aaron became a county-court ward or minor, an Ohio County guardianship record could identify him, a guardian, a parent, or a residence. A positive does not by itself prove parentage; a document's actual relationship wording controls.

**Series and proposed window:** West Virginia and Regional History Center, *Ohio County (W. Va.) Court Records and Miscellaneous Papers*, **OHI 71, Guardian Bond #430, 1797–1866**, beginning with entries dated **1819–1838**. Search the Caton/Caten/Catton/Cayton/Katon variants, then each located guardian or minor for an Aaron identity and relationship. The window covers Aaron's reported birth through likely majority and avoids a broad Ohio County records sweep.

**Coverage inspected:** The WVU online microfilm index was checked on 24 September. It lists OHI 71 as the guardian-bond series and says to ask reference for help with the collection index. The catalog was available; no manuscript page, internal index leaf, or entry was available in this session.

**Result:** **Access established; no name-result claimed.** The catalog confirms the surviving source and the acquisition route, but does not supply a searchable or inspected manuscript. This is an access boundary, not a negative for Aaron, a Caton family, or Wheeling.

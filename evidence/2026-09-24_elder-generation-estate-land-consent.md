# Elder-generation estate/land records and the 1828 William Caton consent

**Date:** 24 September 2026  
**Status:** Partial result; original consent not inspected; two new loose-record routes identified  
**Question:** Can an original estate, land, guardianship, or marriage-consent record name Aaron Caton's parent or clarify the Thomas Caton family structure?

## Scope and method

This was a bounded follow-up to the existing will-book, tax, deed, court, marriage-typescript, and Prince William lost-record work. It did not repeat the closed broad surname searches. The pass instead:

1. tested whether the loose original of the 6 December 1828 consent underlying C092 could be reached;
2. checked official archival descriptions for unsearched loose estate and fiduciary series in Fauquier and Prince William Counties; and
3. identified the exact Culpeper deed-index route to the earlier trust recited in the image-verified 8 April 1834 release involving Aaron Caton, deceased.

No new original record inspected in this pass names an heir, child, sibling, widow, guardian, or residence for Aaron Caton or either Thomas Caton. Aaron Caton's parents remain unknown.

## 1. Fauquier consent dated 6 December 1828

### What the accessible copy says

The retained image at `evidence/2026-09-15_Fauquier_MarriageBonds_v4_image149_WilliamCaton_AnnLawrey_crop.jpg` is a typewritten copy, not the loose 1828 paper. Its material relationship language reads:

> I hereby consent to the intermarriage of my son William Caton with Ann F. Lawrey.

The typewritten name line renders `Thomas Caton Jr (SEAL)`. The witness lines render `Jas. Payne (of Thos)`, `Presley Johnson`, and `Thomas Caton`.

Therefore the derivative copy supports only these bounded observations:

- the copy explicitly represents its signer as William Caton's father;
- the copy prints `Jr.` after the signer's name;
- another `Thomas Caton` is represented as a witness; and
- the copy does not identify William's mother, a guardian, the two Thomases' ages, or their relationship to one another.

The copy cannot establish whether `Jr.` appears on the original, whether a later typist supplied or misread it, or whether the witness and signer were distinct people. `Senior` and `Junior` remain labels rather than proof of kinship.

### Original-image result and custody route

The FamilySearch image route required sign-in in this session, so no underlying loose original was inspected. The Library of Virginia/Archival Resources of the Virginias finding aid for **Fauquier County (Va.) Records, circa 1810–1904, undated** was then checked at collection and component level:

- collection: <https://arvasarchive.org/catalog/vi_vi04941>
- barcode `1203774`, 1810–1866 component: <https://arvasarchive.org/catalog/vi_vi04941_c02>
- barcode `1203779`, 1833–1859 component: <https://arvasarchive.org/catalog/vi_vi04941_c07>

The 1810–1866 component lists clerk's/criminal, fiduciary, tax, business, and estray material but no marriage records. The first component in this transferred collection whose description lists marriage records begins in 1833. This does **not** prove that the 1828 original is destroyed or absent from every Library of Virginia holding. It means this particular transferred-record collection does not presently identify a route to it.

The Fauquier County Circuit Court Clerk's [Records Room](https://www.fauquiercounty.gov/government/departments-a-g/circuit-court-clerk/records-room?locale=en) states that it holds marriage records from 1759 to the present. It is therefore the precise next custodian to ask for the loose original or an image made directly from it. The posted contact is `records.room@fauquiercounty.gov`; no request was sent and no fee was incurred in this pass.

**Result:** Whether `Jr.` appears on the 1828 original remains unresolved. D-012 remains open.

## 2. Fauquier loose fiduciary records, 1833–1940

The official ARVAS finding aid identifies an unsearched collection separate from the indexed will books:

- **Fauquier County (Va.) Fiduciary Records, 1833–1940**
- collection page: <https://arvasarchive.org/catalog/vi_vi05624>
- extent: 12.45 cubic feet in 14 boxes
- collection identifiers/barcodes: `1106654-1107006 circa, 1202626-1203785 circa`
- accessions: `37409` and `37410`
- access: no restrictions stated

The scope includes records filed by trustees, administrators, executors, guardians, and committees managing estates, including bonds, appraisements, audits, inventories, accounts, estate divisions, settlements, and dower material. Those document types are capable of stating widow, heir, child, guardian, or estate relationships.

The electronic finding aid provides no box/folder inventory and no digital objects. A surname search of its description would therefore test only catalog wording, not the papers. No Caton absence may be inferred.

This series begins in 1833. It directly covers the last documented years of Thomas Caton Senior and the 1834 endpoint by which the older Aaron Caton was deceased. The exact next action is a Library of Virginia Archives Reference Services request keyed to collection `vi_vi05624`, accessions `37409/37410`, asking staff to identify any box or folder indexed under Caton/Cayton, Thomas Caton, or Aaron Caton before ordering copies or arranging on-site review.

**Result:** High-value original series located, but no target item or family relationship was found or inspected in this pass.

## 3. Prince William 1834 sheriff-administered estate component

The official ARVAS finding aid for **Prince William County (Va.) Records, 1731–1969** contains one tightly relevant component:

- collection: <https://arvasarchive.org/catalog/vi_vi03418>
- barcode `1050832`, component dated 1834 and 1897: <https://arvasarchive.org/catalog/vi_vi03418_c03>
- component description includes `Settlement of Estates by sheriff as administrator`

The component also describes miscellaneous papers concerning the Magow family of Gainesville. The finding aid does not divide the two dates among the described materials, name the estates represented by the 1834 settlement material, or mention Aaron Caton. No digital object is linked.

This is nevertheless a distinct route from the already searched will-book indexes. A sheriff-administered settlement can represent an intestate or otherwise unadministered estate and can name distributees, creditors, or residences. Because the elder Aaron Caton was recorded as deceased by 8 April 1834, barcode `1050832` is a precise, bounded reference request rather than another broad surname search.

The Library of Virginia's [Prince William County Lost Records Localities digital collection](https://lva.primo.exlibrisgroup.com/discovery/collectionDiscovery?vid=01LVA_INST:01LVA&collectionId=81117106130005756&lang=en) returned zero item-level results for separate searches of `Caton` and `Cayton`. That is a negative only for the collection's current searchable item metadata. It is not a search of every image, every loose record, or barcode `1050832`.

**Result:** Exact 1834 original-series target located; estates unnamed; no relationship fact established.

## 4. Culpeper deed-index route to Aaron Caton's earlier trust

The 8 April 1834 Culpeper release already preserved in G001 recites an earlier deed in which Aaron Caton, deceased, had been trustee. The official [Library of Virginia Culpeper County microfilm guide](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/ccmf/VA/VA067) gives the shortest route to that earlier instrument:

- reel 29: **Index to Deeds, 1749–1913, Grantors A–D** — Caton/Cayton entries;
- reel 29a: **Index to Deeds, 1749–1913, Grantors E–Z** — John White and John Thorn entries;
- reel 30: **Index to Deeds, 1749–1913, Grantees A–Z** — cross-check all parties;
- reels 15–22: deed books spanning 1813–1836 for retrieval after an index citation is found.

The guide marks the index reels as available by interlibrary loan. Trustee names are not guaranteed to have been indexed as grantors or grantees, so a no-hit would not close the trust question. No index reel or original deed was inspected in this pass.

**Result:** Exact original-record route identified; earlier trust still unretrieved; no family fact established.

## Interpretation

The evidence does not support choosing Thomas Caton Senior, Thomas Caton Junior, William Caton, or the elder Aaron Caton as Aaron Caton's parent. The typed consent preserves explicit father-son language for William but does not solve which Thomas wrote it, because the original suffix and handwriting have not been checked. The tax, road, and census appearances of multiple Thomases remain identity and chronology evidence only.

The new value from this pass is archival routing:

1. Fauquier's loose fiduciary collection begins in 1833 and contains the record classes most capable of naming heirs or guardians.
2. Prince William barcode `1050832` isolates a 1834 sheriff-administered estate component not closed by will-book index negatives.
3. Culpeper reels 29, 29a, and 30 provide a finite index test for Aaron Caton's earlier trust.

Parents remain unknown.

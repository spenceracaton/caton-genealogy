## Question and scope

Narrow test of whether the Fauquier Laurie/Lawrey/Laury–Caton cluster left a direct-record trace in Delaware County, Ohio, during the proposed Ohio stop before Aaron Caton's August 1841 Delaware County marriage. Searched only the specified locality, period, and record types/routes: deeds, tax/directories, court, newspapers, and local compiled indexes. Same-name hits and online trees were not treated as identity evidence.

## Direct facts

- The Delaware County newspaper route is documented as continuous local publication in the target period: the Ohio State Gazette ran through 1834, and the Olentangy Gazette began in 1834. The public Delaware County newspaper-excerpt page presently exposes entries through 1834; its page labels 1835–1844 but supplies no retrievable text for those years. This establishes a source-coverage boundary, not a surname non-hit. Source: Ohio Genealogy Express, “The Ohio Gazette Newspaper Clippings of Delaware County, Ohio,” https://ohiogenealogyexpress.com/delaware/delawareco_news_delawaregazette.htm; Library of Congress, Delaware Gazette history, https://www.loc.gov/item/sn83035595/.

- A searchable secondary alphabetical index derived from *Delaware County, Ohio, Pioneer Families* surfaces Andrew Lowry at 1827 and 1838, and John Lowry at 1825 and 1838. It does not provide the underlying deed, tax, court, directory, or newspaper image in the search result, and it does not show Laurie, Laury, Caton, or a relationship to the Fauquier cluster. These are leads only, not direct residence proof. Source: https://www.scribd.com/document/486320307/Delaware-County-Ohio-Pioneers-Alphabetical-order (entries under “LOWERY (Lowery)”).

- The available Delaware County local-history search results also surface an Andrew Caton born in 1822, but the cited biographical account places him in Seneca County in 1843 and does not identify him with Aaron Caton or the Fauquier cluster. It is outside the requested 1835–1838 direct-record test and was not merged. Source: *Memorial Record of the Counties of Delaware, Union and Morrow, Ohio*, https://ldsgenealogy.com/OH/books/Memorial-record-of-the-counties-of-Delaware-Union-and-Morrow-Ohio-part-57.htm.

## Inference

The Lowry entries are compatible with a Delaware County presence during the broader period, but the accessible index does not establish that either man was Daniel Laurie, Ann (Caton) Laury's family, or connected to Aaron. The newspaper and index material therefore does not strengthen the Fauquier-parentage hypothesis beyond the already documented fact that Daniel Laury married Ann Caton in Fauquier in 1835 and that a son Joseph was born somewhere in Ohio about 1835.

## Bounded non-hit and limits

- No verified 1835–1838 Delaware County deed, tax duplicate, court record, directory, newspaper item, or original image was located that names Laurie, Lowry, Laury, Caton, or a linked Payne household.
- The public newspaper page does not expose the target years; the Pioneer Families result is a secondary index without underlying record images; and the Delaware County Treasurer search is a modern parcel lookup, not an 1835–1838 tax-duplicate archive. These access/source gaps prevent a complete negative finding.
- The separate image-verified 1840 Delaware County census test remains controlling: it found Jesse Caton and James Cayton, but no Thomas, William, Laurie, or Payne household. That later result does not prove the cluster was absent in 1835–1838 and does not locate Joseph Laurie's Ohio birthplace.

## Disposition

T28 produced no new A/B evidence and no relationship proof. Aaron's parentage remains unknown. No DNA evidence was considered. No claims, handoffs, or other files were edited.

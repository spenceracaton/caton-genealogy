## Headline result

The manuscript remains unread. A public 15-page typeset derivative titled *Overseers of the Poor 1804-1845* was inspected, including its pages 7–12 covering 1826–1838, but it contains annual officer lists and selected administrative notes rather than a full transcription. A companion five-page *Indentures* derivative contains one unrelated 1835 apprenticeship in the target window. Neither derivative contains Caton, Cayton, Caten, Catton, Kitton, or Katon. These derivative non-hits are not a search of the manuscript and do not establish that Reel 116 lacks a Caton-family entry. Aaron Caton's parents remain unknown.

The best exact acquisition route is **Library of Virginia interlibrary loan of Fauquier County Reel 116 through a participating local public or university library**. A free, one-hour **Library of Virginia Virtual Reading Room** appointment is a second exact route and may be faster for a bounded first inspection, but one hour is unlikely to cover the full 1826–1838 window.

This note does not repeat the finding-aid discovery recorded in S02. It resolves the practical acquisition choices, tests current alternate-copy routes, and records what each route can and cannot supply.

## Pre-registered proof test

Before any manuscript reading:

- An entry identifying an age-compatible Aaron and explicitly stating a parent or prior guardian would materially affect the parentage case.
- A master or guardian named Thomas is not Aaron's father unless the wording expressly states that relationship.
- An anonymous child, a surname-only entry, shared tailoring trade, or proximity to a Caton does not establish identity.
- A complete 1826–1838 non-hit would narrow only this volume and weaken only the poor-board apprenticeship explanation. It would not disprove private apprenticeship, Fauquier origin, or any parent-child relationship.

## Acquisition routes

### 1. Interlibrary loan — best route for the full window

The official Fauquier County microfilm inventory marks Reel 116 `ILL`, meaning a loan copy is available. The user asks a local public or university library's interlibrary-loan department to request the reel from the Library of Virginia. The borrowing library may submit through OCLC or by an ALA interlibrary-loan form.

Current LVA terms:

- 49-day loan.
- One renewal for 28 additional days unless the reel is overdue or on hold.
- Five-reel limit per patron.
- No charge from LVA for loans or copies; the borrowing library may impose its own fee or handling rules.
- The borrowing library should confirm that it has a microfilm reader/scanner and whether the reel must remain in-library.

This is the highest-confidence route for a page-by-page 1826–1838 read because it supplies the complete reel rather than requiring an entry date or page number in advance.

### 2. Virtual Reading Room — free exact remote access

LVA's Virtual Reading Room offers a free one-hour Zoom session in which staff operates the reader while the researcher directs movement through one reel of microfilm. Up to ten selected pages may be photographed and emailed after the session. A researcher may schedule up to three appointments per month; an unfinished reel requires another appointment.

The service is not a research consultation. Reel 116 is not within the published exclusion list, but LVA staff retains final authority to accept or decline the request. The booking must identify the title and Reel 116. This route is suitable for locating the volume's internal arrangement, year breaks, and a small number of target entries; it is not a dependable substitute for borrowing the reel for a thirteen-year page-by-page read.

As of 24 September 2026, LVA's site posts a temporary Public Services closure for 21–26 September during an internet-service transition. That may delay access; it does not change the published route.

### 3. LVA archival reproduction — useful after an entry is located

The current Archives Materials Request portal charges a nonrefundable $10 service fee for one person in one record from specific indexed material. The fee includes up to twenty photocopies, PDFs, or 300-dpi JPGs from the service copy, including microfilm; additional pages are invoiced separately. The portal estimates four to eight weeks and limits users to eight requests per month.

Reel 116 has no located index, page count, or frame map. A request to search thirteen years may therefore exceed the portal's defined service. Reproduction is the best follow-up after an exact date, page, or frame is found through ILL, a Virtual Reading Room session, or an onsite read.

The general digitization price list separately quotes microfilm prints or email/FTP delivery at $0.50 per image with a $5 minimum. Archives Reference Services should resolve which ordering path applies to an identified Reel 116 frame before payment. No order was started.

### 4. Onsite use at LVA

Reel 116 can be read at LVA. The microfilm area allows free scans to a user-supplied flash drive; paper prints cost $0.50 per page. A valid LVA card is required for printing and for any closed-stack retrieval. This is an exact route but was not used in this pass.

## Public derivatives inspected

The Gott Library's Fauquier Poorhouse Project links two public, searchable PDFs created in 2026. Neither PDF contains manuscript images or identifies Reel 116, barcode 1125621, an LVA call number, or a page/frame concordance. Their evidentiary weight is therefore limited to the text they reproduce.

### *Overseers of the Poor 1804-1845*

- Fifteen typeset pages; PDF metadata identifies Laura Kelsey as author.
- Pages 7–12 cover the target years 1826–1838 and list annual presidents, overseers, clerks, meeting places, other officers, and selected administrative notes.
- Exact text searches for Caton, Cayton, Caten, Catton, Kitton, and Katon returned no hit. A year search located both 1826 and 1838, confirming that the intended span was present in the searchable text.
- The derivative has no ordinary child-placement or apprenticeship entries in those pages. Its silence cannot be transferred to the underlying minute book.

### *Indentures*

- Five typeset pages; PDF metadata identifies Arlene Jenkins as author. The derivative contains five selected transcriptions rather than a chronological run of Reel 116.
- The only target-window item is headed `1835-02 Fauquier County Courthouse Deed Room — Indenture of Washington Woolingham to John Feagans`.
- **Date:** county-court order, June term 1835; indenture dated 23 June 1835.
- **Child and parent:** Washington Woolingham, about eight years old; the copied court order identifies him as son of Polly Woolingham.
- **Master and trade:** John Feagans, also rendered Fegans/Feagan in the derivative; farmer.
- **Overseers:** the opening names Sidney F. Chapman and Richard Rixey, while the operative text and signature name Wilfred A. Smith and Richard Rixey. The derivative does not resolve the discrepancy.
- **Terms recorded:** ordinary support and instruction, plus $12 to Washington at the end of the term.
- Exact text searches for Caton, Cayton, Caten, Catton, Kitton, and Katon returned no hit. A control search for Woolingham found the 1835 transcription.

This unrelated indenture shows that apprenticeship material exists in the project's derivatives and supplies a dated court-order lead, but it does not identify Aaron, Joseph, Thomas, or another Caton and does not establish what the full overseers' volume contains.

## Reading protocol once acquired

1. Capture the reel title target, volume title/header, first dated page, and the method used to identify frame/page position.
2. Read 1826–1838 page by page, recording each examined date and frame/page handle.
3. Check Caton, Cayton, Caten, Catton, Kitton, Katon, and plausible handwriting variants; do not rely on OCR alone.
4. Record every named child, parent, prior guardian, master, trade, meeting date, order date, execution date, indenture, release, reassignment, and linked court order.
5. Preserve target and adjacent context at high resolution only where publication is permitted. The catalog states no restrictions, but any reproduction terms must still be followed.
6. If no hit appears, extend to 1820–1825 and 1839–1841 and state the exact additional coverage. Keep unexamined years explicit.
7. Follow any dated entry to the corresponding Fauquier Minute/Order Book or deed book; a master-only index can conceal a child's surname.

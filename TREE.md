## 5. The surname

**"Caton" here is a late Americanisation of KITTON / KITTEN**, not the reverse.
Every contemporary record of the immigrant generation spells it Kitton or Kitten,
and the changeover is visible on the 1790 Washington County schedule. The English
target is **Kitton**. "Caton of Lancashire" is an artefact of the modern spelling —
do not write it into a tree.

Cooper County is no longer under blanket quarantine: the direct line lived there
1866–79 alongside several of these households. Keep the identities separate; the
separations now have to be re-argued against a corrected timeline, not assumed.

## 7. What would change the shape of this tree

1. **Y-DNA.** Spencer is an unbroken patrilineal Caton. An FTDNA Y-37, joined to the
   Caton surname project, tests join 4 directly — whether Aaron's male line belongs
   to this family at all. A negative deletes sections 4 and 5 from his ancestry in
   one result. Still not started, and it is the highest-value action in the file.
2. **Missouri 1842–45** (T10). Aaron was in Missouri by Dec 1843, twenty years before
   any other Missouri record. The 1859–79 residence is a return, not a first migration.
3. **John Boring × Eliza Ann marriage record.** One document. If her maiden name is
   Caton, Harrison's parents become findable, in the right surname, county and
   generation to bear on Aaron.
4. **Harrison Caton in the 1900 Gentry Co., MO census.** The 1900 schedule gives both
   parents' birthplaces — the nearest this file has come to the generation above Aaron.
5. **Aaron's CMSR jacket at NARA**, and his pension file.

# S08 — Identify the women and prove the Caton–Lawrey relationships without presupposing a remarriage

**Target in every stream:** Aaron Caton, reported born 16 May 1820 in Virginia, married Sarah Ann Gardner in Delaware County, Ohio, in August 1841, later a Missouri resident and tailor. His father and mother remain unidentified. These are investigation instructions, not newly proved relationships. [R1]

## Core question

Who could have been Aaron's mother, and what do records of women and in-laws reveal about the proposed family? This stream should not be postponed until a father is completely proved: a woman's estate, remarriage or inherited-land transaction may identify the children more clearly than the father's surviving records. But finding a wife of a candidate father does not automatically identify the mother of every child.

The immediate structure to test is the trio of documented/reported Fauquier marriages: Thomas/Elizabeth Lawrey, William/Ann F. Lawrey, and Ann Caton/Daniel Laury. The marriages themselves are distinct propositions from the claim that three Caton siblings married three Laurie siblings. No such reciprocal sibling structure should be assumed just because the pattern is attractive. [R2:C088,C092,C098]

## Correct starting assumptions

The 1810 census's older woman is not identified as the wife, nor its younger woman as the daughter. The younger woman could have been a wife already, or could have had another relationship; the 1810 and 1820 Thomas entries could also concern different men. Therefore the old search restricted to an 1811-1819 remarriage to a widow is too narrow. [N2]

The identity of the 1828 Elizabeth Lawrey groom remains unresolved. Do not use an online birth/death pair or military service by similarly named children to attach that groom to the older Boonville tailor or the Kentucky household. S01/S03 own those identity checks. This stream asks which women and children each actual document identifies.

## 1. Build the named-women register from the marriage packets

Reuse the complete packets acquired by S01. Separate Ann Caton from Ann F. Lawrey and Elizabeth Lawrey. Record every spelling, surname, signature or copied name, named father, stated age/status, bondsman, witness and date. Do not normalize Ann into Nancy, add a middle name, or change Lawrey into a particular modern spelling without evidence.

For each parent consent, identify whose consent it was and to whom it applied. A statement that someone was 21 may attest legal age rather than an exact birthday; a voluntary consent may not conclusively establish minority. Preserve the wording instead of computing an unjustifiably exact birth boundary. In the 1835 packet, distinguish bond, permission, recorded and ceremony dates, rather than resolving conflicting dates by assumption. [A7,A8]

The William packet associates Thomas Guttridge, James Payne of Thomas, and Presley Johnson with the transaction. Those are leads to the named bride/groom's social network, not automatic blood relatives. James R. Payne in 1850 Missouri should not be equated with the 1829 bondsman without an independent identity bridge.

## 2. Prove Laurie parents and sibling links

Treat Catharine/Catherine Laurie, Joseph Laurie, Daniel, Elizabeth, Ann F., Newton and Joseph N. as separate subjects until a document connects them. Do not merge two Catharines from inconsistent ages in later histories. Search the parents' wills/administrations, marriage consents, land partitions, heirs' deeds and powers of attorney. Extract whole heir lists including married daughters and representatives of deceased children.

Work the records in which women have an economic interest: a release of an inherited share, a distribution receipt, a settlement, or an agreement joining a husband and wife. Ask what legal interest the instrument actually conveys; a dower release names a wife but does not normally enumerate children. An in-law estate may provide the explicit sibling connection missing from census proximity.

## 3. Acquire the specific family-paper target

The SHSMO finding aid for Cochran-Jeffress Family Papers,C4669, identifies folder 2 as the Jeffress Family Bible,1831-1942; Isabel Laurie is indexed in folders 1,2,4. Request the relevant Bible pages and genealogical references with title/publication pages and contextual notes if available. This is a precise existing holding, but the finding aid does not establish Isabel's parents or her relationship to the Caton spouses. [A20]

Read the Bible entries for when and by whom they were written. An 1831 date inside a later family Bible can be retrospective. Keep family-history books, Bible entries and modern pedigree notes distinct, even when they are filed together. Check for copied language and citations before treating a repeated relationship as independently corroborated. If the collection concerns a different Laurie family, record that and stop the branch.

S07 uses any Ohio county/date revealed here; it should not submit a duplicate archive request. S06 owns the Dumm papers, not this collection.

## 4. Broaden the candidate-wife search responsibly

Begin with named spouses emerging from records, then search the relevant Virginia marriage indexes and originals. A practical initial window around 1795-1823 can test a wife present by 1810 and later possibilities, but it is a starting window, not a biological or legal exclusion. Expand earlier if the demonstrated household history warrants it. Prior search negatives must retain their exact county, volume, date range, variants and original-versus-abstract limits.

Prioritize Fauquier/Prince William and the counties supplied by actual parties, sureties or residences. Fairfax or Stafford should enter on a documented reason, not simply because all adjacent counties were named in an old checklist. Search widows under a former married name when evidence identifies one; do not require widowhood from the census alone.

Once a plausible woman is identified, trace her parents/siblings and estate only far enough to determine whether Aaron appears as a child or grandchild. A named maternal grandparent's distribution to Aaron could identify his mother without first solving every Thomas.

## Tests that decide the question

An explicit statement tying target Aaron to a mother, accompanied by age/residence or family evidence resolving identity, is the strongest result. A record naming a candidate father's wife after 1820 does not prove she gave birth to Aaron. A documented earlier wife dying before his birth, remarriage after his birth or an explicit step-relationship can separate those propositions.

A wife/daughter identification in 1810 should be rejected if an independent record assigns the women differently. If William and Ann prove children of different Thomases, discard the assumed sibling model rather than fitting both into one census household. If the Lawrey spouses are not siblings, that weakens the proposed intact-family migration interpretation but does not undo their individually documented marriages.

## Deliverable

Produce one evidence-backed relationship table, with separate columns for spouse, parent and sibling links and the document supporting each. Unknown women remain unknown. Include a candidate-wife timeline with overlapping/contradictory evidence visible, a list of exact maternal/in-law estate targets, and the source-dependence status of any Bible/history material.

The first pass ends after the shared marriage packet analysis and the specific C4669 acquisition or request. Do not turn this into a survey of every Laurie family or a search for daughters' naming patterns before the women's identities are documented.

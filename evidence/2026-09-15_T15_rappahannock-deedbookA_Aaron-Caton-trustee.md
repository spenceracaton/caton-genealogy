# T15 — Rappahannock Deed Book A: Aaron Caton, deceased trustee

STATUS: found

## Research question

Does the Rappahannock County Deed Book A lead identify the elder Aaron Caton in
an original record, and does it provide a date boundary or family relationship?

- FamilySearch catalog record: [Deeds, 1833–1905; general index, 1833–1952](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/koha:388386)
- Film/digital note: `Deed books, v. A-B 1833–1837`, film 33660, DGS 8357621.
- Direct FamilySearch image/transcript route: [Rappahannock Deed Book A, image 192](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSYD-V9KB-B?view=fullText&keywords=Aaron+Caton&searchForm=advanced&lang=en)
- The FamilySearch full-text search for the exact phrase `Aaron Caton` in DGS 8357621 returned one result, Deed Book A, image 192. The viewer identifies the handwritten page as printed/page number 305; the facing page begins printed/page number 306.

## Image-level observation

The image is a handwritten deed recorded in Rappahannock County. The entry begins:

> This Indenture made this 8th day of April 1834 between John White of the County of Fauquier and State of Virginia of the one part, and John Thorn of the County of Rappahannock and State aforesaid, witnesseth; ...

The release concerns land in Rappahannock County then occupied by John Thorn. In the consideration clause, the earlier security/deed is described as one “in which Aaron Caton Decd was Trustee.” The page is followed by the Fauquier acknowledgment of John White dated 8 April 1834 and the Rappahannock clerk’s recording entry dated 22 April 1834.

The handwritten page supports the reading `Aaron Caton Decd` / `Aaron Caton deceased`; it is not merely an OCR artifact. The preceding deed date is not filled in legibly in the visible entry, so this image does not recover the date or terms of the earlier trust deed.

## Interpretation boundary

- **Evidence:** An original Rappahannock deed recorded 22 April 1834 refers to an earlier deed in which Aaron Caton, deceased, was trustee; John White is identified as a Fauquier resident and John Thorn as a Rappahannock resident/occupant.
- **Inference:** The elder Aaron Caton died before the 22 April 1834 recording, and his public activity extended into the Fauquier–Rappahannock land network.
- **Not established:** The deed does not name Aaron’s heirs, parents, wife, or relationship to Thomas Caton, Moses Caton, or the direct-line Aaron born in 1820. It cannot by itself choose between Thomas Sr. and Moses as the direct-line Aaron’s father.

GRADE: A for the deed-level wording and date boundary; B for the geographic-network inference.

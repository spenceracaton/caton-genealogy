# Caton genealogy — working brief

**State:** research synthesis last reviewed 15 September 2026; 209 claims are
registered. **Research question:** Who were the parents of Aaron Caton, born
16 May 1820 in Virginia? They remain unknown. The parentage case is grade C; do
not convert the leading Fauquier hypothesis into a proved relationship (C099).

“Wheeling” is unsourced (C075). Records for Aaron say Virginia; no Caton heads a
household in Ohio County in 1820. Keep Wheeling as an unverified lead, not a search
anchor.

## Competing origin explanations

**Fauquier / Prince William is the stronger working hypothesis, not proof.** Three
Caton men—Thomas, Aaron, and Moses—were taxed together on Broad Run, Prince William
County, in the 1790s (C153–C157). The elder Aaron was an adult there, childless in
the 1810 census, absent as a household head in 1820, and called deceased in a 1834
deed. Thomas moved to Fauquier by 1810. His 1820 Fauquier household held two boys
under ten and a wife aged 26–45; the wife may be a second wife (C080, C159). This
is age-compatible with the direct-line Aaron, but neither the census nor another
record names Aaron as Thomas's son.

Moses's 1820 Fairfax household also held a boy under ten, but C181 identifies that
slot as Moses B. Caton Jr. (the identification remains derivative). In 1830 neither
the elder Thomas nor Moses had a boy under fifteen at home (C082, C166); no Caton
headed a Prince William household (C167). Joseph Caton, a tailor born in Fauquier
about 1823, and the William–Ann Lawrey Caton group moving Fauquier → Ohio → Cooper
County, Missouri, support a regional connection (C076–C077, C098, C189, C191).
The 1898 notice calling Joseph “the guest of his niece, Mrs. Sawtell” supports a
Joseph–Aaron sibling hypothesis, but does not prove it (C110). The Ohio chapter in
Delaware County remains unexplained.

**Maryland–Pennsylvania / Ohio County remains a weaker competing route.** Catons
appear in Ohio County, Virginia, and Delaware County, Ohio, but no record connects
that group to Aaron; the proposed Wheeling origin is unsupported (C073, C090,
C105–C106). A Thomas Caton of Tyler County is a separate candidate with no located
1820/1830 household (C139–C143). Neither this route nor Fauquier explains where
Aaron was enumerated in 1840: he was not a household head and may be a tally in
another household.

**Do not make George W. Caton the father.** The Alexandria-born Boonville tailor's
1840 household has age-compatible young men, but he headed no household in 1820
(C121, C129–C135). The 1840 census does not identify those young men as Aaron or
Joseph.

## Proven line and Aaron's anchors

Spencer Caton → Philip Brettun Caton (1950) → Chandler Maynard Caton (1912–2002)
→ Louis Merrill Caton (1886–1970) → William Bramwell Caton (1847–1941) → **Aaron
Caton (16 May 1820; parents unknown).**

- Aaron married Sarah Ann Gardner in Delaware County, Ohio, in August 1841. Their
  1850 Delaware household is canonical as `US-1850-OH-DELAWARE-AARON-01`. It records
  Aaron as a Virginia-born tailor; the image and later evidence support daughter
  Julia's Missouri birthplace. The derivative “Massachusetts” reading is preserved
  as a conflict, not silently erased (C005–C006, C038).
- Julia was born in Missouri in December 1843; the family was back in Delaware
  County by June 1844 (C037, C144). The 1850 household also includes Sarah, Mary E.,
  William B., and Nancy. A son Phineas died in 1845 (C185). Sarah's Gardner family
  is an Ohio anchor (C186).
- Aaron served in Company E, 2nd Missouri Cavalry, from 1861 to 1865 (C023). In
  1870 he was in daughter Julia Sawtell's Cooper County household, recorded age 55
  and Virginia-born; the age is an outlier against the 1850/1860 evidence. He died
  in Boonville on 17 January 1878 (C041, C108). Census counts belong in the census
  ledger, not narrative re-transcriptions.
- William Bramwell's Missouri residence and later Kansas move are documented
  through 1879. Aaron's route is Ohio → Missouri → Ohio → Missouri; the reason for
  the 1843 Missouri visit is unknown (C046).

## Identity boundaries and earlier ancestry

Use `people.jsonl` for stable disambiguation IDs. It keeps these working identities
separate unless evidence justifies a merge: the direct-line Aaron, elder Aaron,
Aaron F. of Cooper County; elder/younger Fauquier Thomas and Ohio County Thomas;
the two George W. Catons of Boonville and Muskingum; and the Fairfax/Muskingum
Moses candidates. The IDs distinguish research subjects; they do not prove kinship.

Aaron F. Caton (born about 1842) was a son of William and Ann F. Lawrey and is not
the direct-line Aaron (C052, C149). Cooper County also had Caton households of
Delaware, Kentucky, and Virginia origins (C096). Keep the two Josephs distinct:
the Fauquier-born tailor and William's son (C149). `people.jsonl` covers the core
ambiguous identities needing stable IDs; other exclusions remain in their linked
claims.

The English–Maryland–Pennsylvania chain is a separate problem. “Caton” is a late
Americanization of Kitton/Kitten in the Washington County, Pennsylvania line, but
its parentage links remain incomplete. The connection from that line to Wheeling
and Aaron is unproved; if the Fauquier hypothesis is correct, the earlier chain may
describe another family (C105, C127). Do not use surname similarity to bridge it.

## Record and evidence rules

- `DATA_MODEL.md` defines schemas and authority. The census ledger owns household
  observations; `sources.jsonl` citation bundles; `people.jsonl` identity
  boundaries; `claims.jsonl` assertions; `searches.jsonl` search scope/outcomes;
  and `discrepancies.jsonl` competing readings and adjudication. `NEXT-SESSION.md`
  is only the action queue. Dated handoffs are historical context.
- Citation bundles and claim classifications migrated from older prose are marked
  as such. Negative searches not yet decomposed remain explicitly
  `legacy_unstructured`; do not treat unknown query or coverage fields as known.
- Grade A is primary/near-primary; B is strong, identified secondary; C is a lead
  only. Never promote a C-level relationship into the working tree without A/B
  corroboration. A name match, index hit, or census age slot alone does not prove
  identity or kinship.
- Before searching, check claims and search records. Preserve each new record in
  its own evidence file with image/page, source URL, transcription, confidence,
  and limitations. Do not edit another agent's evidence note; the coordinator
  merges canonical records, updates task state, regenerates the claims index, and
  runs `python3 tools/check-project.py`.
- Claim IDs are unique: Claude Code allocates `C###`; ChatGPT allocates `G###`.
  Never reuse or renumber an existing ID. Keep parents unknown until proved.

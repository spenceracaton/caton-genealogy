# S02 — Find an apprenticeship, guardianship or other childhood record naming Aaron

**Target in every stream:** Aaron Caton, reported born 16 May 1820 in Virginia, married Sarah Ann Gardner in Delaware County, Ohio, in August 1841, later a Missouri resident and tailor. His father and mother remain unidentified. These are investigation instructions, not newly proved relationships. [R1]

## Question and reason for priority

Was target Aaron bound to a trade, placed under a guardian, maintained by local authorities, or named in a family court proceeding before his 1841 marriage? A record joining his name to a parent, master, place and age could resolve more than another generation of surname-and-census comparisons.

Aaron and the probable relative Joseph were tailors; their absence from the presently reconstructed 1830 Caton households motivates this search. It does not prove that they were brothers, poor, orphaned, or formally apprenticed. The search remains worthwhile even if a Thomas identity changes. Do not condition all childhood research on first proving a particular Thomas destitute. [R1; R2:C195]

## Source map and corrections

The principal bounded source is Fauquier's Overseers of the Poor Minutes,1804-1845, LVA Reel 116. The official inventory marks it available for interlibrary loan. The Gott Library's Poorhouse Project is a separate access/contact lead, not proof that the register has a complete public transcript. [A1-A3]

Gott's *Fauquier County, Virginia, Guardian Bonds,1759-1871* is another index target. Do not use catalog 642321, film 1689208/DGS 8682856: the previous citation points to Chatham County, North Carolina. [N1; A6]

Fauquier Fiduciary Records,1833-1940, include loose guardians' and other fiduciaries' papers in fourteen boxes. Their overall date range does not guarantee a relevant 1830s file. Ask for an internal list before requesting an undirected search of every box. [A4]

## Acquisition and review sequence

**Access first.** Check the shared request register for any outstanding inquiry. Ask the Gott Library whether its overseers/indenture work has a transcript or index, and whether Gott's guardian-bond volume has Caton variants. Request precise page/box citations and costs before paid work. If no usable index exists, arrange authorized access to Reel 116 through the holding institution or an eligible local library. A catalog hit is not a successful register search.

**Read the public-placement material in a bounded window.** Start with 1823-1835, then extend 1836-1841; expand earlier only when an entry or family death warrants it. Search Caton, Caten, Catton, Cayton, Katon and justified handwriting variants, and terms such as bound, apprentice, orphan, guardian, indenture, maintenance and tailor/taylor. Where names are not indexed, read the relevant sequence rather than relying on full-text search alone.

**Follow entries through time.** Capture the initial order, executed indenture, approval, master, trade, term, stated age/birthdate, named parent, security, cancellation, reassignment and completion. A later release can identify the same child after a sparse first order. Extract nearby related entries but do not declare neighboring names relatives.

**Then test private or court-ordered arrangements.** Relevant county court volumes include 1822-1828 on Reel 57,1829-1835 on Reel 58 and 1836-1840 on Reel 59. Search deed indexes and deeds for a privately recorded indenture; do not assume the child is the indexed party. Once a master is named, rerun the bounded indexes under that person's name and follow the master's relocation only as far as it can connect the boy. [A1]

**Use guardianship/loose files as their own record class.** Extract the ward, person whose property is managed, guardian, bond date, securities, settlement and any relationship wording. A guardian's name is not a father's name. A file may concern inherited property rather than the child's day-to-day residence. A court or document term should be interpreted in its actual historical context, not turned into an unsupported claim that both parents were dead.

## Decision rules and safeguards

An entry naming Aaron, a father, and a birthdate compatible with 16 May 1820 is a major positive, but distinguish any same-name child through later trade/residence/kin evidence. A record with Aaron alone is a locator, not a parentage finding. A master named Thomas is not a father unless the wording or separate evidence establishes that relationship.

A named father other than Thomas must be preserved immediately and tested, not absorbed into a supposed Thomas guardianship. An explicit illegitimacy or step-relationship also matters; do not translate legal care automatically into biological parentage. Read the record's exact language.

No public binding after a complete review would close that source/window, not prove no apprenticeship ever occurred. A failed search at Fauquier would not eliminate another county, private instruction, unrecorded employment or a missing record. Low assessed property may motivate a hypothesis but does not itself establish destitution.

## Geographic expansion and division of work

Do not call Prince William, Fairfax, or Washington a dead end solely because one overseers series is missing. Inventory alternate court/deed sources and expand only when family locality or a named master supplies a reason. Do not assume an Alexandria record of the D.C. period belongs to Washington City; establish the correct court and place before retrieving.

S01 supplies Thomas observations, but this stream can search the children's names immediately. S07 owns Ohio local childhood/employment records, and S04 owns the older Aaron's specific estate/trust chain. Share a discovered master or guardian rather than assigning two agents to the same volume.

Include sources searched under child, parent, guardian and master. The objective is a named relationship or a bounded elimination of a record source—not confirmation of the apprenticeship story itself.

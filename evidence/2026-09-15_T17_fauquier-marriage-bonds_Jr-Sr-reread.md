# T17 — Fauquier marriage-bond reread: Thomas Caton Jr. and Senior

STATUS: found; clarification of evidence boundary

## Source

- FamilySearch catalog: [Fauquier marriage bonds and returns, 1759–1854](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/koha:363669)
- The catalog states that volumes 1–6 are **typed transcripts** made in 1933
  from worn original bonds; volume 7 is the original-record volume.
- Volume 4, 1820–1832: DGS 7578973, image 149. [Direct image/transcript route](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-89XF-K9SG-S?view=fullText&keywords=Caton&searchForm=advanced&lang=en)
- Volume 5, 1830–1849: DGS 7578973, image 45. [Direct image/transcript route](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-L9XF-K9SX-V?view=fullText&keywords=Caton&searchForm=advanced&lang=en)

## Image-level observations

### William Caton–Ann F. Lawrey, 1828–1829

The typed copy on volume 4 image 149 contains:

- a bond dated 12 January 1829 for William Caton and Ann F. Lawrey;
- William Caton and James Payne “of Thos” as the bound parties;
- a consent dated 6 December 1828 stating, “I hereby consent to the
  intermarriage of my son William Caton with Ann F. Lawrey”; and
- the consent's name line rendered as `Thomas Caton Jr (SEAL)`, witnessed by
  James Payne “of Thos,” Presley Johnson, and Thomas Caton.

### Daniel Laury–Ann Caton, 1835

The typed copy on volume 5 image 45 contains:

- a bond dated 27 March 1835 naming Daniel Laury and `Thomas Caton junr` as
  the bound parties;
- a condition saying Ann Caton was “Daughter of Thomas Caton senr.”;
- a clerk's entry on 7 April 1835 that `Thomas Caton Junr` swore Daniel Laury
  was over twenty-one;
- a consent dated 7 April 1835 saying Ann was the writer's daughter, with the
  name line rendered as `Thomas Caton Senior (SEAL)`; and
- witnesses listed as Thomas Guttridge, Thomas Caton, and William Caton.

## Interpretation boundary

- **Evidence:** The 1828–1829 copy identifies Thomas Caton Jr. as the father of
  William Caton. The 1835 copy identifies Thomas Caton Senior as the father of
  Ann Caton, while Thomas Caton Jr. is the co-obligor and affiant on the bond.
- **Evidence:** `Jr`, `junr`, and `Senior` are typeset in the 1933 transcript;
  they are not handwritten examples from which the individuals' own naming
  habits can be judged.
- **Inference:** The documents are consistent with a two-man Jr/Senior family
  structure, but they also leave open the possibility of one man using an
  inconsistent suffix in different instruments. No relationship between the
  two Thomases is stated.
- **Not established:** These records do not name Aaron Caton, do not prove that
  Thomas Jr and Thomas Senior were father and son, and do not prove that Ann and
  William were siblings or that either was Aaron's sibling.

GRADE: A for the typed-transcript statements; B for the unresolved identity
interpretation. This reread does not alter the direct-line parentage conclusion.

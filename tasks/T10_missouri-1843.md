# T10 — Where in Missouri was the family in 1843–44?

**Priority: 1. Replaces T01, which was void on a misread census cell.**

Julia Caton was born in **Missouri on 15 December 1843** (C037, her own death
certificate, naming Aaron Caton and Sarah Gardner as parents). Aaron married in
Delaware County, Ohio in **August 1841** and his next child, Mary E., was born in
**Ohio c.1845**. So the family was in Missouri for a window of roughly
**1842–1844** and back in Ohio by c.1845.

**This is twenty years earlier than any previously documented Missouri presence**
(C046). The 1859–1879 Missouri residence is a *return*.

**Target:** reconstruct the shortest documented route: Delaware County, Ohio in
August 1841 → Missouri by 15 December 1843 → Ohio by c.1845.

## Phase 1 — original marriage packet

The indexed marriage is insufficient. Pull the original page from **Marriage
Records Vol. 1, 1835–1845**, plus every surviving related paper in the early
marriage-bond series (1832–1865):

- Aaron Caton and Sarah Ann Gardner
- license issued 7 August 1841; marriage 8 August 1841
- request the license, bond, return, consent (if any), clerk's entry, surety,
  witnesses, and officiant

The bound-register image is now verified and supplied no additional associates.
Request any separate loose packet from the Delaware County Records Center. Do not request a mere certified abstract;
the value is in every name and signature on the original material.

## Phase 2 — Missouri, bounded by Phase 1

Search the people and places exposed in Phase 1 before expanding geography.
If it exposes nothing, work the later-county prior in this order:

1. Boone County court records, 1842–45; deeds, 1841–45.
2. Cooper County tax, court and deed records, 1842–45.
3. Livingston County tax, court and deed records, 1842–45.

The Missouri State Archives database is browseable by county, office, record
type, and date, but not by personal name. Use record indexes before page-level
reading. FamilySearch has Boone image groups for court records (1831–52) and
deeds (1839–45); access may require sign-in.

Missouri had **no statewide vital registration** in 1843, so there is no birth
record to find. A deed or federal-land non-hit does not exclude a tailor who was
renting. The later counties are priors, not evidence.

## Consequence for the do-not-merge list

Every Missouri Caton separation in `BRIEF.md` §6 was reasoned against the belief
that Aaron reached Missouri around 1859. That belief is now wrong. **This merges
nothing** — the separations may still be correct — but each must be re-argued on
age, spouse, children and occupation rather than on "Aaron wasn't in Missouri yet."

Re-examine in particular the **Jesse Caton / Warren–Linn County** cluster and the
**Boone County Kentucky line**, both dismissed partly on timing.

## Result

The exact FamilySearch image URL was tested first because the supplied typescript already locates the target by volume, pages, DGS, and image. The available browser reached FamilySearch's sign-in page, not the image viewer, and no authenticated browser session was available. The original therefore was not inspected online. No OCR was run: the retained source is a clear typescript and OCR cannot recover handwriting, omitted wording, or material absent from that derivative.

The retained 4,993 × 3,728 image confirms that the located record is a typescript. It renders the consent as “my son William Caton,” places `Thomas Caton Jr` in the represented signer/name line, and lists `Jas. Payne (of Thos)`, `Presley Johnson`, and `Thomas Caton` as witnesses. It cannot show whether the original had `Jr`, who wrote any suffix, whether Thomas signed or marked, whether the witnesses wrote their own names, or whether the typist omitted or normalized text.

The Library of Virginia's current Fauquier microfilm inventory still identifies Reel 67, Marriage Bonds and Returns Vol. 4, 1820–1832, as a typescript. It does not list a microfilm of the corresponding loose originals. The county's current secured remote-access description begins its online marriage index in 1965, so that subscription is not a route to this 1828 packet.

The Fauquier County Circuit Court Clerk's Records Room remains the precise first contact. Its official page states that it holds marriage records from 1759 to the present; accepts record searches when a reference is unknown, covering up to a ten-year span; lists regular copies at $0.50 per page; and posts the Records Room address and email. The broader Clerk page independently lists the same Records Room email and gives the office telephone number and hours.

## What the original could resolve

A complete front-and-back image of the original could establish whether `Jr` is actually present; its position relative to the body, signature or mark, seal, and docketing; whether the same or different hands supplied the body, name, suffix, and witness lines; whether the father signed or marked; the exact original witness wording; and any language or endorsements omitted from the typescript. Comparison with another independently identified Thomas Caton signature or mark could then become an identity test, subject to normal handwriting limits.

## What the original could not resolve by itself

Even if the original says `Thomas Caton Jr`, the suffix would not by itself identify him with the 20–30-year-old `Thomas Caton Jun` in the 1830 census; relational suffixes were contextual rather than permanent identifiers. The document would prove only the relationship it expressly states between the consenting Thomas and William. It would not identify William's mother, prove that the signer was the elder or younger 1830 household head, prove a relationship between the signer and the witness Thomas Caton, or name Aaron Caton as a son or sibling.

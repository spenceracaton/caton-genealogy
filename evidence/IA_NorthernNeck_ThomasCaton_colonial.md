## Summary of the Colonial Thomas Caton

A gentleman landholder, "Mr. Thomas Caton of Frederick Co.," active 1742–1757:
- Member of Ancient South River Club, Anne Arundel MD (after 1742)
- Northern Neck grants totaling at least 826 acres:
  - 230 acres (assigned to John Carlyle, surveyed 4 Apr 1750)
  - 400 acres in Frederick Co. (20 Oct 1750, Grant G-459)
  - 196 acres in Frederick Co. (30 May 1751, Grant G-521)
- "Gentleman and Justice" in 1757 Frederick County depositions
- Militia captain in Fauquier County (date uncertain — the book covers the proprietary
  period, roughly 1759–1780s)

## Relation to the Broad Run Catons

The three Caton brothers on Broad Run, Prince William County — Thomas (b. ~1760–65),
Aaron (b. 1765–74), Moses (b. by 1781) — first appear on the land tax in 1782. Prince
William is within the Northern Neck (between the Rappahannock and Potomac). The colonial
Thomas Caton's Northern Neck land activity, his Frederick County grants, and his Fauquier
militia captaincy all place him in the geographic and chronological position to be the
father of the Broad Run brothers. The 230-acre tract assigned to John Carlyle (the
Alexandria merchant) may have been in the Prince William/Fauquier area, though the exact
location is not recoverable from the snippet.

This Thomas Caton is **not yet proven** as the father of the Broad Run group. The
connection is: same surname, same general region (Northern Neck/Valley), plausible
timeframe (active 1742–57; sons born 1760–81). Further evidence needed from Prince
William deed books or Frederick County court records.

# Fauquier chancery 061-1861-012: Caton/Lawrie collateral-heir evidence

## Research question

Test A6 in `PARENTAGE-TESTS.md` asked whether Fauquier chancery material could identify a Caton/Lawrie relationship relevant to Aaron Caton's parentage.

## Source and retrieval

- Virginia Library of Virginia, Fauquier County chancery case `061-1861-012`, `John N. Caton & wife et al. v. executor of Henry Bussey et al.`
- Case page: <https://old.lva.virginia.gov/chancery/case_detail.asp?CFN=061-1861-012>
- The case file is indexed as 40 scanned images. The downloaded case PDFs and rendered page images were reviewed locally.
- Handwritten pages were handled crop-first. Full-page views were used only where the crop clipped the beginning of a line or where the will text required page context.

## Image-level evidence

- Image 1 is the file index. It lists `John N Caton + wife Sarah E`, `Richard Burke + wife Mary J`, and `Geo. W. Lawrie (infant by Newton Vowles his gdn + next friend)` as the plaintiff groups. The indexed surnames include Caton, Lawrie, Laura, and Bussey.
- Image 3 identifies the plaintiffs as John N. Caton and Sarah Elizabeth his wife, Richard Burke and Mary J. his wife, and George W. Lawrie, an infant represented by Newton Vowles. It says Henry Bussey died about 27 March 1843 and had provided his daughter Susan Lawrie with an $800 legacy.
- Image 13 orders the executor to pay the $800 legacy in three equal shares, with one share to John N. Caton and Sarah E. his wife, one to Richard Burke and Mary J. his wife, and one to George W. Lawrie by his guardian.
- Image 27 is a copy of Henry Bussey's 17 September 1842 will. It names a daughter Susan Lawry and leaves her $800, payable by his son William two years after Bussey's death.
- Image 39 records Newton Vowles as guardian of George W. Lawrie, described as a nonresident Virginia infant under fourteen and an orphan of Newton Lawrie.

## Interpretation boundary

The primary images establish a collateral relationship: Sarah Elizabeth Caton, Mary J. Burke, and George W. Lawrie were identified in the suit as issue of Susan Lawrie/Lawry, Henry Bussey's daughter. The spelling varies in the file/index material.

This file does not name Aaron Caton, Thomas Caton, or a Caton parent. It therefore does not prove or disprove Aaron's parentage and does not connect this Lawrie branch to Aaron's direct line. Any such connection remains inference and is not added to canonical claims.

## What this settles

1. **Julia is 7 and born Massachusetts** — so born c.1843, in Massachusetts.
   The parallel pass of 10 Sep 2026 read the image as "Julia, age 1, birthplace
   appears to read Missouri" and proposed that a Missouri birth would rewrite the
   migration timeline. The proofread transcription does not support that. Julia is
   7, Mass, and attending school. **The Massachusetts interval 1842–45 stands**,
   and task T01 is unaffected.
2. **Nancy Caton exists** — 9/12, i.e. nine months old in August 1850, born Ohio.
   She is the first line of page 179b, which is why a single-page read missed her.
   The compiled claim of a daughter **Nancy Roseman Caton b. 10 November 1849** is
   an exact fit for a nine-month-old in August 1850 and is now **corroborated**,
   not refuted. The parallel pass concluded the opposite from the truncated page.
3. **Mary E. Caton, 5, born Ohio** is a genuine addition — she was not in the
   ledger at all. Born c.1845 in Ohio, which brackets the Massachusetts interval:
   Julia b. Mass c.1843, Mary E. b. Ohio c.1845. **The family was back in Ohio by
   c.1845.**
4. Aaron is 30 and **born Virginia**, independently confirming the two identifiers
   the whole search rests on.

## Migration timeline this produces

- Aug 1841 — marries Sarah Ann Gardner, Delaware Co., Ohio
- c.1843 — Julia born **Massachusetts**
- c.1845 — Mary E. born Ohio
- 1847 — William Bramwell born Ohio
- Nov 1849 — Nancy born Ohio
- Aug 1850 — enumerated, Town of Delaware, Ohio

The Massachusetts window is therefore roughly **1842–1844**, tighter than the
1842–45 given in T01.

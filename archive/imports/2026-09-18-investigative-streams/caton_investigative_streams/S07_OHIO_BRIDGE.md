# S07 — Reconstruct how Aaron reached Delaware County and test the Laurie migration connection

**Target in every stream:** Aaron Caton, reported born 16 May 1820 in Virginia, married Sarah Ann Gardner in Delaware County, Ohio, in August 1841, later a Missouri resident and tailor. His father and mother remain unidentified. These are investigation instructions, not newly proved relationships. [R1]

## Question and independence

Where was Aaron immediately before his August 1841 Delaware County marriage, who brought him into that community, and did the Fauquier Caton/Laurie network pass through the same place? This is the missing geographic bridge. It is not necessary to assume the families migrated together in order to search for whether they did.

The repo documents the 1841 marriage and later Ohio records. It reports an 1844 quitclaim involving Aaron, Sarah and Jonathan Gardner; an 1846 chattel mortgage naming James A. Asher, a Johnson, John Hoffman, E. R. Thompson and Hector Pettibone; and debt litigation. The Laurie family's Ohio-born child around 1835 is a route clue, not a proven Ohio county or evidence that Thomas accompanied them. [R2:C144-C146,C186,C189]

## 1. Complete the records already found

Start with the full 1841 marriage packet, not just the minister's return. Check for application, license, bond, consent, witness, affidavit and docket cross-reference. Aaron's stated birthday makes him 21 inAugust1841, so do not expect a parental consent as a certainty. Extract every actual residence and identity statement.

Read both sides/continuations of the 1844 quitclaim. The ledger identifies DGS 007842562 image 597 and notes that the acknowledgment/recording continuation was not yet seen. Capture execution, acknowledgment and recording dates separately, and locate the acknowledgment. Ownership of Ohio land, or a deed recorded in Ohio, does not alone prove the signers were physically there when the instrument was made. Determine the origin of Sarah's interest rather than simply assuming every quitclaim is an heir's release. [R2:C144]

Read the 1846 mortgage and associated litigation as a connected set. Determine which people were creditors, co-obligors, bail, grantors or witnesses. Complete abbreviated names from signatures and parallel entries. A title bond or debt note could lead to an earlier agreement or residence; it does not automatically establish a business partnership or kinship. [R2:C145-C146]

## 2. Work backward through named associates

Give James A. Asher, the identified Johnson, Pettibone, Hoffman and Thompson small targeted profiles: occupation, Ohio residence, earliest contact with Aaron, and any Virginia or Missouri connection. Use a fixed question—could this person have trained, employed, housed, financed or introduced Aaron?—instead of building unrelated multigeneration genealogies.

A shared debt is a stronger social connection than merely appearing near someone on a census page, but its nature still needs evidence. An 1850 neighboring tailor might be an employer or just another tradesman; locate a partnership, advertisement or contract before assigning the role. Religious affiliation and marriage location must likewise come from records, not from assuming the officiant married only members of his own congregation.

## 3. Test the 1833-1838 Laurie stop directly

Search Ohio local records for Daniel Laurie/Laury/Lawrey/Lowry, Ann, William Caton and independently identified companions, initially 1833-1838 and extending to 1841 where necessary. The priority places are Delaware County and any more specific Ohio county obtained from family documents, not an undifferentiated statewide scan.

Use deed grantor/grantee indexes, tax duplicates, court appearance dockets, town/business records, newspaper notices, and surviving church transfer/admission registers. Examine the evidence establishing Joseph Laurie's Ohio birth: a later census state entry is not an itinerary. S08 owns acquiring and interpreting the Laurie family papers; this stream uses any resulting place/date and does not duplicate that archival request.

A negative 1840 Laurie household search cannot decide where the family was in 1835, especially if they had already reached Missouri. Nor does an Ohio stop by Daniel prove that the older Thomas or Aaron accompanied him. Treat each person's residence as a separate proposition.

## 4. Use the known gaps in previous Ohio searches

C141 reports no Caton folder in the reviewed loose probate/guardianship C section. C142 reports a plaintiff-oriented Common Pleas index negative, while noting unsearched appearance dockets,1835-1841 and 1842-1847, DGS 7710150. The already-found 1846 debt records themselves show why a broad statement that Aaron had no court footprint would be wrong. [R2:C141-C146]

Do not repeat those same index searches without a new variant or documented omission. Instead, consult the cited unsearched dockets after confirming coverage and numbering, and search under a named counterparty. First probe short windows suggested by the actual debts or an earlier associate, then extend only on evidence. S02 owns Virginia apprenticeship books; Ohio arrangements belong here.

## 5. Precise tests

Strong positives would include an 1830s Ohio record naming Aaron with a Virginia parent, a apprenticeship/employment agreement identifying his previous locality, or a Laurie/Caton document explicitly connecting their movement. An explicit shared residence or joint transaction would strengthen the route hypothesis; it would not alone prove parentage.

A same-year appearance of a common Lowry surname in Delaware County is weak without age, spouse and other identity features. An alternative employer or family network could explain Aaron's Ohio arrival even if the Fauquier family remains relevant elsewhere. Preserve that independent explanation rather than treating every record as confirmation of a group migration.

If a complete bounded local-record review shows no relevant Laurie stop, report the tested source/window. Do not claim the stop was elsewhere solely from that absence. An 1843 Missouri birthplace for a child does not tell us whether Aaron made a family visit, pursued work, or temporarily relocated; do not make motive part of the evidence.

## Deliverable and boundaries

Return a date-and-place table separating stated residence, document execution place, recording place and inferred location; an associate map with roles; and a short assessment of each proposed migration link. The first useful output is the completed 1844 deed plus 1846 document chain, followed by a defined 1833-1838 local search—not another general Aaron biography.

S05 owns federal and late-life testimony; S06 owns Joseph/Dumm; S08 owns Laurie family kinship and papers. This stream owns Ohio local sources and any new locality derived from them. More distant Maryland/Pennsylvania or western-Virginia controls are activated only by a concrete Ohio record, not by the unsourced Wheeling tradition.

# S06 — Test the Aaron–Joseph relationship and acquire the Dumm family testimony

**Target in every stream:** Aaron Caton, reported born 16 May 1820 in Virginia, married Sarah Ann Gardner in Delaware County, Ohio, in August 1841, later a Missouri resident and tailor. His father and mother remain unidentified. These are investigation instructions, not newly proved relationships. [R1]

## Questions

Were Aaron and Joseph Caton brothers, half-brothers, related through marriage, or related in some other way? What did Joseph's descendants say about his parents and Virginia origins? This stream has two separate proof obligations: identify Joseph's parentage, and establish how that parentage connects to Aaron. Evidence for one is not automatically evidence for the other.

The repo's best reported kinship item is an 1898 Boonville visit notice calling Mrs. Sawtell Joseph's niece. The identification of Mrs. Sawtell as Aaron's daughter Julia is strong enough to investigate, but the ledger has no retained image or complete issue locator. The article's wording and identities still need source-level confirmation. [R2:C110]

## 1. Recover the niece notice and test the relationship

Use the existing project citation trail to locate the exact 1898 title, date, page and column. Preserve the complete item and enough adjacent context to detect continuation, mistaken attribution or an omitted spouse's name. Verify Joseph through occupation, residence history and named family; distinguish the tailor from other Missouri Joseph Catons.

Verify the Mrs. Sawtell identity independently through her marriage, residence and immediate family. A newspaper's use of niece can describe a relationship by marriage, a half relationship or informal family language; it does not itself identify which parent supplies the relationship. Check the plausible spouse-side connection before treating Joseph as Aaron's biological brother. Do not make a same-surname assumption do the work of a relationship record.

If the notice belongs to a different Mrs. Sawtell or Joseph, withdraw that support. If it is the correct pair, retain the exact kinship wording and state which alternative pathways remain possible.

## 2. Follow the existing Boulder request, without sending a duplicate

C118 records a request sent by Spencer on 12 September 2026. That is the recorded project status, not a fresh inbox check. Before another inquiry, inspect the actual reply/status with authorized email access or obtain Spencer's update. Do not claim it is still unanswered merely because the repo has not changed. [R2:C118]

The current official catalog exposes two particularly precise targets:

- Sue Caton Dumm's 1932 historical data record, call number 791-10-4d, two pages, object 56835.
- A 1932 Dumm family record credited to Clint O. Dumm, call number BHS 328-1-85, two pages, object 1568.

These are distinct catalog entries, not established independent narratives. Acquire both or ask the archivist whether they duplicate one another. This avoids treating one copied family account as two corroborating sources. The catalog's actual pages, not just subject indexing, are the needed evidence. [A18,A19]

Ask for recto/verso pages, attachments and source/provenance notes. Record reproduction conditions; the catalog carries restrictions on depositing duplicated materials elsewhere. Keep scans out of the public GitHub repo unless permitted. Source citations, analytic notes and allowed excerpts can be handled separately.

## 3. Analyze testimony before expanding to diaries

Transcribe every parent, sibling, maiden name, place and migration statement relevant to Joseph. Record who supplied the information, the compilation date, and whether a specific source or family informant is named. A recollection by Joseph's daughter could transmit valuable family knowledge while still being retrospective evidence of his birth.

When Sue and Clint say the same thing, compare language and source references before counting the statements independently. When they disagree, preserve both. Ask for diary or correspondence material only in the years or subjects suggested by the two-page records, rather than requesting the entire photographic/diary collection. Look for named visits, deaths, family letters and explicit statements about Aaron, not generic Dumm ancestry.

## 4. Reconcile Joseph's birthplace at the document level

Existing research alternates between a claimed Fairfax reading and an obituary printing a garbled county rendered Forkware. The repo itself records disagreement about whether the alleged Brunswick notice was ever inspected. Treat neither a repaired spelling nor a recalled quotation as a settled birthplace. [R2:C076,C077,C120]

Recover the actual issues underlying each claim, transcribe the printed county without normalization, and separate death date from issue date. Compare whether notices are reprints or independent accounts. A matching reprint is not new testimony. The inference Fauquier or Fairfax should be justified separately and should guide relative priority rather than act as an exclusion filter.

If family papers name Fairfax, investigate that statement on its own merits; do not force it to match the current Fauquier hypothesis. If they name another locality, feed it directly to S02/S07/S08.

## 5. Narrow corroboration and explicit limits

Use descendant marriage/death records, obituaries and identified family Bibles to corroborate a newly named grandparent or sibling. Do not reconstruct every Joseph descendant simply to accumulate the surname. Contemporary family correspondence naming both Aaron and Joseph is particularly valuable.

Joseph's 1910 Saline County probate remains parked at Spencer's instruction. Do not reopen it in this plan without authorization. Nor should anyone assume that a man with descendants must have left a document naming siblings: the useful content of a probate file depends on the actual estate and relationships at issue.

## Deliverable and discriminating outcomes

Return two separate assessments: (1) exactly how Joseph is connected to Aaron, and (2) what is established about Joseph's parents and origins. Include a source-dependence map for the notices and 1932 records. An explicit statement that Joseph and Aaron had the same parents would be major evidence, not a reason to stop checking identities. Evidence of half-brotherhood would prove only the shared parent's connection. Evidence of unclehood by marriage would require rewriting the existing brother inference.

The first pass should produce the two-page records and the niece notice, or exact pending requests for those specific items. Their absence is an acquisition problem, not a reason to substitute more unsourced online trees.

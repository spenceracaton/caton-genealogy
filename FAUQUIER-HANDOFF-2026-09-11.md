## 1. "Wheeling" is unsourced (C075) — grade A

Every record that states Aaron's birthplace says only **"Virginia"**:
- 1850 census, Delaware Co., OH (C006)
- 1870 census, Boonville (C041)
- Julia's 1916 death certificate — father's birthplace "Va" (C037, C040)

**No record says Wheeling.** The archived ledger carries it from pass 1 as "later
sources report Wheeling" and never names a source. William B. Caton's 1901 biography
does not mention it. And the same ledger records that **the 1820 Ohio County census
contains no Caton household at all** — checked early, written down, never acted on.

Supporting Caton presence in Ohio Co. is thin and indirect: Elizabeth Caton m. Gosney
there 1818 (C012, a marriage, not a household); Thomas Caton there by 1830 (C073) and
1840 (C090) but **not** 1820; Harrison boarding there 1850 (C011); and **no Caton in
any published Wheeling county history** — *History of Greater Wheeling* (2 vols) and
*History of Belmont and Jefferson Counties* full-text searched, zero hits (C066).

Wheeling may still be right. It must be carried as **C**, and the search must not be
anchored to it. Virginia in 1820 ran from Princess Anne County to the Ohio River.

## 2. Joseph Caton's obituary — found, and it names a county (C076, C077)

**Marshall Republican (Marshall, Saline Co., MO), 27 May 1910, p.1, "DEATHS":**
> "JOSEPH CATON, one of Marshall's oldest and highly respected citizens, departed for
> the better world Saturday morning, May 21 at the advanced age of nearly 87 years.
> Mr. Caton was born in **Forkware county, Va., on July 24, 1823**. As a young man he
> located at Brunswick, Mo., and 36 years ago came to Marshall and until a few years
> ago was active in the tailoring business… a staunch Christian and active member of
> the M. E. Church, South… survived by three sons and two daughters H. D. Caton of
> Leavenworth; O. O. Caton of Salisbury; J. E. Caton, Kansas; Mrs. Sue Dumm, Boulder,
> Colo.; Mrs. H. Schanz, Marshall. His remains were taken to Brunswick… and laid to
> rest beside his wife."
https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn89066240/1910-05-27/ed-1/?sp=1

**Verified against the page image:** "Forkware county, Va." is what the newspaper
printed — the compositor's error, not OCR. Two birthday notices in the same paper (26
Jul 1907, "84th"; 30 Jul 1909, "86th") independently fix the birth at **late July 1823**.
Chariton Courier, 3 Jun 1910: "Joseph Caton, 87 years old, a native of Virginia."

**"Forkware" → FAUQUIER.** The letter-groups map onto a compositor misreading
handwriting: *Fau→For, q→k, u→w, ier→are*. Fauquier is northern Piedmont, ~200 miles
east of Wheeling. This reading was then **independently corroborated** by §3: a Caton
household really was in Fauquier in 1820. Grade B. Fairfax is the alternative that
cannot be formally excluded (§7).

No Missouri death certificate exists for Joseph despite his dying after registration
began — searched Caton/Caten/Catron 1910–11, three records statewide, none him (C078).

## 3. The Fauquier Caton family, 1820–1835 — grade A throughout

**1820 census, Fauquier Co.** (C080) — one Caton household in the county:
**Thos. Caton** — males 2 under 10, 1 aged 10–16, 1 aged 16–18, **1 aged 45+**;
females 1 under 10, 1 aged 26–45. *Aaron, b. 16 May 1820, was three months old on
census day and fits "under 10."* Joseph, b. 1823, correctly does not appear.

**1830 census, Fauquier Co.** (C082) — FamilySearch confirms exactly three:
- **Thomas Caton Sen.** — 1 male 60–70; females 2 under 5, one 10–15, one 15–20, one 40–50
- **Thomas Caton Jun.** — 1 male 20–30, 1 male under 5; 1 female 20–30
- **William Caton** — 1 male 20–30; 1 female under 5, 1 female 20–30

**Marriage papers** (C092, ChatGPT's own find — Fauquier Marriage Bonds & Returns 4 and 5):
- William Caton m. Ann F. Lawrey; bond 12 Jan 1829; **Thomas Caton Jr.'s consent, 6 Dec
  1828: "my son William Caton."**
- Daniel Laury m. Ann Caton; bond 27 Mar 1835; Thomas Caton Jr. co-obligor; Ann is
  **"Daughter of Thomas Caton senr."**; Thomas Sr.'s permission: "my Daughter."

Against the 1830 census — where the only Caton man over 30 is aged 60–70 — the Jr./Sr.
labels shifted between 1828 and 1835 when an older Thomas died. **One elder Thomas
(b. 1760s) is father of William, Thomas, and Ann.** His 1820 wife (26–45) is 40–50 in
1830; his 1820 sons aged 16–18 and 10–16 are the two 20–30 heads of 1830.

**Road order, 4 Dec 1830** (C087, ChatGPT): Daniel Lawry, Newton Lawry, Thomas Caton,
Wm Caton and "Tho Caton sen." assigned together as road hands.

**Chancery 1820-011** (C093): a **Moses Caton**, known locally since c.1805 — another
Fauquier Caton adult, unplaced.

**LVA chancery, all 107 Virginia localities swept for Caton** (C084): 69 causes; Northern
Virginia holds 46 — **Fairfax 20, Arlington 17**, Fauquier 3, Prince William 2. Fauquier
is not an isolated anomaly; it sits inside a dense Northern Virginia Caton population.

## 4. The Fauquier cluster moved intact to Cooper County, Missouri (C096–C098) — grade A

**1850 census, Cooper Co., MO, transcription p.23, verified against the page image:**
four consecutive Virginia-born households —

| hh | head | | notable |
|---|---|---|---|
| 352 | James R. Payne, 38, Va | | a James Payne was bondsman on William's 1829 marriage |
| 353 | Catharine Laurie, 63, Va | | Daniel's mother; with Isa Payne and Abeam **Newton** |
| 354 | **Daniel Laurie, 44, Va** | **Ann, 33, Va** | children: Joseph 15 **b. Ohio**, then Missouri-born from c.1838 |
| 357 | **William Caton, 38, Va** | *no wife* | John W. 18 **b. Va**, **Aaron F. 8 b. Mo**, Sarah Ann 6, Elizabeth 4, Wingfield 2 |

**Ann Laurie, 33, is Ann Caton, daughter of Thomas Caton Sr.** — the 1835 bride, the
girl aged 15–20 in Thomas Sr.'s 1830 household. Her brother William is two doors down.
The Lauries' son born in Ohio c.1835 shows the route: **Fauquier → Ohio c.1835 → Cooper
Co. by c.1838.**

**Consequences:**
- The William Caton of Cooper 1850 (b. Va) = the Wm. Caton of Bates Co. 1860 (b. "Md",
  C055) — same son Aaron, ages align; the birth state conflicts and one census is wrong.
- **Aaron F. Caton** — walled off as unrelated since pass 1 — is **William's son, b. 1842**.
  If our Aaron is the elder Thomas's son, Aaron F. is his **nephew, named for him** the
  year our Aaron reached Missouri.
- C088's "Elizabeth Lowrey (1804–1883), wife of Thomas William Caton" is **not** William's
  wife — he is a widower in 1850. That part of the 1999 descendant account is wrong.
- Cooper Co. also holds Delaware-born John Caton (C095) and Kentucky-born Thomas Caton,
  father of Nimrod (C096) — three distinct Caton origins in one county.

## 5. Joseph as Aaron's brother — now B (C062, C110)

Five shared attributes (birth state, tailoring, ages three years apart, Ohio-born
wives, Missouri in the 1840s) put this at C. **The 1898 Boonville article — Joseph "the
guest of his niece, Mrs. Sawtell"** — is a contemporary statement of kinship. Mrs.
Sawtell is Julia A. (Caton) Sawtell, Aaron's daughter, by convergence: she is the Mrs.
Sawtell of Boonville (C041, C111); Aaron lived in her household in 1870; Henry's 1880
obituary calls her his sister (C109). If Joseph is Julia's uncle and a Virginia-born
Caton three years Aaron's junior, he is Aaron's brother. **B, not A** — a newspaper's
report, not a document naming parents.

The two threads reached this independently by different routes. Note also: the WikiTree
GEDCOM naming "Thomas Caton" as Aaron's father is called **124-DeCoursey.ged**, and
Joseph's eldest son was **DeCourcey Caton** (C112, C113). If that tree came down Joseph's
line, the Thomas link is Joseph's family's own tradition. Still C.

## 6. The fork (C107) — the honest state of the question

**Story A — Fauquier.** Aaron is a son of the elder Thomas; brother of Joseph, William,
Thomas Jr., Ann. The family's route Fauquier → Ohio c.1835 → Cooper Co. tracks every
documented move of Aaron's life; he died in Cooper Co. beside them (C108).
*Cannot explain:* why Aaron was in **Delaware Co., Ohio** — no Fauquier household was
ever there (C102), and the 1830 Fauquier census has no boy of his age (C082).

**Story B — Maryland–Pennsylvania / Wheeling.** Thomas Caton in Ohio Co., VA in 1830
(male 5–10 present — Aaron turned ten sixteen days before census day, C073) and 1840
(that boy gone, aged 15–20, C090). The only two Catons in Delaware Co. in 1840 are
**Jesse Katon b. 1807 Maryland** and **James Cayton b. 1804 Pennsylvania** (C100–C106) —
the Maryland Kitten → Washington Co. PA line — kin who would explain why a 20-year-old
came there.
*Cannot explain:* Joseph, born Fauquier 1823; or Aaron dying beside the Fauquier cluster.

**Both failed their direct 1840 test.** No male 20–29 in the Wheeling household; no
Fauquier household in Delaware Co. **In 1840 Aaron was a tally mark in some master's
household** — apprenticed, as a tailor would be — and the 1840 census names only heads.

**Reconciliation, unexamined:** the Fauquier Catons may themselves be a Maryland branch,
making the two streams one family.

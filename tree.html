England to Pennsylvania

the ancestry of a family Aaron has not been shown to belong to

In this family Caton is a late Americanisation of Kitton / Kitten, not the reverse. The English target is Kitton — a rare Fenland surname, some 63 bearers in the whole of the 1881 census. "Caton of Lancashire" is an artefact of the modern spelling.

Theophilus Kitton

Baptised 4 May 1660 — "Theophilus ye sonne of widdow Kitton (Posthumus)". Born a month after his father's burial. He then stops appearing in England entirely: no burial 1600–1800, no marriage 1600–1750, anywhere indexed.

Why it fits: a pauper orphan of a labourer, aged 15 in 1675 — the textbook indentured-servant profile.
Against: at national level Kitton is a Norfolk name, which weakens the Lincolnshire reading. The only candidate found so far — not "the family came from Spalding".

Join 4 does not hold.

Nothing connects the Washington County Catons to the Wheeling Catons, and nothing connects either to Aaron. Six research passes have not moved it.

Aaron was born 16 May 1820, reportedly at Wheeling, Virginia. He next appears at his own marriage in Delaware County, Ohio in August 1841, aged 21. The twenty-one years in between are empty, and his parents are unknown. Everything above this band is a hypothesis about the ancestry of a family he has not been shown to belong to.

1820
A

Aaron Caton

Sarah Ann Gardner, b. c.1824 Ohio — married 8 August 1841, Delaware Co., Ohio

Born 16 May 1820, reportedly Wheeling, Virginia. Tailor to 1860, labourer by 1870. Private, Co. E, 2nd Missouri Volunteer Cavalry — Merrill's Horse — enlisted at Chillicothe, Missouri on 15 August 1861, reenlisted 5 January 1864, mustered out at Nashville 19 September 1865. Last seen alive 6 June 1870 in Boonville, lodging in his married daughter's household, no wife present.

Birthplace Virginia confirmed four times over: 1850, 1860 and 1870 censuses, and by his daughter's 1916 death certificate — the first statement from a family informant rather than an enumerator.
Ages drift; keep 1820. The service card's 44 and the 1870 census's 55 are third-party outliers.
Death: between June 1870 and June 1880. The widely repeated 11 Jan 1878 Boonville date is consistent with that bracket but still unproven.
Sarah Ann died before 1910, or outside Missouri — a full sweep of the 1910–75 Missouri death certificates returns no candidate.

Not this family

same surname, separately documented, kept apart on purpose

Aaron was in Missouri by December 1843 — twenty years before any other Missouri record. The 1859–79 residence is a return, not a first migration, which means every separation below was reasoned against a prior that has since changed. That merges nothing. It means the separations must be re-argued rather than assumed.

  • Aaron F. Caton — b. c.1836 Missouri, Otterville, Cooper Co., wife Nancy, eight children, d. 1886. Sixteen years younger than ours, wrong state. The Aaron Caton × Nancy Johnson marriage of 12 Oct 1869 is probably his, not ours.
  • Three Missouri men named Harrison Caton — Wheeling b. c.1836 Va; Bates Co. b. c.1825 Ohio (ruled out); Gentry Co., raising a family by 1883. Do not match on forename.
  • Andrew Caton / New York line — Emma J. Caton of Delaware Co., Ohio is Andrew's daughter, not Aaron's.
  • Thos. H. Caton, b. Kentucky, Roche Twp., Boone Co., MO 1860 — a separate Kentucky–Missouri line in the same county.
  • Nimrod Caton × Nancy Starke, and Joseph T. Caton — both Cooper County, both distinct.
  • Jesse Caton, Warren–Linn Co., Missouri — older, separate family.
  • George Wesley Caton of Muskingum Co. is not George W. Caton, the Missouri tailor.
  • William Caton, transported 1674 — a different immigrant recorded in the same Liber 18.

What would change the shape of this tree

in order of leverage

The first item is not a research task. It is a purchase.

  1. Y-DNA — FTDNA Y-37, joined to the Caton surname project

    Tests join 4 directly: whether Aaron's male line belongs to this family at all. A negative deletes the entire England-to-Pennsylvania section from your ancestry in one result, and redirects sections' worth of work. Highest-value action in the file, and still not started.

  2. Missouri, 1842–1845

    Julia was born there in December 1843. Where the family was in those years is the newest open question and the one that reframes everything downstream.

  3. The John Boring × Eliza Ann marriage record

    One document. If her maiden name is Caton, Harrison's parents become findable — in the right surname, county and generation to bear on Aaron.

  4. Harrison Caton in the 1900 Gentry County census

    The 1900 schedule gives birth month and year and both parents' birthplaces for the head. If that Harrison proves to be Aaron's kin, this is the nearest the file has come to the generation above him. Missouri's 1890 census burned; 1880 is the wrong instrument, since the family arrived after it.

  5. Aaron's full CMSR jacket at NARA, and his pension file

    The index card's Remarks field is blank. The consent paper for William's underage enlistment, if it exists, is in the jacket — as is whatever the pension file says about Sarah Ann.

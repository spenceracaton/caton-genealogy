# John Caton of Cooper County, Missouri — collateral analysis

> original schedule image are now retained, but the row marks could not be
> independently aligned to John Caton. The 1840 age-band sentence below is a
> prior claim, not an image-verified transcription. Use
> `CATON_CENSUS_LEDGER.md` record `US-1840-MO-COOPER-JOHN-01`; do not use the
> old tally or derived county-wide age conclusion as settled evidence until
> manual row review. The 1850 Delaware-born John remains a separate record;
> continuity is not proved.

## Question

Could the John Caton who headed a Cooper County household in 1840 be Aaron
Caton's father or otherwise supply a documented parentage lead?

## Primary and near-primary records

- **1840 U.S. census, Cooper County, Missouri, p. 141.**  John Caton's
  household has one male aged 40–49, one male under 5, one male 5–9, two
  females under 5, one female 10–14, and one female 20–29.  This is the
  previously preserved observation in C057.
- **1850 U.S. census, Cooper County, Missouri, District 23, p. 268,
  household 902, enumerated 9 October 1850.**  John Caton, 53, farmer,
  real estate $2,000, born Delaware; with Columbus M., 19, farm hand, born
  Missouri; Benjamin, 13, Missouri; Elizabeth, 11, Missouri; and Ophelia,
  9, Missouri.  The public transcription is available at
  <https://cooper.mogenweb.org/Census/1850_Cooper_Census.pdf>.
- **1860 slave schedule, Cooper County.**  A John Caton is listed with four
  enslaved people in the section covering Black Water, Lebanon, Pilot Grove,
  and Saline townships.  This identifies an owner of that name, not a proven
  match to the 1850 household.

The 1840 and 1850 enumerations are plausibly the same household: the 1840
male under 5 and male 5–9 fit Columbus (about 1831) and Benjamin (about
1837), while the young girls fit Elizabeth and Ophelia.  This is an
identification inference, not a parent-child connection to Aaron.

## Locality leads, not proof of identity

- A historical marker transcription lists **John Caton** among Pilot Grove's
  early landowners and settlers in **1831**.  It does not provide a deed
  citation or identify his family:
  <https://www.waymarking.com/waymarks/WM3JCJ_Pilot_Grove_1873>.
- The 1876 *History of Cooper County* refers to a location long known as the
  **"John Caton place"** near Clifton.  The passage does not date Caton's
  ownership or identify him.  It therefore cannot establish that this is the
  1850 Delaware-born farmer.

## Court-index check

The Missouri State Archives provides digitized, handwritten indexes to the
Cooper County Court records.  I reviewed the C-surname pages of both indexes
for **Caton**, **Caten**, **Catton**, and **Cayton**.

- 1819–1845 index: no entry under those forms.  The pages do include separate
  Catron/Cater-style names, which were not merged with Caton.
- 1845–1866 index: no entry under those forms.  Again, similarly spelled
  entries were kept separate.

This is a bounded index result only.  It does **not** prove that John left no
estate, owned no land, or was unrelated to Aaron; it means this public county
court index did not supply the hoped-for probate/guardianship shortcut.

## State land-sales index check

The Missouri State Archives Land Records database associates **John Caton**
with *Abstract of U.S. Land Sales*, volume 3, page 066 (record 274512).  The
linked scan was inspected and preserved.  It is instead a Township School
Lands patent in Morgan County dated 28 February 1894 to **James S. Franklin
and Robert S. Harvey**.  It does not name John Caton.  The online aggregate
name index therefore cannot be used as evidence of a John Caton land purchase
without a corrected archival image or a separate record.

## Conclusion

John Caton was a Delaware-born Missouri farmer with a documented Missouri-born
household, rather than a demonstrated Fauquier County man.  No record found
connects him to Aaron Caton, Aaron's Virginia birth, or the Fauquier Catons.
Keep him as an unmerged collateral possibility only.  The direct parentage
path remains the Fauquier 1817–23 minute and guardian records, where a death,
guardianship, or migration order could name Aaron.

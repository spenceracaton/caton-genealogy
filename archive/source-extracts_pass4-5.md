## 3. Act of Assembly, 1727, ch. 19 — Theophilus Kitton, planter, and his son Edward

Source: *Archives of Maryland*, Vol. 38, p. 408 (Assembly Proceedings 1694–1728),
<https://msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/speccol/sc2900/sc2908/000001/000038/html/am38--408.html>.
Cross-references: AoM 36:38, 36:55, 36:71, 36:75, index 36:604; Bacon's Laws AoM 75:724.

    An Act for the Makeing Good and Valid in Law a Certain Indenture or Deed of
    Bargain and Sale from John Gray of Ann Arundell County to Theophilus Kitton
    late of Baltemore County Planter.

    Whereas Edward Kitton by his humble Petition to this Present Generall Assembly
    hath set forth that the said John Gray did bargain & sell unto Theophilus Kitton
    father to the said Edward Kitton by his deed of bargaine and Sale bearing date
    the Sixteenth day of November 1699 duly made Executed and Acknowledged for the
    Consideration therein Mencond a Certain Tract of Land Called Grays Luck Contg
    three hundred & Sixty one Acres Lying then in Baltemore County but now in Ann
    Arundell County ... the deed afd hath never been Recorded ... it is not in his
    Power to gett any Confirmation of his title to the said Land from the said John
    Gray or his heirs there being none of them as he the said Edward Kitton Knows of
    to Apply to.
    ... Provided that the said Edwd Kitton his heirs or Assignes shall procure the
    same deed to be Recorded in the Land Records of Ann Arundell County within Six
    Months after the End of this Present Sessions of Assembly ...

Established at A level by this act:
- Theophilus Kitton, planter, of Baltimore County, deceased by 1727.
- He bought "Grays Luck", 361 acres, from John Gray by deed of 16 Nov 1699.
- Edward Kitton is his son.

## 7. THE 1675 HEADRIGHT — Maryland Land Office, Patent Record Liber 18, folio 329

**Retrieved and read in full.** Maryland State Archives, LAND OFFICE (Patent Record),
MSA SM2-21 / SR 7359, "18, transcript of MM", 1672–1675. Free public multipage PDF:
<http://msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/stagserm/sm1/sm2/000000/000021/pdf/mdsa_sm2_21.pdf>
(198 MB, 246 images). Transcript page 329 = PDF page 212, right-hand page.
Original manuscript reference in the left margin: **Lib: MM (852)**.
Images saved: `MDPatents_Liber18_f329_full.jpg`, `MDPatents_Liber18_f329_headright_detail.jpg`.

Transcription (expansions in brackets):

    June 18th 1675
    Came Michael Stuppell and Stephen Barnerd of London, marchants, and proved
    their Rights to one thousand seven hundred and fifty acres of Land for
    Transporting Henry Southern, Jeremiah Simpson, Jo:n Vickers, Tho: Holder,
    W:m Rosbridge, THEOPHILUS KITTON, Richard Dicon, Jo:n Wood, W:m Peirce,
    Tho: Dunston, Ste:n Yates, Edward Carver, Tho: Constantine, John David,
    Joseph Cupping, William Griffith, Tho: Busfeild, Ralph Hammerslee,
    W:m Whettingam, W:m Hornbee, Jo:n Fromelly, W:m Hocman, James Bybee,
    Jo:n Buther, Joseph Smith, Jo:n Whelbes, Tho: Titton, Clodius Bennett,
    Geo: Herrington, John Tomson, Mary Joanes, Elizabeth Pointer, Ann Wilson,
    Ann Joanes & Judith Balding into this province to Inhabite.

    Eodem die — Warrant then Granted to Michael Stuppell & Stephen Bernerd for
    one Thousand Seven hundred and fifty acres of Land due to them for
    Transporting the above named Thirty five persons into this province to Inhabite.

    [margin] Stuppell & Bernerd Rts &c. 1750 acres. fees paid.

**What this settles**
- Exact date of the claim: **18 June 1675**.
- Theophilus Kitton was one of **35 persons** transported — the compiled "came to
  America 1675 w/34 other people" is confirmed from the record itself.
- The claimants were **merchants of LONDON**, not Bristol. The shipment was
  London-fitted.
- The spelling in the earliest American record of the man is **KITTON**.

**What this rules out**
- The Bristol servant registers (Coldham, *Bristol Registers of Servants Sent to
  Foreign Plantations 1654–1686*) are the wrong source. Drop that line.
- The surviving London "Register of Servants sent to Foreign Plantations" begins
  1682 and so cannot cover this voyage.

**What it does not settle**
The other 34 names are regionally mixed — Welsh (John David, William Griffith),
northern English (Busfield, Hornby, Hammersley), East Anglian (Balding) — which is
normal for a London-assembled shipment and does not localise anyone. Note
"Tho: Titton" in the same list; possibly a distinct family, possibly a clerk's
slip for Kitton. Unresolved.

Searches for the merchants Michael Stuppell and Stephen Barnard in TNA Discovery
returned nothing for either man (Stuppell is otherwise a Kent surname). Tracing
them needs London port books or Chancery, not available here.

## 8. THE 1790 CENSUS — Theophilus Kitten and Thomas Kitten, Washington County, PA

        Frances Swelling
        Tho:s Lackey
        Joseph Laycock
        Elisha Laycock ...

Columns are the 1790 standard: free white males 16+ including head / free white
males under 16 / free white females including heads / all other free persons /
slaves / total.

Images saved: `PA1790_WashingtonCo_p192_Kitten_page.jpg` (whole page),
`PA1790_WashingtonCo_p192_Kitten_names.jpg` (names, high resolution),
`PA1790_WashingtonCo_p192_Kitten_counts.jpg` (the two rows with their tallies).

**Completeness statement.** Across the whole of Washington County in 1790 there are
**exactly two households spelled KITTEN and none spelled CATON.** Two look-alikes
were checked at full resolution and eliminated: page 202 "Tho:s **Hutton**" (not
Kitton) and page 203 "William **Canson**" (not Caton). Other unrelated names in the
county include Knox, Knotts, Kidd, Cotton and Seaton; none is this family.

**What this settles**
- The Kitten→Caton name change in the Pennsylvania branch is now documented from
  the record, not from a forum claim. The men recorded as **Kitten** in 1790 are the
  men recorded as **Caton** in Washington County deeds and probate in the 1790s.
- Thomas and Theophilus were **immediate neighbours**, which is what you expect of
  brothers and is consistent with Michael Barr's argument that Thomas Caton
  (husband of Susannah Plummer) and Theophilus Caton were brothers.
- Thomas was alive in 1790 and dead by early 1795, matching the Washington County
  record of Susanna as his widow.

## 9. English records — further searching, and the limits reached

**FreeREG, "family members" option enabled, all counties.** After his 1660 baptism,
**Theophilus Kitton appears in no English parish record at all**: no marriage
1600–1750, no burial 1600–1800, and he never appears as the father in any baptism
1660–1740. He is present once, at his own christening, and then gone.

**The National Archives Discovery** (federated national catalogue, covering TNA at
Kew plus county record offices). Searches run 10 Sep 2026:
- `"Theophilus Kitton" OR "Theophilus Kitten"` — **0 results**.
- `Kitton` — 134 results. Distribution by repository: The National Archives 60,
  **Norfolk Record Office 24**, The London Archives 5, Devon 2, and single hits
  elsewhere. Probate and service records name Kittons of Bagthorpe, Fakenham,
  Little Hockham, Great Ringstead, Norwich, Worstead and Salhouse — all **Norfolk**.
- `Kitton` at **Lincolnshire Archives** — 1 result only (Spalding Sewers accounts,
  ref. Spalding Sewers/490/4/a).
- Useful corroboration that the two spellings are one name:
  **WO 121/186/170 — "WILLIAM KITTEN alias WILLIAM KITTON, Born NORWICH, Norfolk."**

**Interpretation.** At national-catalogue level the surname's centre of gravity is
Norfolk, not Lincolnshire. FreeREG's 17th-century concentration at Spalding is
partly an artefact of that parish being thoroughly transcribed while most are not.
The Spalding candidate therefore rests on the **forename**, not on surname
geography, and an untranscribed Norfolk Theophilus Kitton cannot presently be
excluded. Testing that requires the paywalled Norfolk baptism indexes
(FindMyPast / Ancestry / FamilySearch) or the Norfolk Record Office.

## 12. AARON CATON'S MISSOURI SERVICE CARD — RETRIEVED (A level)

Missouri State Archives, Soldiers' Records: War of 1812 – World War I, database
entry S73935; service card image at
<https://www.sos.mo.gov/Images/Archives/Military/s00824/s00824_0961.pdf>
(free; direct download requires a browser Referer header).
Saved as `MO_ServiceCard_AaronCaton.pdf` / `..._2ndMOCav_CoE.jpg`. Card no. 2199.

    2 Reg't Cav. Vols.
    Caton, Aaron                Age 44
    Rank Pvt.                   Co. E
    Captain Norville
    Enlisted   Aug. 15, 1861
    Where      Chillicothe, Mo.
    Mustered in Sept. 3, 1861
    Where      Benton Bks, Mo.
    Remarks    Reenlisted Jan. 5, 1864
               [ink annotation] RESIDENCE, CHILLICOTHE, Mo.
    Mustered out Sept. 19, 1865
    Where      Nashville, Tenn.
    Form No. 212, A.G.O.

**What this changes**
1. **Enlistment was 15 August 1861, not 7 September 1861.** The handoff's 7 Sep
   date (from Erwin) is neither the enlistment nor the muster-in (3 Sep) on this card.
2. **Age given as 44.** A birth of 16 May 1820 would make him 41 in August 1861.
   The 1850 census (age 30 in August 1850) supports c.1820. The card supports
   c.1817. One of the two is wrong; flag it, do not silently pick.
3. **Aaron survived the war.** He re-enlisted as a veteran volunteer on
   5 January 1864 and was mustered out on 19 September 1865 at Nashville — four
   years' service. The handoff treated his fate after enlistment as open; his
   post-war life (1865 onward) is now a defined research window.
4. **Residence of record: Chillicothe** — Livingston County, not merely the place
   he happened to enlist. The 1860 search should lead with Livingston County.
   Against that, William was at Sturgeon, Boone County, in October 1862, so the
   family may have moved between 1860 and 1862. Search both.
5. Company E was commanded by **Captain Norville** — a FAN-club name.

## 14. WILLIAM BRAMWELL CATON'S 1901 BIOGRAPHY — RETRIEVED (B level)

D. A. Millington & E. P. Greer, *History of Cowley County, Kansas* (Winfield:
The Winfield Courier, 1901), pp. 118–119, plus the "Caton's Marble Works" notice.
Internet Archive item `historyofcowleyc00mill`, open access. Full extract saved as
`1901_CowleyCo_KS_WBCaton_biography.txt`.

Key statements (OCR as printed):

> "Mr. Caton is the senior member of the Caton Marble Works ... He was horn [born]
> in Delaware county, Ohio. October 24, 1847. When quite young, he with his parents
> moved to the state of Missouri, where he was one of the foremost to respond to the
> call of his country in 1861; first enlisting in **Capt. Reed's company, a local
> organization**, and later as **bugler** in Co E. 2nd Mo Vol. Cavalry, known
> generally as 'Merrill's Horse,' in which he served until October 1865 when he was
> mustered out at Nashville, Tenn., reaching home just in time to celebrate his
> eighteenth birthday. The following spring he secured a position as apprentice in
> the **marble works of Boonville, Mo.** In 1869, he was married to **Miss Julia
> Blankenmeister of Boonville, Mo.** Five children were born to them, Harry A. the
> eldest, being junior partner of the firm. In 1879, he with his family joined the
> caravan headed for Southern Kansas, landing one beautiful October day in Winfield
> ... resides at 1702 Millington street."

And of his wife:

> "Mrs. W. B. Caton is a native of **St. Louis, Mo.**, and was educated and married
> in that state. She is a graduate of the Missouri Female College, and came to
> Winfield, Kansas, with her husband in the fall of 1879 ... mother of five children,
> the eldest thirty-one the youngest three ... In 1880 she was chosen as a teacher in
> the public schools ... In 1888, she was nominated and elected to the office of
> **superintendent of public instruction**."

**New facts**
- **William Bramwell Caton born 24 October 1847, Delaware County, Ohio** — an exact
  date, consistent with his age of 2 in the August 1850 census.
- He served first in **Capt. Reed's local company** before the 2nd Cavalry, and was
  a **bugler**.
- **Spring 1866 to 1879 the family's centre was Boonville, Cooper County, Missouri**,
  where he apprenticed in the marble trade and married in 1869.
- Wife: **Julia Blankenmeister**, of Boonville, native of St. Louis.
- Arrived Winfield **October 1879**.
- Eldest child aged 31 in 1901 → **Harry A. Caton b. c.1870**.

**Correction to section 19C.** The county officer list in the same book records
"**Julia B. Caton, superintendent**, 1889 to 1891". That is **William's wife Julia
Blankenmeister Caton**, elected in 1888 — not a daughter Julia and not Aaron's
Massachusetts-born daughter Julia. Do not build anything on that name.

**Confirmation of section 19C's reasoning.** Harry A. Caton was born c.1870, so he
was about ten in 1880 and cannot be the adult tailor "Harry Caton" boarding at the
Baker Hotel in the 1880 Winfield directory. Further, the family reached Winfield
only in October 1879. The Eugene DeCourcey "Harry" Caton hypothesis therefore
survives intact and remains unproven.

**Significant reframing.** The direct line was resident in **Cooper County,
Missouri, from 1866 to 1879** — the same county as George W. Caton the Boonville
tailor, the William/Aaron F. Caton household, and (per the 1898 biography) Joseph
Caton in later life. Section 2's instruction not to *merge identities* remains
correct. But the assumption behind it — that the Cooper County Catons are a
separate, irrelevant cluster — is no longer safe. The direct line lived among them
for thirteen years.

**Consequence for section 19K.** The quarantined record of an **Aaron Caton marrying
Nancy Johnson in Cooper County on 12 October 1869** should be downgraded from
"probably a different man" to **"plausibly the direct-line Aaron, unproven"**.
Aaron was alive from September 1865; his son was living and marrying in Boonville
in 1869; the marriage is in the right county in the right year. What is missing is
evidence about Sarah Ann (Gardner) Caton's death. That is now a priority.

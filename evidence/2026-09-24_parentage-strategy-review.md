# Aaron Caton parentage: evidence and research priorities

Review date: 24 September 2026. This is an analytical review of the current repository, not a new genealogical discovery or a fresh transcription of manuscript images. Canonical claims and task states are unchanged. Latest substantive local synthesis reviewed: 18 September.

## Assessment

Aaron's parents remain unknown. The Fauquier–Prince William family is the leading research hypothesis, but the evidence connects a regional and occupational network more securely than it connects Aaron to a particular father. Work backward from Aaron and forward from a carefully identified candidate; require the two trails to meet in evidence.

The September 18 census rereads resolve the earlier image-access problem (C208/C209). They do not identify the unnamed children. The young Thomas in 1830 is 20–30; William's consenting father is called Thomas Jr. in a later typescript (C092). This remains an identity problem, not a proved third generation.

## Where the reasoning exceeds the evidence

- **C099 overstates the conclusion.** Its assertions that Aaron is part of this family and that finding the cluster in the same Ohio county would close the case are not warranted. County-level co-location would strengthen association, not identify a parent. Its `kind: fact` and `claim_type: observation` also obscure that its central proposition is a hypothesis.
- **C110 is an unverified quotation in the present evidence chain.** The 1898 niece notice lacks a retained newspaper title, exact date, page, and image. If recovered, it still requires identification of Mrs. Sawtell and the route of kinship. Joseph could be a full brother, half-brother, or uncle by marriage. Joseph's parents cannot automatically be assigned to Aaron.
- **Apprenticeship is a useful test, not an established explanation.** Unnamed boys absent from surname-headed households are not identified missing sons. Shared occupation and childhood household absence have alternative explanations. A binding may identify a parent, mother, master, or no parent; its exact wording decides. Do not require a poverty finding from personal-property taxes before searching.
- **C205 presents an age conflict.** The Boonville derivative gives Thomas age 77 in 1850, approximately born 1772–73. That conflicts with the proposed Fauquier man's 1810 age 45+ and 1830 age 60–70. It fits the broad 1820 age 45+ category only. Verify the original before reconciliation.
- **Suffixes do not establish fixed identities or relationships.** “Senior” and “junior” in different records need independent linking. The 1846 debt entry (C206) neither says father and son nor proves residence in Cooper County in 1846. Its opening claim language is stronger than its own qualifications.
- **Negative searches remain narrower than some summaries.** One tithable in a named tax entry is not proof Aaron was absent from the county. No Caton file in a reviewed probate segment is not proof no Caton died there. A missing adult man in a census does not rule out grandchildren whose father was deceased or elsewhere.
- **A candidate father's marriage is not yet Aaron's mother's identification.** Both the father–Aaron link and the relevant wife–Aaron link need evidence. The second-marriage premise is itself unresolved (C207).

## Ranked pathways

### 1. Aaron's own federal records: highest value for directing the entire search

Retrieve the complete compiled military service record for Aaron, Company E, 2nd Missouri Cavalry, especially enlistment, descriptive, and reenlistment papers. The state service card already retained is not the complete federal file. A county or town of birth, prior residence, or named prewar acquaintance could redirect every county search.

Resolve pension application 233783 through the T289 organization index and underlying service evidence. C184 and T24 disagree about the card for “Aaron Caten,” Company C, 3rd Missouri Cavalry. Neither the spelling/unit mismatch alone nor a roster non-hit settles identity. Agreement between indexes can repeat the same error; compare the actual soldier's life details. Search Sarah/Sarah Ann widow entries separately. Obtain the pension file if identified, examining the basis of each witness's knowledge.

Success: a birthplace below the state level, a childhood associate, or explicit family relationship. Stop expanding into military history if the files provide none.

Source guidance: https://www.archives.gov/research/military/civil-war/resources — checked during this review; CMSRs can contain birthplace and personal papers, and pension files are a separate record class.

### 2. Boonville Thomas: best concrete candidate-parent investigation

First verify the 1850 manuscript, Boonville dwelling/family 66, in John P. Maddex's household. Record age, occupation, birthplace, household composition, and any page continuation. Keep the 40-year-old Kentucky-born Thomas separate.

Then examine Cooper probate indexes and packets, approximately 1850–1870, for administrations, distributions, receipts, and litigation naming heirs. Trace Maddex's marriage and wife's family only far enough to test a relationship to Thomas. Seek the original 1846 note in Isaac Martin's estate and any collection proceedings; it could supply signatures, residence, or an estate reference, although it may give no kinship.

The 1850 Thomas–Elizabeth deed supplies a specific parcel: NE quarter of NE quarter, section 35, township 46, range 19. Follow its later conveyances or partition for named relationships. Keep that couple separate from the elderly tailor until linked. The phrase “heirs of her body” alone names no children and is not evidence Aaron was hers.

Success: explicit children/heirs, a prior Virginia residence, or evidence identifying a different Thomas. A different identity is a useful result, not a failed search. Do not require a prior Virginia link before examining an otherwise well-identified estate's relatives.

### 3. Fauquier apprenticeship records: strongest bounded childhood test

The Library of Virginia lists Reel 116, Overseer of the Poor Minute Book, 1804–1845: https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/ccmf/VA/VA083. Its current search result confirms the locator; no manuscript was retrieved in this review.

Start with 1826–1838, examining Caton/Cayton/Caten and relevant spelling variants and recording every identified master or parent. If that window is negative, distinguish it from the unexamined early childhood and late minority years; extend approximately 1820–1841 only as coverage and findings justify. Follow any order to its indenture, court confirmation, or later dispute. Court/deed records can cover arrangements not present in the poor-board book.

A surname-wide full-text pass across record types is a cheap locator check, not a substitute for reading this volume. Property-column analysis should not block access to the actual minutes.

Success: an identifiable Aaron or Joseph with parent/master/trade. Even “Aaron son of Thomas” needs age, chronology, or later association to connect the child to the adult Aaron.

### 4. Joseph and Sue Dumm: high upside, two separate links to prove

Recover the original 1898 niece notice and test both identities and the kinship route. The repository records a Boulder request sent September 12; current reply status was not checked, so do not presume it remains unanswered or send a duplicate.

Prioritize the two-page 1932 Sue Caton Dumm family record (791-10-4d) before reading decades of diaries. If it names Joseph's parents, distinguish the informant's knowledge from later compilation. Look separately for an explicit Aaron relationship. Even a proved half-brother relationship transfers only the shared parent.

The catalog surfaced in current search, but direct retrieval failed. No family-history contents were verified here. Joseph's Saline probate remains parked under the existing user instruction.

### 5. Original 1828 consent: cheap structural clarification

Retrieve the original December 6, 1828 consent in the William Caton–Ann F. Lawrey packet. Compare names, suffixes, signatures/marks, and associates with the Thomas–Elizabeth marriage and the 1835 Ann Caton papers. A typescript correction may simplify the family structure, but it will not by itself connect Aaron. Do this early if readily accessible; do not make it a prerequisite for the four pathways above.

## Defer

Broad searches for Thomas's earlier ancestry; repeated census-index sweeps; further generalized Ohio migration reconstruction; the presumed second marriage; and surname/naming-pattern arguments. Resume Ohio locality work when a named master, relative, or source supplies a specific connection. Keep Maryland/Pennsylvania and Moses alternatives available without treating derivative exclusions as final.

## Proof target

A strong outcome could be a securely identified estate naming Aaron, an indenture naming his parent coupled to his later identity, or independent family testimony corroborated by contemporary records. A proof argument can also be built from indirect evidence, but it must explain the age/suffix conflicts, evaluate alternative parents, and distinguish independent witnesses from repeated compilations. No single same-name hit, census slot, trade, or shared county closes the case.

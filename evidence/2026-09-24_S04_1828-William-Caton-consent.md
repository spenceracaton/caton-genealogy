## Targeted transcription of the only located consent copy

The typescript reads:

> To the Clerk of the County Court of Fauquier. I hereby consent to the inter-
> marriage of my son William Caton with Ann F. Lawrey, and authorise you to issue
> a license for such intermarriage. Given under my hand and seal, this 6th day of
> December 1828.                         Thomas Caton Jr     (SEAL)
> Witness  Jas. Payne (of Thos)   Presley Johnson   Thomas Caton

The bond immediately above is dated 12 January 1829 and names William Caton and James Payne “of Thos” as the bound parties. The clerk's entry below says Thomas Guttridge made oath that Ann F. Lawrey was twenty-one. The typescript therefore states a father–son relationship between the writer and William, but it does not name William's mother, call the writer a guardian, or state a relationship between the two Thomas Catons.

“Jr.” appears only in the typeset name line attributed to the consenting father. The located page does not preserve the original body hand, signature, mark, seal, witness hands, docket hand, or any visible distinction between text written on 6 December 1828 and text added later. No claim about who wrote “Jr.” or when can be made from this copy.

## Holdings correction and access result

The official Library of Virginia inventory describes the relevant holdings as:

- Reel 65: index to Marriage Bonds and Returns, Vols. 1–6, 1759–1854.
- Reel 67: Marriage Bonds and Returns, Vol. 4, 1820–1832, 397 pages, explicitly marked “typescript.”
- Reel 68: Marriage Register, Vol. 7, 1854–1906, 314 folios.

Vol. 7 begins twenty-five years after the bond and cannot hold this 1828–1829 original. This corrects the older handoff and D-012 locator. The inventory proves the form and date coverage of the filmed volumes; it does not prove that the loose bond and consent were destroyed or that they survive elsewhere.

The Fauquier County Circuit Court Clerk's official Record Room page says it holds marriage records from 1759 to the present and accepts searches when a reference is unknown. It is the precise remaining custodian route: https://www.fauquiercounty.gov/government/departments-a-g/circuit-court-clerk/records-room?locale=en. The page gives `records.room@fauquiercounty.gov` and 29 Ashby Street, Warrenton, Virginia 20186. No request was sent and no fee was incurred.

## Required answers

1. **DOES THE ORIGINAL SAY “JR.”?** Unknown. The original was not located or inspected. The later typescript says `Thomas Caton Jr`.

2. **WHERE DOES THE SUFFIX APPEAR, AND CAN ITS HAND OR DATE BE DETERMINED?** In the typescript, `Jr` appears in the name line attributed to the consenting father, between `Thomas Caton` and `(SEAL)`. The original hand, author, and date of the suffix cannot be determined from the typescript. The underlying consent is dated 6 December 1828; that dates the represented act, not necessarily every element of the later transcription.

3. **WHAT DOES THE ORIGINAL STATE ABOUT WILLIAM’S FATHER?** Unknown because the original was not inspected. The later typescript states that the writer, rendered as `Thomas Caton Jr`, consented to the marriage of “my son William Caton.” It directly represents Thomas as William's father, not as guardian, master, surety, creditor, or administrator.

4. **WHICH 1830 THOMAS, IF ANY, CAN BE THAT FATHER?** The 60–70 `Thomas Caton Sen` is age-compatible and is the strongest identified candidate. The 20–30 `Thomas Caton Jun` is not age-compatible as father of the 20–30 William if the census identities and age brackets are correct. The documents do not prove the elder Thomas was the signer. A third non-householding adult Thomas is possible but unsupported by the already-held tax, census, and road-order evidence.

5. **EFFECT ON THE HYPOTHESIS THAT THE OLDER THOMAS FATHERED AARON.** No direct change: neither the typescript nor the reviewed comparison records name Aaron. If the elder Thomas is eventually proved to be William's father, that would simplify the Fauquier family structure and strengthen the case that the elder Thomas headed the local Caton cluster, but it would still not identify Aaron as his son. The Fauquier parentage hypothesis remains indirect and unproved; Aaron's reported 16 May 1820 birth date and the conflicting service-card age of forty-four in 1861 (approximately 1817) both remain in scope.

6. **NEXT DISCRIMINATING RECORD.** The loose original William Caton–Ann F. Lawrey consent/bond packet from the Fauquier County Circuit Court Clerk's Record Room, compared with the original Thomas Caton–Elizabeth Lawrey packet executed the same day. The favored elder-Thomas identification would be weakened by a father-signature or mark demonstrably matching the younger Thomas, or by contemporary docketing that explicitly identifies the consenting father as the later 1830 junior.

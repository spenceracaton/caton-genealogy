# Fauquier Chancery 1820-011 — Caton collateral record

## Source

- **Repository:** Library of Virginia, Chancery Records Index, Fauquier County, case 1820-011, *Executor of John F. Carter v. John Evans*.
- **Online index:** https://old.lva.virginia.gov/chancery/case_detail.asp?CFN=061-1820-011
- **Format:** 48 scanned pages. The downloaded source package is retained as `Fauquier_1820-011_LVA_case.zip` beside this note.
- **Case timing:** The index records the bill as dismissed in December 1820. The Caton-related depositions are dated April 1819 and concern events in 1816–1818.

## Direct observations

- Page 38 asks a witness what he knows of **Moses Caton**, whether he knows him well, and his opinion of Moses's character and truth. The answer says the witness had known him about fourteen years and considered his character fair.
- ~~Pages 19–23, 30, 39–46 repeatedly refer to “Mr. Caton” in testimony about disputed mares~~ **CORRECTED 12 Sep 2026 (C137): scans 0030 and 0038 read at full size say “Sd Carter” / “the said Carter” — the buyer is John F. Carter, the plaintiff's testator. Thomas Hop's Alexandria trip (April 1817) concerns Carter's mares, not any Caton.** Moses Caton (p.38) appears as a deponent whose character the defence tested.
- No document page expressly equates the unnamed “Mr. Caton” references with Moses Caton. The only fully legible given name is Moses on page 38.
- Aaron Caton and Joseph Caton do not appear. The case states no relationship between Moses Caton and Thomas, William, Aaron, or Joseph Caton.

## Use and limitation

This is direct evidence of a local adult named Moses Caton in the Fauquier record environment by 1819, and possibly of a Caton involved in a nearby horse/farm dispute. It is collateral locality evidence only. It does not advance a parentage assignment for Aaron Caton.

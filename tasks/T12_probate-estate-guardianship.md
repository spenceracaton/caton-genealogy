# T12 — Probate, estate and guardianship records

**The highest-value surviving loose-record class in the project.**

An audit of `claims.jsonl` on 10 Sep 2026 found **no will, no estate file, no
guardianship, no deed and no tax list** for any American county in Aaron's own
generation or the one above it. Every probate-shaped claim in the file belongs to
the 17th-century Maryland set (C002, C003) or is a keyword false positive.

Census, military and vital records have all been worked hard. The records
*designed* to state parentage have never been opened.

## Why this is the right class

- **A will names children.** An estate distribution names heirs.
- **A guardianship names a minor's father.** Aaron was under 21 until 1841, so if
  his father died before then, a guardianship should exist.
- **A deed can name a wife or a "heirs of" party**, which is how landless families
  still surface.

## Targets, in order

1. **Fauquier County, Virginia**, 1826–1838. Read the Board of Overseers of the
   Poor Minutes on LVA Reel 116 for apprenticeship or placement entries naming Aaron,
   a parent, master, or trade. The exact acquisition routes are documented in G034.
2. **Fauquier loose fiduciary records**, 1833–1940. Ask LVA Archives Reference Services
   to identify Caton/Cayton material in collection vi_vi05624, accessions 37409/37410.
   These estate, guardianship, division, settlement, and dower papers are separate from
   the already searched will-book indexes (G040).
3. **Elder Aaron routes.** Test Prince William barcode 1050832 for 1834
   sheriff-administered estate material and Culpeper deed indexes on reels 29, 29a, and
   30 for the earlier trust named in the 1834 release (G041-G042).
4. **Delaware County, Ohio**, 1835–1870. Where Aaron married (Aug 1841) and lived
   through 1850. Look for **any** Caton/Caten estate, will or guardianship — and
   note that a second Caton family was in that county (C067, C068), so distinguish.
5. **Ohio County, (W)Virginia**, 1815–1850. Wheeling. This is now a weak competing
   route because Aaron's own signed paper names Fauquier County as his birthplace.
   Note C066: no Caton appears in any published Wheeling county history, so expect
   a poor or transient family — check guardianships and estates of small value.
6. **Belmont County, Ohio**, same period — across the river, and the Alfred Caton /
   John Harrison Caton family sits there (BRIEF §6).
7. **Chariton County, Missouri** — **Joseph Caton's estate**. He was alive at
   Salisbury in 1896 (C065), born c.1822, so he died c.1896–1915. His probate would
   name his children and, if intestate without issue, his **siblings**. If C062 is
   right, that file names Aaron or Aaron's children.
8. **Cooper County, Missouri**, 1870–1885 — the direct-line Aaron died between June
   1870 and June 1880 (C054). An estate or administration would confirm the death and
   name heirs.

## Note on access

FamilySearch holds most county probate images and requires a login this session
cannot use. Free alternatives worth trying: published will abstracts and county
histories on Internet Archive and HathiTrust; county probate court online indexes;
USGenWeb Archives transcriptions.

## Checked and kept
- **Nathan T. Caton's biography is real** — but in *An Illustrated History of the Big Bend
  Country* (1904) pp.432–433, not the Klickitat volume the import cited. Verbatim: father
  George W., tailor, b. 1800 Alexandria, d. 1865; mother Sarah H. Moore of "Westmoreland
  county, Maryland" [sic]; paternal grandfather an Irishman at Monmouth who died at 98 and
  whom Nathan (b. 1832) remembered; to Boonville 1832; financial reverses. **Names no
  siblings** (C121).
- **1883 Howard & Cooper history**: George W. Caton *the* tailor of Boonville 1830–40; G. W.
  Caton a charter member of Cooper Lodge No. 36 in 1841 beside **Joseph N. Laurie** and
  Thompson Moore (C122). George W. was already in BRIEF §6 as "the Missouri tailor";
  now he has an origin and a family.
- 1850 St. Clair household as transcribed (C123) — image not seen. George R. b. D.C.
  c.1828 fits Alexandria under D.C. jurisdiction 1801–46, the import's one good method point.
- **Negative**: no Caton Revolutionary pensioner anywhere in the 1840 pensioner census (C125).

## Checked and discarded (C120)
The import merged Aaron F. Caton (Otterville, wife Nancy, son Gillie Lee — C052) into our

## Where it leaves the question
Not a third fork. Thomas Caton Sr. of Fauquier (b. 1760s, 45+ in 1820 with two boys under
ten and no male 26–45; gone from Fauquier by 1840) is the right age and shape to be the
Irish soldier of the biography, with George W. (b. 1800) an elder son and Aaron and Joseph
late sons — the "much younger brothers" reading the import reached independently (C124).
Boonville's only tailor shop of the 1830s–40s belonging to a Virginia-born Caton is the
first plausible *mechanism* for two young Virginia-born Caton tailors turning up there in
1843 and 1847. Still C. Still nothing in the tree. Still no explanation for Delaware Co.

## 14 Sep, late — LVA chancery, Fairfax (C164)
The LVA index re-run (exact surname) for eight Northern Virginia localities: Prince
William holds nothing before 1909; Fairfax holds twenty causes, the early ones with Caton
only as an indexed surname. **Fairfax 1851-009, Buckley v. Buckley**, read from the
scans: John Buckley of Castle Branch left thirteen children, two of whom married Catons —
Mildred m. George W. Caton, Artemisia m. Wm P. Caton — and the Catons were non-residents
of Virginia by July 1851. That is George Wesley Caton of Roseville, Muskingum Co., Ohio,
son of Moses B. Caton (1767–1839). So the Muskingum Catons came from Fairfax, next to
Prince William, and Moses B. is in all probability the Fauquier Moses; C162 raised to B.
Fairfax 1820 census (reel 137) is two pages an image, by district, ~40 images — left for
the FamilySearch index. Fairfax 1829-001 and 1836-004 (Caton among surnames) unread.

# T26 — elder-generation Fauquier estate/land route

## Question

Can Fauquier post-1835 probate, order-book, or deed records identify heirs or
kin of the elder Thomas Caton, potentially naming Aaron or Joseph?

## Route selected

The highest-value untried route is the direct Fauquier record series after
Thomas Sr.'s last documented appearance: Will Books 14–18 (1835–1844), the
contemporaneous Order Books 1836–1842, and Deed Books 36–40 (1835–1841).
This route is preferable to a 1840–1850 Missouri probate search because it is
local to the suspected elder's residence and could contain a will, fiduciary
appointment, estate settlement, partition, or heir deed close to the relevant
death window. T19 already checked the Fauquier indexed will-book layer; T26
targets the uninspected record images and adjacent court/land series.

## Record fact

The Library of Virginia's Fauquier County microfilm guide lists Will Books 14
(1835–1836), 15 (1836–1838), 16 (1838–1840), 17 (1840–1842), and 18
(1842–1844), each with an index. It separately lists Order Books 1836–1838,
1838–1840, and 1840–1842, and Deed Books 36 (1835–1837), 37 (1837–1838), 38
(1838–1839), 39 (1839–1840), and 40 (1840–1841). The guide marks these holdings
as available through interlibrary loan.

Source: Library of Virginia, Fauquier County Microfilm,
https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/ccmf/VA/VA083 (retrieved 15 Sep 2026).

## Retrieval result

No record-level image or handwritten page from these T26 series was retrieved
in this session. FamilySearch's accessible route did not expose the required
post-1835 Fauquier image set, and the public digitization endpoint for the
Fauquier minute/order-book material was unreachable from this environment.
Accordingly, no page was interpreted and no crop was created.

## Bounded non-hit / access boundary

The result is a bounded failure to retrieve the direct images, not a negative
search for Thomas Caton, Aaron Caton, Joseph Caton, heirs, or land. It does not
weaken or close the Fauquier hypothesis. T19's indexed will-book checks remain
separate and are not repeated here. T20 Culpeper probate and the 1840 Cooper
household check are outside this test and were not repeated.

## Inference and next target

Because the elder Aaron Caton is documented deceased by 1834 and Thomas Sr.'s
Fauquier household remains the leading elder-generation route, a direct image
review of the listed Will Books 14–18 should come first, followed by the
1836–1842 Order Books and Deed Books 36–40. Search every index spelling variant
(Caton, Cayton, Catton, Caten, Kayton) and inspect the referenced pages; crop
handwritten entries before interpretation. A named heir, administrator,
widow, or partition party would be record evidence. Any connection from that
person to Aaron born in 1820 remains an inference unless separately proved.

### Independence assessment

The Sue and Clint records cannot be counted as independent testimony until their pages are compared. Both are dated 1932, are two pages, use nearly identical titles/descriptions, invoke the same committee, and share many Dumm-family subjects. Their author/donor and collection metadata differ. They may be parallel questionnaires, duplicate copies, or distinct responses based on shared family information. Metadata cannot decide among those possibilities.

## Sawtell identity

### Federal censuses

The 1880 original page for Boonville, ED 138, p. 295A, dwelling 165, family 198, was inspected through Ancestry. It records:

| Person | Age | Status/relationship |
|---|---:|---|
| Julia Sawtell | 34 | widowed head; tailoress |
| Stella Sawtell | 10 | daughter |
| Gibson Sawtell | 8 | son |
| Mabel/Mobel Sawtell | 6 | daughter |

The 1900 original page for Boonville Ward 4, ED 47, p. 7, dwelling 139, family 159, again places Gibson Sawtelle with widowed Julia, indexed as her son. Gibson's occupation index begins `Bookkeeping`; its final words are handwriting-sensitive and were not normalized. The 1900 middle initial indexed for Julia was likewise not used against her better-supported identity.

These two censuses directly make Gibson Julia's son. They also correct the chronology: the four-month-old child with Julia in 1870 was Stella; Gibson was born later.

### Cleveland evidence

The retained public-domain scan of the 1894–1895 directory reads:

> Sawtelle Solomon G. cashier J. J. Flick Provision Co. r. 1884 Pearl, Bkln.

This independently matches the 1898 article's Cleveland residence and provision/packing-house bookkeeping work. The directory does not name his mother.

The Cleveland Public Library necrology database transcribes a 1919 notice as:

> Name: Sawtell, Solomon Gibson
> Date: April 15, 1919
> Source: unknown, Reel #71
> Notes: Sawtell-Solomon Gibson, beloved husband of Jessie G. Sawtell, died Sunday, 11:30 p. m. Funeral Tues., 2:30 p. m. at Koebler's chapel, E. 55th street. Burial at Highland Park cemetery. Booneville (Mo.) papers please copy. Age 49.

This expands Solomon G. to Solomon Gibson and independently preserves the Boonville association. Because the original clipping was not exposed, the transcription is B evidence. The site's request route for a clipping was not used.

### Newspaper chain

The 11 March notice says only that Joseph was “the guest of his niece, Mrs. Sawtell.” The 18 March item prints:

> —Our young friend, Gip Sawtell, of Cleveland, Ohio, is in Boonville, the guest of his mother, Mrs. Mary Sawtell. He is now holding the responsible position of bookkeeper in a very large packing house in Cleveland, Ohio. Gip is a native of Boonville, where he has many friends, who are pleased to know that he is getting on well in the world.

The full biographical bundle identifies Gip as Gibson/Solomon Gibson: Julia's son in two censuses; Boonville-born; Cleveland resident; employed by a provision company; and named in a Cleveland death notice that asks Boonville papers to copy. `Gip` may be a printed form or error for `Gib`, but that mechanism is not independently established.

No independent original record was found for a Boonville Mary Sawtell who fits the item. Exact-name 1880 and 1900 census controls found no such Cooper County woman, while the 1900 census directly places Gibson with mother Julia. The bounded negative cannot exclude spelling variants, moves, death, or omission. It does show that G025's “second Mrs. Sawtell” was never independently established.

The 18 March mother is therefore probably Julia, misnamed `Mary` in the item. That makes Julia the strongly leading candidate for the unnamed Mrs. Sawtell one week earlier. The two items remain dependent: same newspaper, same town, same social-news practice, one week apart. Neither explicitly says that Joseph's hostess was Gip's mother. The last identification remains C.

### Source dependence

- The 1880 and 1900 federal censuses independently identify Gibson as Julia's son.
- The Cleveland directory independently supplies Solomon G.'s Cleveland residence and provision-company employment.
- The necrology transcription independently supplies `Solomon Gibson`, age, and Boonville association, but depends on an unseen newspaper clipping.
- The two March 1898 items are not independent witnesses and cannot bootstrap each other into direct identification.
- The Dumm catalog entries are metadata, not narratives, and cannot be treated as two witnesses without page comparison.

## Conclusion

The Dumm pages remain the decisive unresolved records. Their current inaccessibility is an access boundary, not a negative result, and their catalog metadata does not satisfy the proof test.

The collateral newspapers strengthen Joseph's known child network: H. D. and Otis are explicitly called his sons; Otis's obituary names Joseph and Harriett Dumm Caton as his parents, says he was one of six children, and names Sarah Schanz as his surviving sister; the 1915 notice identifies Sue as formerly Susie Caton and Clinton as her son. None of this names Joseph's parent, sibling, exact Virginia origin, or relationship to Aaron.

The Sawtell control removes the principal reason S06 treated Mary as an independent competitor. The 18 March item almost certainly describes Julia's son Gibson/Solomon Gibson and probably misnames Julia as Mary. Julia is therefore the strongly leading, but not directly proved, identity for Joseph's unnamed hostess. The final explicit-name and relationship-language sweep produced no original source that resolves the hostess as Julia rather than Mary. Aaron's parents remain unknown.

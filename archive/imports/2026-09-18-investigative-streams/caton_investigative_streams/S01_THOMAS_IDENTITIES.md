# S01 — Resolve the Fauquier Thomas identities before extending the pedigree

**Target in every stream:** Aaron Caton, reported born 16 May 1820 in Virginia, married Sarah Ann Gardner in Delaware County, Ohio, in August 1841, later a Missouri resident and tailor. His father and mother remain unidentified. These are investigation instructions, not newly proved relationships. [R1]

## Question and purpose

Which records concern the same Thomas Caton, and which concern different men? In particular, can William's consenting father be identified with an older Fauquier household head, with the Elizabeth Lawrey groom, or with neither? This is an identity investigation, not an attempt to fill census slots with desired children.

The copied December 1828 consent calls William the son of Thomas Caton Jr., while the reported 1830 census places both William and Thomas Jun in the 20-30 bracket. That conflict invalidates a simple same-name merger; it does not prove a specific alternative pedigree. The copy names another Thomas among the witnesses, but two appearances of a name are not by themselves a complete identification of two individuals. [N1; R2:C082,C092,C193]

## Starting evidence and uncertainties

The 1810 and 1820 counts have external transcription checks, but the current work has not independently recovered both manuscripts. The 1810 entry has one male 45+, one female 16-26 and one female 45+; the 1820 entry has two boys under 10 and no male 26-45. The women are unnamed and their relationships unknown. The 1830 transcription available online skips the target page. [N2]

Use observation labels, not a premature family tree: `TH-FQ1810`, `TH-FQ1820`, `TH-FQ1830-SEN`, `TH-FQ1830-JUN`, `TH-CONSENT1828`, `TH-GROOM1828`, and `TH-CONSENT1835`. These labels do not assert seven distinct men. S03 separately owns `TH-BOON1850-77` and `TH-COOPER1850-KY40`.

## Exact acquisition sequence

**First, obtain Fauquier 1830 p. 471.** Capture the full names/age page, printed column headings, all three Caton rows, neighboring rows, and the facing continuation if present. Establish the actual roll and frame from the viewer. Do not assume a previously guessed roll number is verified merely because the page number was repeated. The important reads are the adult-male brackets, all young-male columns, and the older household's female counts.

**Second, retain the two earlier manuscripts.** The derivative locators are 1810 p. 263B, transcription line 11, handwritten p. 398, M252-68; and 1820 p. 46, last household after William Cockrill, M33-136. Read against the actual headings and neighboring rows. Preserve the anomalous 1820 16-18/16-26 combination; do not silently repair it. [A9-A11]

**Third, acquire the entire connected marriage packet once.** William/Ann F. Lawrey: DGS 007578973 image 149, typed p. 279; Thomas/Elizabeth Lawrey: same group, reported image 145; Ann Caton/Daniel Laury: volume 5 image 45 at A8. Capture the index, body, endorsements, witnesses, dates, and any pointers to originals. Distinguish execution, bond, permission, license, return and ceremony dates. Ask the custodian whether loose originals survive before promising signature analysis. S08 should reuse this acquisition rather than fetch it again. [A7,A8; R2:C092]

**Fourth, read the already-located tax hits and their annual context.** C156 records 17 Full-Text results, including Thomas entries in the 1809-1819 and 1820-1832 volumes. Open these before starting another surname search. Capture year/title/header pages, assessment dates, districts, and all Caton entries in each sampled list. Then fill the annual gaps needed to establish coexistence and disappearance, initially 1809-1835, extending 1836-1840 only where informative. A first surviving appearance does not necessarily mean a young man just reached taxable age; two tithables do not identify two sons. [R2:C156,C196]

## Analysis protocol

Create a row per record containing exact name/suffix, date, locality, age information, spouse named, children explicitly named, occupation, associates, original/copy status, locator and competing identities. Separately record each proposed equivalence and the evidence for and against it. Keep a conflicting age visible even when other features match.

Read census ages without first displaying the preferred pedigree. A second reader should repeat only the disputed cells, ideally without being told which answer would help the hypothesis. This is quality control, not a second independent historical source. Treat blanks, illegible cells and zeroes distinctly. On the stated birth dates, Aaron was ten and Joseph six on 1 June 1830; use the correct 10-14 and 5-9 categories without calling Joseph seven.

## Predetermined tests

If the younger 1830 Thomas is confirmed 20-30, reject the ordinary identification of that particular man as the father of the adult William shown 20-30. Do not reject every older Thomas, and do not automatically create a third father. If the younger Thomas is 40-50, the age conflict is reduced, but identity is still not proved. If the older head appears in successive tax lists under different suffixes, that may support changing local styles, not establish fatherhood by itself.

If the 1820 count changes, revise only conclusions dependent on that count. The presence of another adult would not make the two boys grandsons automatically; its absence would not make them sons. If the evidence distinguishes the 1810 and 1820 heads, the marriage/mother reconstruction must be rebuilt rather than patched with an unexplained age adjustment.

## First-pass endpoint, dependencies and handoff

The smallest useful result is a retained 1830 image with verified counts and an identity matrix showing what remains unresolved. After one bounded access session, switch from repeated failed downloads to a precise image/copy request. Do not hold all other streams hostage while waiting.

This stream owns Virginia Thomas census, tax and marriage acquisition. S03 owns Missouri Thomas manuscripts and later estate records; S08 owns broader wife and Lawrey kinship analysis. Return a proposed identity map, not a new genealogy. Success means fewer unsupported equivalences, whether or not Thomas becomes a stronger father candidate.

Return `found`, `not-found`, `partial`, or `blocked`, identifying the exact record(s), image/page range, transcription, source grade, inference grade, conflicts with existing claims, and the next discriminating test. Retain full-page images plus relevant crops and source locators when permitted; do not put private correspondence, restricted archival copies, or modern personal information in a public repository without appropriate permission.

# A2 — neighboring Virginia marriage-route audit

## Question

After the authenticated Prince William search returned no target result, which
surviving neighboring-county marriage records remain plausible for Thomas
Caton's second marriage, approximately 1810–1819?

## Record and catalog checks

- **Fairfax:** Fairfax County's Historic Records Center says very little
  official marriage information survives before 1853. The relevant surviving
  record types are pre-1853 license bonds and ministers' returns, but the page
  supplies no searchable 1810–1819 index or image:
  [Fairfax Historic Records Center — marriages](https://www.fairfaxcounty.gov/circuit/historic-records-center/marriages).
- **Stafford:** Library of Virginia metadata lists Marriage Register No. 1 as
  1854–1927. No 1810–1819 marriage volume is listed in the surviving
  microfilm inventory:
  [Stafford County microfilm inventory](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/ccmf/VA/VA265).
- **Culpeper:** Library of Virginia metadata identifies a compiled marriage
  register covering approximately 1781–1853 and marriage indexes; the original
  Marriage Register No. 1 was not filmed:
  [Culpeper County microfilm inventory](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/ccmf/VA/VA067).
  Existing project evidence C182 covers the Culpeper compilation through 1815
  with no Caton entry. The 1816–1819 segment remains unverified.
- **Alexandria:** Library of Virginia metadata lists surviving marriage bonds
  for 1802–1852, including an 1815–1820 volume:
  [Alexandria microfilm inventory](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/ccmf/VA/VA510).

## Interpretation boundary

**Proof:** These catalog and institutional pages establish the stated survival
or catalog boundaries. No target Thomas Caton marriage record was retrieved in
this route audit.

**Inference only:** Alexandria's 1815–1820 bond volume is the strongest next
retrieval target. Culpeper's 1816–1819 material also remains live. Fairfax could
still hold an unindexed bond or minister's return despite the county's limited
survival statement. The absence of a listed Stafford volume is not proof that
no relevant record ever existed.

The later Norfolk `Thos Caton` record remains separate: it is a 1868 father-of-
groom entry, not evidence of the proposed 1810–1819 marriage.

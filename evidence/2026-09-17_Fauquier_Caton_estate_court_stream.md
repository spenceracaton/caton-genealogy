# Fauquier Caton/Cayton estate and court stream — bounded review

**Research date:** 17 September 2026  
**Question:** Can Fauquier County estate, deed, chancery, order-book, guardianship, or loose-paper records, 1819–1845, identify Thomas Caton Sr.'s heirs or connect Aaron Caton to him?

## Material findings

1. The retained Fauquier chancery file **061-1820-011**, *Executor of John F. Carter v. John Evans*, contains one explicit Caton identity: a witness was asked about **Moses Caton** and said he had known him about fourteen years and considered his character fair. The file does not mention Aaron Caton or Joseph Caton, and no page states a relationship between Moses and Thomas, William, Aaron, or Joseph. The repeated unnamed “Mr. Caton” references in the horse dispute were corrected in the repository: the scans read “said Carter,” not Caton. This is collateral locality evidence, not parentage evidence.

2. The repository's prior FamilySearch review of Fauquier **Will Books 13–18 (1832–1844)** reported no Caton in the volume indexes. That negative does not cover a guardian or administrator indexed under another person's name, and it does not cover the separate Superior Court of Law **Deeds, Wills, Etc.** volume.

3. The Library of Virginia's Fauquier catalog shows a continuous county-court sequence covering the target period: Minute Books 1819–20, 1820–21, 1821–22, 1822–24, 1824–25, 1826–28, and 1829–31; Order Books 1832–33, 1834–35, 1836–38, 1838–40, 1840–42, and 1843–46; and Law Order Books A–D for 1831–48. It separately lists **Land Causes 2 (1816–1833)** and **Land Causes 3 (1833–1850)**. The catalog also identifies **Superior Court of Law Deeds, Wills, Etc., 1809–1829**, with the explicit warning that those records are not indexed in the general deed or will indexes. These are access/coverage facts, not evidence that a Caton entry is present.

4. The same catalog lists Fauquier Deed Books 24–45 as covering 1819–1846, including Deed Books 34–45 for 1833–1846. No image-level Caton/Cayton deed from this sequence was retrieved in this pass. The 1834 Rappahannock trust-deed lead reciting **“Aaron Caton dec'd”** remains the strongest document pointing to the elder Aaron's death by 1834, but it is not an estate file and does not name heirs in the material currently retained.

## Exact negative scope

- **Chancery:** the LVA-wide surname sweep already recorded in `CATON-VA-CHANCERY-LINKS.md` found no Fauquier 1819–1845 cause with Caton as a named party. The retained Fauquier 1820-011 file was read; it is collateral and contains no Aaron/Joseph/Thomas relationship.
- **Fauquier will-book indexes:** prior review covered Will Books 13–18, 1832–1844; no Caton was reported. It did not establish that no guardianship, administration, or estate appears under another person's name.
- **Not searched in this pass:** the Fauquier county-court minute/order-book images; Deed Books 24–45 images; Land Causes 2–3; the Superior Court of Law Deeds, Wills, Etc. volume; and loose papers. No negative conclusion is drawn for those series.

## Assessment

**No new relationship evidence was obtained.** The estate/ court stream remains open at the image level. The highest-value unresolved route is the unindexed Superior Court of Law Deeds, Wills, Etc. (1809–1829), followed by Fauquier county-court minute/order books and Land Causes 2–3. Any future result must be reported from the actual image with book/reel, page or image number, parties, date, and exact relationship language. The surname suffixes **Sr./Jr.** must not be treated as kinship unless the original record states it.

## Pre-registered proof test

Before opening the target index, the identity threshold was fixed as follows:

- Resolving evidence had to be an original estate, court, deed, or newspaper record naming children or heirs, stating a prior Virginia residence, stating a Maddex relationship, or directly connecting the man to Aaron, Joseph, or the 1846 `Thos Caton Snr / Thos Caton Junr` debt.
- Age, occupation, Virginia birthplace, neighborhood, surname similarity, catalog entries, derivative indexed fields, OCR, and search snippets were non-identifying.
- The proposed merge of the older Boonville man with the Fauquier elder would be weakened by an original estate naming incompatible wife, children, heirs, origin, or another Thomas line.
- The proposed link to the 1846 `Senior` would be weakened by an original debt or estate record identifying Senior as another man.
- A complete index non-hit could only modestly weaken a local-probate explanation. It could not prove no death, no estate, no descendants, or no residence.

The result met only the last condition. No source reached the identity threshold.

## Conclusion

The required original Cooper probate `Inventory of Names` was completely checked under C and K, and four downstream original series were checked at their relevant surname indexes. No requested spelling produced a target locator. Authenticated Publisher Extra searches produced no probate or death item; original-page verification reduced the earliest exact-name OCR result to a cross-column error and excluded the 1892–1899 civic appointees from the older tailor on chronology.

This modestly weakens a Cooper County probate explanation but neither identifies Thomas nor disproves a Fauquier origin. It does not merge the older tailor with the younger farmer, Aaron, Joseph, or Tabitha. The next bounded test is the original Cooper County deed-index route for 1850–1860.

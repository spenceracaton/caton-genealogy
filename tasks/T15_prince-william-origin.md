# T15 — The Prince William origin: who was our Aaron's mother, and what did the elder Aaron leave?

## Targets, in order

1. **Thomas Caton's second marriage, Fauquier, c.1811–15.** His 1810 wife was 45+
   (pending the index transcription of C156's 1810 page); his 1820 wife was 26–45
   (C080). The bond names her; a consent or surety names her father. Fauquier Marriage
   Register No. 3 (1810–20) index, C page; then the bond. Also Prince William marriage
   bonds for the same years in case he married there.
2. **The elder Aaron's estate, Prince William 1813–20.** Full-Text `Caton`, Prince
   William, 1810–1835, no record-type filter — an administration, sale, or order
   naming his widow, brothers, or nephews. His wife of 1810 (26–45) may have remarried;
   her name is in any such record.
3. **The 1834 Rappahannock deed** (John White, "Aaron Caton dec'd, trustee") — open
   both images; the recited trust deed's date brackets Aaron's death and names the
   debtor.
4. **Moses after 1806 — is he the Moses B. Caton (1767–1839) of Roseville, Muskingum
   Co., Ohio (C162)?** Full-Text `"Moses Caton"` with no filters: a Fauquier/Prince
   William deed or power of attorney by "Moses Caton of Muskingum County, Ohio," or his
   1839 Muskingum estate naming Virginia kin. Also the 1820 and 1830 census index,
   Muskingum Co., surname Caton. If he is, story A has an Ohio branch from c.1807 —
   the first plausible reason for Aaron to be in central Ohio by 1841. **14 Sep evening:
   LVA Fairfax chancery 1851-009 (C164) shows George W. Caton's wife Mildred Buckley was a
   Fairfax heir and the couple non-resident by 1851 — the Muskingum family came from
   Fairfax, next door. Now B. Remaining test: Fairfax tax lists 1807–38 for Moses (Full-Text
   `Caton`, Fairfax, 1800–1840) and the 1820/1830 Fairfax census index; Fairfax chancery
   1829-001 and 1836-004 (Caton among the surnames) unread.**
5. **The unnamed father on Broad Run.** Prince William land and tax records 1782–96
   for any Caton — the generation above the three brothers. Land tax 1782+ and the
   1787 personal property list.

## Record-loss warning
Prince William's court books are badly broken (Civil War losses). Joan Peters' extracts
(archive.org, `pwc-slave-free-negro-records-1752-1865`) show minute/order books surviving
only for 1752–63, 1766–69, 1804–06, 1812–14 and 1833–65 — so the 1813 jury entries
survive by luck and an 1814–20 estate order for Aaron may simply not exist. Will books
and deed books survive better; use them first. Peters' extracts hold no Caton.

# Family Bible surname tree — user-supplied photograph

## Provenance

Spencer supplied a photograph of a framed family-tree artwork and reports that
his parents compiled it around their marriage after looking through family
Bibles. The marriage date, compilers, Bible titles, locations, and original
notes are not presently recorded. Spencer further identifies the left side as
probably the Caton/paternal cluster and the right side as the Elfers/matrilineal
cluster. That side assignment is provenance supplied by Spencer, not a label
visible in the artwork. The artifact is preserved as the [original
photograph](2026-09-15_family-bible-family-tree_photo.jpg), with a [focused
crop](2026-09-15_family-bible-family-tree_crop.jpg), [left crop](2026-09-15_family-bible-family-tree_left-crop.jpg),
and [right crop](2026-09-15_family-bible-family-tree_right-crop.jpg).

## Visual transcription

The artwork is a surname compilation rather than a conventional pedigree:
there are no dates, places, generation labels, parent-child lines, spouses,
or source citations. `CATON` is emphasized near the lower-left trunk and
`ELFERS` near the lower-right trunk. The two bold names support the reported
two-side reading as a plausible layout inference, but the artwork itself does
not establish relationships.

Clearly or substantially readable names include:

`Ames`; `Braun`; `Bushman`; `CATON`; `Clift`; `Cropster`; `ELFERS`; `Featherly`;
`Frost`; `Froauf`; `Gardner`; `Hebrich`; `Hermes`; `Krum`; `Lloyd`; `Milham`;
`Powell`; `Smith`; `Stringham`; `Swaney`; `Thees`; `Van Antwerp`; `Wettendorf`;
`Wilber`; `Winter`; and `Zorn`.

The following readings require caution:

- The upper-left outer name appears to read `Blankenmeister`, though the
  photographed lettering may be read as `Blankenmister`. `Blankenmeister` is
  used here only as the normalized reading because that spelling is already
  independently documented for Julia, wife of William Bramwell Caton.
- The bold lower-right name is now read as `ELFERS`, consistent with Spencer's
  identification of the matrilineal line; the photograph's first capital letter
  is stylized and was initially misread.
- `Kunegunda` is visible in the center-left. It is likely a given name rather
  than a surname and is not treated as a surname lead.
- The left outer name is read as `Fowler`; the photograph is sufficient for a
  lead but not for a record-grade spelling assertion.

## Research value and limits

The artifact independently repeats two surnames already in the documented
family network: `Gardner` (Sarah Ann Gardner, Aaron's wife) and likely
`Blankenmeister` (Julia Blankenmeister, William Bramwell Caton's wife). That
is useful corroborative family tradition, not proof of Aaron's parents.

The other surnames are leads for collateral-family and marriage searches.
`Clift`, `Fowler`, `Hebrich`, `Wettendorf`, and `Wilber` are especially suitable
for controlled cross-checks against the Virginia, Ohio, and Missouri records
already in scope. `ELFERS` is a new matrilineal anchor in this repository, but
the photograph alone does not identify which Elfers generation or marriage is
represented. No surname is assigned to Aaron's parentage from this artifact,
and no claim is added to `claims.jsonl`.

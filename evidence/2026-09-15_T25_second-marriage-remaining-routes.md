# T25 — Thomas Caton probable second marriage: remaining routes

**Retrieved:** 15 September 2026

## Scope

T25 tested only routes remaining after T23. The completed Culpeper Reel 50 index/register check was not repeated. The FamilySearch Prince William exact-name search and its catalog boundary were treated as already tested; this note does not restate either as a new negative.

## Prince William: surviving bond books are the precise next local route

The Library of Virginia’s Prince William County microfilm inventory lists **Bond Book, 1815–1826** and **Bond Book, 1815–1833**, both on reel 34, each with an unpaged index. These are the most precise surviving Prince William local records identified for a marriage-adjacent search that could still recover Thomas Caton, a second wife, a surety, or a consent-related bond. The inventory does not label these volumes as marriage books, so the target record type and name coverage must be verified from the reel or a local copy before treating a search as complete. [LVA Prince William County microfilm inventory](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/ccmf/VA/VA227)

The same inventory lists Prince William Marriage Certificates only from 1854–1865 and Marriage Licenses from 1865 onward. That is a catalog boundary for the explicitly labeled marriage series, not proof that an 1810–1819 marriage did not occur or that no earlier bond survives in another series. The inventory also warns that many pre–Civil War Prince William records were lost, destroyed, or stolen in 1863. [LVA Prince William County microfilm inventory](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/ccmf/VA/VA227)

**Result:** no Thomas Caton bond image was retrieved in T25. The 1815–1826 and 1815–1833 Bond Books remain untested at image/index level and are the highest-value remaining Prince William route.

## Fairfax and Stafford: survival-limited, not negative

Fairfax County’s official research page states that very little official marriage information survives before 1853. It describes the historical mechanism: clerk’s license bonds, parental or guardian consent for parties under twenty-one, and ministers’ returns. It provides no searchable 1810–1819 Fairfax item in the current interface. This preserves a plausible bond/return route but supplies no target hit. [Fairfax County Historic Records Center, Marriages](https://www.fairfaxcounty.gov/circuit/historic-records-center/marriages)

Stafford’s listed marriage register begins in 1854. No pre-1854 Stafford marriage image or index was retrieved. This is a survival/catalog gap only; it is not a negative result for Thomas Caton’s probable second marriage.

## Virginia Chronicle

The Library of Virginia identifies Virginia Chronicle as a free, full-text searchable newspaper archive with digitized images. The public search page was reachable as a repository description, but no item-level search result for `Thomas Caton`, `Caton`, or the 1810–1819 marriage window was retrievable in this session. OCR is explicitly imperfect, so even a successful newspaper search would be a lead screen rather than proof of no announcement. [Virginia Chronicle description](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/node/42)

**Result:** Virginia Chronicle remains untested at item level. No newspaper evidence was added.

## LVA / Prince William Reliquary (RELIC)

Prince William County’s official research page identifies RELIC as the county library’s local-history collection and points researchers to the Library of Virginia for wills, deeds, marriage bonds, church records, and tax records. RELIC’s resource page says staff assist with genealogical research and that the online catalog should be used to identify particular titles and locations. No RELIC catalog item or indexed abstract naming Thomas Caton was retrieved in this session. [Prince William County Circuit Court research resources](https://www.pwcva.gov/department/circuit-court/research); [RELIC resources](https://www.pwcva.gov/department/library/RELIC-resources)

**Result:** RELIC remains a precise local follow-up: request a catalog/staff check for `Thomas Caton`, `Caton`, `Cayton`, and marriage-bond or Dumfries Episcopal material for 1810–1819. This was not completed here and is not a negative.

## T25 conclusion

No parentage evidence, marriage identity, bride’s name, consentor, or new Caton relationship was found. The remaining non-duplicative routes are:

1. Image/index review of Prince William Bond Books 1815–1826 and 1815–1833, reel 34.
2. A direct RELIC catalog/staff search for Prince William marriage-bond, church, and local-index material.
3. An item-level Virginia Chronicle search, with OCR limitations recorded for every query.
4. Any surviving Fairfax pre-1853 bond or minister’s return located through the county/LVA holdings.

Stafford remains survival-limited before the listed 1854 register and cannot be scored negative. Culpeper’s unfilmed Marriage Register No. 1 remains outside T25 and outside the completed T23 result.

**GRADE:** C — repository and survival-route evidence only; no target record retrieved.

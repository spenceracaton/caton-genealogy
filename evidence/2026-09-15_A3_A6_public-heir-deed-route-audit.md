# A3/A6 — public heir, deed, and chancery route audit

## Question

Can a public index or catalog route expose a 1836–1850 Thomas Caton estate,
deed, chancery suit, or heir list that names Aaron or Joseph?

## Routes checked

- [Library of Virginia Chancery Records Index](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/chancery)
  and its [search guidance](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/chancery/use).
- [Fauquier County deed-book holdings](https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/ccmf/VA/VA083),
  including the 1835–1841 Deed Books 36–40 sequence.
- [Prince William County Deed Book 21, 1837–1851](https://arvasarchive.org/catalog/vi_vi01288_c01_c256).
- [FamilySearch Virginia wills and deeds](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/collection/3158846).
- [Culpeper County genealogy and local-history holdings](https://www.cclva.org/genealogy-local-history).

## Result

No record-level Thomas Caton estate, heir list, deed, or chancery case naming
Aaron or Joseph was retrieved through the public routes reviewed. One unrelated
hit names a much earlier Thomas Caton in a Frederick County land-office dispute,
1759–1763; it does not connect to the Fauquier family or to Aaron.

## Interpretation boundary

This is a route and catalog boundary, not negative proof. Public indexes may
omit names inside scanned case papers, and the relevant deed/probate images
still require direct collection-level inspection or reproduction. A3/A6 remain
open; no canonical claim is added.
